## About the book

Hermit Crab has outgrown the shell on his back, so he climbs out of the water and sets off along the shore to find a new house. He needs somewhere safe from the prickle pine fish. Each stop is a fresh candidate: a rock, a rusty tin can, a piece of driftwood, a small plastic pail, a hole in the sand, a fishing net. Each time the question comes round, "Is this a house for Hermit Crab?", and each time something is wrong.

The steady refrain and the scritch-scratch of his steps along the shore repeat at every stop, and each verdict is a short comparison: too heavy, too noisy, too dark, too deep. Listeners who like repetition, sound words and a small animal on a quest will settle right in.

Kirkus, reviewing a 2024 reissue with new illustrations by Katherine Tillotson, called the book a "time-tested tale."

## Author and illustrator

Megan McDonald was born in Pittsburgh in 1959 and trained as a children's librarian. *Is This a House for Hermit Crab?* was her first book. It began as a story she told to children at a library, and it was published after patrons kept asking her where they could find it. It won a 1991 Children's Choice Book award. She is best known for the *Judy Moody* series, which has sold more than 30 million copies, and its spin-off about Judy's younger brother, Stink.

S.D. Schindler was born in Kenosha, Wisconsin, in 1952, studied biology and lives in Philadelphia. He has illustrated well over a hundred books, and his first, *The First Tulips in Holland*, appeared in 1982. For that first book he received a 1982 Parents' Choice Award for illustration. Picture books he has illustrated that listeners might look for next include *Catwings*, *Don't Fidget a Feather!* and *If You Should Hear a Honey Guide*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the host's lead-in: "I'm just gathering some materials so we can build a house. My friends need a place to live." He adds that this is a story about someone who goes house hunting. The lead-in refers to a scene that was cut, so the child hears it without that context.

Eartha Kitt reads the book. The audio ends on its last line: Hermit Crab has found a new home, and it fit just right. Content note: a wave sweeps Hermit Crab out to sea and a fish chases him.

Not in the podcast audio: the host cuts a small round door into the birdhouse he is building for wrens. A segment called "Lifestyles of the Wild and Tameless" then shows animal homes, including a bat cave, a kangaroo's pouch and a termite mound. The kids' book reviews are also not in the audio.
