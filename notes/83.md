## About the book

*Sam the Sea Cow* follows one manatee calf from the day he is born in the spring in southern Florida. His mother carries him on her back down a river toward the sea, lifting his head above the water so he can take his first breath. She teaches him to breathe air and hold his breath underwater, and he nurses from beneath her flipper, because manatees are mammals.

The first turn comes early. A motorboat strikes Sam while he is still small, and the propeller leaves a scar between his eyes that he will keep for life. The text plainly calls motorboats a manatee's worst enemy. Growing up, Sam eats water plants and surfaces every five minutes to breathe. He leaves his mother at two years old, and then his curiosity about a big round pipe gets him into trouble.

The sentences are short and rhythmic, with sound effects such as "Eep, eep" and "Munch, munch." That makes it a good fit for young listeners who like animal babies, and for families curious about how manatees live.

## Author and illustrator

Francine Jacobs wrote this book as *Sewer Sam, the Sea Cow*, published by Walker in New York in 1979 with illustrations by Harriett Springer. Walker reissued it in 1991 under the title *Sam, the Sea Cow*, with new illustrations by Laura Kelly. The Internet Archive catalog describes it as following "the adventures of a manatee, or sea cow, from birth till after he leaves his mother." This is the 1991 edition that the Reading Rainbow episode featured.

The Internet Archive catalog lists many other nature titles under the name Francine Jacobs, including *Sea Turtles* (1972), *Coral* (1980) and *Lonesome George, the Giant Tortoise* (2003). The last is described as a children's book about the last living Pinta Island giant tortoise in the Galapagos.

Laura Kelly is credited as the illustrator of the 1991 edition. A listener who likes this book may enjoy *Lonesome George, the Giant Tortoise*, another animal story by the same author.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the host at SeaWorld of Florida, about to give a bottle to Rachel, a baby manatee: "Hi, there, you're just in time because I'm about to give Rachel her bottle." Rachel was hit by a boat when she was one week old, was rescued and has lived at SeaWorld since. The host then introduces Sam, one of the first manatees rescued, who poked his nose into an underwater sewer pipe and could not get out.

Jason Robards reads the book, and the audio ends on the last line, "Goodbye, Sam."

Content note: the audio mentions a baby manatee hit by a boat, Sam cut by a propeller, Sam stuck and crying out, and Sam feeling lonely.

Not in the podcast audio: a manatee rescue crew freeing an animal from a drain pipe, a visit to a manatee recovery spring, the release of a recovered manatee named Barnacle Bill, an airboat ride, a manatee song, and reviews.
