## About the book

Camille and Leon Lizardo run the local insect market, and after long days of selling bugs and flies they decide they have earned a vacation. They drive to the Hotel Animal, the favorite getaway for animals from around the world, and find everything built for much bigger guests. The key is hard to reach, the room is too big, and a hippo's splash at the pool sends the two lizards flying. The restaurant lists no insects, and the other diners are shocked by the lizards' table. Then a dance-floor mishap with a balloon separates the couple.

The book is about size and feeling out of place, and Publishers Weekly lists it for ages 3 to 8. In April 1994 the magazine called it "something of a mixed bag": the hard-edged illustrations "recall early cartoon animation," but the reviewer found the text repetitious, since most animals are simply called "guests" and some are hard to identify. The verdict was that this tale of the vertically challenged comes up short.

## Author and illustrator

Keith DuQuette wrote and illustrated *Hotel Animal*, a 32-page picture book published by Viking in 1994.

His later picture books, also written and illustrated by him, are mostly about animals. *The House Book* (Putnam, 1999) uses rhyming couplets and a two-story house to look at the parts of a home. *They Call Me Woolly: What Animal Names Can Tell You* (Putnam, 2001) groups 35 animals by how they got their names, in watercolor, gouache and colored-pencil art. *Cock-a-Doodle Moooo!* followed in 2004.

*Little Monkey Lost* (Putnam, 2007) follows a squirrel monkey who drifts downstream on a lily pad and learns a skill from each kind of monkey he meets. Kirkus called it "a lovely tale on its own, and a good springboard for discussing 'what if . . . ' with young children." Listeners who like the lizards may enjoy any of these next.

## In this episode

The audio opens partway into a skit: a knock, "Who is it? I'll be right there," a package from Hats For You, and a dream about being very small. Kids then say what would look huge to them, such as grass like a forest or an apple like a bowling ball, and the host introduces the story of two lizards who look small beside the other guests. The skit's opening is cut.

*Hotel Animal* is read by John Cunningham, and the audio ends on the book's last line, the sign Thomas paints for the dollhouse hotel.

Content note: in the reading, Camille is carried away from Leon, is frightened alone in a dark attic, and cries herself to sleep.

Not in the podcast audio: the Matchbox car makers scaling a Camaro Z28 down to toy size; a 6-foot hero sandwich built at Hero Boy in New York City; and three young book reviews.
