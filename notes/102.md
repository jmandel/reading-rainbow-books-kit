## About the book
*Borreguita and the Coyote: A Tale from Ayutla, Mexico* is a trickster tale for listeners who like a clever small hero. A little lamb named Borreguita, whose name means "little lamb," is tied to a stake in a field of red clover when a hungry coyote strolls up and announces that he means to eat her. She does not run. She talks, and the coyote keeps coming back, only to find that she has another idea ready.

The telling is brisk and full of repeated lines that toddlers can learn to anticipate: the coyote's growl, the lamb's "Baa! Baa!", and a sprinkling of Spanish such as "Señor Coyote" and "Está bien."

Kirkus said that Verna Aardema "paces this saga expertly" and praised its "pleasingly sly wit," and credited Petra Mathers with "her exquisite sense of design and luminous color." The review closed with the verdict "A fine story; outstanding illustrations," and recommended the book for ages 4 to 9. Knopf published it in 1991.

## Author and illustrator
Verna Aardema (1911-2000) was born in New Era, Michigan, and graduated from Michigan State University in 1934 with a journalism degree. She began writing children's books when her daughter would only eat while being read to. She is best known for adapting traditional tales, usually from Africa and Mexico. *Why Mosquitoes Buzz in People's Ears* (1975), illustrated by Leo and Diane Dillon, is listed with the 1976 Caldecott Medal, and *Bringing the Rain to Kapiti Plain* and *Who's in Rabbit's House?* are other titles to look for.

Petra Mathers was born in 1945 in Todtmoos, Germany, and trained in the book trade rather than at university. She settled in Oregon with her husband, taught herself to paint, and began by making pictures for her son's room. She illustrated more than 40 books, writing 12 of them herself, and died in 2024. Her *Sophie and Lou* won the Boston Globe-Horn Book Award in 1991. Young listeners who enjoy her style might look for her own *Sophie and Lou*, *Kisses from Rosa* and *Lottie's New Friend*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "The barrio is a great place to find songs that are excelente, foods that are muy sabroso, and stories that are fantástico." The barrio visit itself was cut, so a child hears the word without context. Olga Merediz reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line.

Content note: the coyote repeatedly threatens to eat the lamb, and the lamb outwits him with a new trick each time.

Not in the podcast audio, the rest of the episode visits a Los Angeles barrio:
