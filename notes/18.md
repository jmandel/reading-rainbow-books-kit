## About the book

*Ox-Cart Man* follows one New England farm family through a year. In October the father loads the ox cart with everything the family made or grew and left over: wool, mittens, candles, shingles, birch brooms, potatoes, apples, honey, maple sugar. He walks ten days to Portsmouth market. There he sells the goods one by one, and then the cart and the ox themselves, before buying a few things the family needs and heading home.

Kirkus Reviews called the text "plain but pleasingly cadenced, concrete as the list of commodities that makes up much of the text, yet radiating a sense of life's cyclic rhythms." It added that Cooney's pictures, "in the manner of early American folk painting," make "a satisfying, full (and eye-filling) experience of the everyday round."

The book suits young listeners who like lists and repeated phrases: many sentences begin "He packed" or "He sold." It also gives a clear picture of the farm seasons, from sugaring in March to planting in May.

## Author and illustrator

Donald Hall (1928-2018) was a poet. In 1975 he and his wife, the poet Jane Kenyon, moved to Eagle Pond Farm, his grandparents' former home in Wilmot, New Hampshire, where he lived for the rest of his life. He served as New Hampshire's poet laureate from 1984 to 1989 and as the 14th U.S. Poet Laureate, beginning in 2006. *Ox-Cart Man* began as a poem printed in *The New Yorker* on October 17, 1977, which he then revised heavily for the picture book. Another of his books for children is *When Willard Met Babe Ruth*.

Barbara Cooney (1917-2000) was born in Brooklyn, New York, and studied history at Smith College before training at the Art Students League. Over more than sixty years she wrote or illustrated 110 children's books. She won the Caldecott Medal twice: for *Chanticleer and the Fox* and for *Ox-Cart Man*, which received the 1980 medal. Her *Miss Rumphius* shared a National Book Award, and *Roxaboxen*, *Island Boy* and *Basket Moon* are other picture books by her.

## In this episode

The audio opens with LeVar: "Would you believe it? I've come all this way to Old Sturbridge Village Massachusetts and my car dies." He jokes about ox plugs, then hitches a ride on an ox cart. A toddler will hear this comic scene without seeing it, and his talk of Old Sturbridge Village refers to the cut visit. Lorne Greene then reads the whole book, and the audio ends on the geese "dropping feathers as soft as clouds."

Not in the podcast audio, because it comes after the book:

- Young oxen in training at the village, with their handler explaining the commands "gee" and "haw."
- A visit to a kitchen with guessing games about old tools, then LeVar helping a blacksmith make a door latch and being paid in cheese.
- A turn at the printing press in the Isaiah Thomas print shop.
- Kids reviewing *Wagon Wheels*, *A Winter Place* and *Round Trip*.
