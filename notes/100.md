## About the book
On May 11th, Holly Evans launches vegetable seedlings into the sky from Hohokus, New Jersey, as a science experiment. Her classmates are speechless. Then, on June 29th, enormous vegetables begin drifting down across the country: cucumbers over Kalamazoo, lima beans over Levittown, and a broccoli that lands in Holly's own backyard. The first turn comes when Holly notices that the giants include vegetables she never planted, and she starts to wonder whose broccoli it is.

The telling is deadpan and it suits children who enjoy silly what-if stories, funny place names and long lists of foods. Kirkus Reviews called the "brief, tongue-in-cheek text" "a plus for storytime" and said the "witty, wonderfully imaginative pictures reward closer study, too." Publishers Weekly, as quoted by Encyclopedia.com, called the book "Spectacular to look at, great fun to read." Clarion Books published it in 1992 as a 32-page picture book, and Kirkus listed it for ages 5 and up. Because the pictures carry so much of the humor, the story rewards a second listen with the book in hand.

## Author and illustrator
David Wiesner wrote and illustrated the book. He was born on February 5, 1956, in Bridgewater, New Jersey, and earned a B.F.A. in illustration from the Rhode Island School of Design. Encyclopedia.com reports that his first professional break came when Trina Schart Hyman, art director of *Cricket*, offered him a magazine cover from him. He works mainly in watercolor and is known for picture books built on playful "what if" ideas, many of them wordless.

This book followed *Tuesday*, which won the Caldecott Medal; Kirkus described the new book as its follow-up. Wikipedia lists later Caldecott Medals for *The Three Pigs* and *Flotsam*, and *Free Fall* was named a Caldecott Honor Book. Encyclopedia.com adds that *June 29, 1999* received a Parents' Choice citation in 1992.

A young listener who likes this one might try more of his picture books:

- *Tuesday*
- *Free Fall*
- *Hurricane*
- *Art & Max*

## In this episode
LeVar's lead-in begins, "Now, prepare yourself for another intriguing tale," and calls it "the remarkable story of a young girl named Holly Evans," with the place, Hohokus, New Jersey, and the date, June 29, 1999. The phrase "another" points back to an earlier fact-or-fiction story about a fossil hunt that is not in the podcast, so the child hears it without that context.

The book is read by Charles Kimbrough, starting at "The place is Hohokus, New Jersey" and ending on the line "Where would their supper come from?"

Not in the podcast audio: a visit with pumpkin grower Howard Dill of Windsor, Nova Scotia, who explains how he raises pumpkins that weigh hundreds of pounds; a made-up story about a woman who sees a flying saucer; a look at real UFO reports; and a tale of an overdue library book. Children also recommend *Growing Vegetable Soup*, *Cloudy with a Chance of Meatballs* and *Time Train*.
