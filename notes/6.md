## About the book
*Digging Up Dinosaurs* is a Let's-Read-and-Find-Out nonfiction picture book that answers a simple question: how did a dinosaur get from the ground into a museum? It begins with the skeletons children see on a museum visit, then goes back to the fossils themselves, how they formed, and how people came to find them. From there it follows the slow, careful work of a dig.

It suits curious preschoolers and early elementary listeners who like dinosaur names and want to know how anyone learned about them. The pictures carry some of the fun: Kirkus noted the "talking pictures" that extend the text and called the presentation "sprightly," giving "some new life to the old bones," while observing that the book adds little to the information already available. Publishers Weekly, reviewing the later paperback edition, called it "Aliki's latest stellar addition to the Let's-Read-and-Find-Out series". Kirkus also flagged one factual slip: protoceratops eggs appear in a nineteenth-century museum scene, though they were found much later.

## Author and illustrator
Aliki wrote and illustrated this book herself. She was born Aliki Liacouras on September 3, 1929, in Wildwood Crest, New Jersey, to parents of Greek descent who taught her Greek as her first language. She graduated from the Philadelphia Museum School of Art in 1951, worked as a freelance artist creating advertising and display art, and taught art. After meeting Franz Brandenberg she married him in 1957 and moved to Switzerland, where she wrote her first book, *The Story of William Tell* (1960). The couple later lived in London and then returned to New York City.

Her subjects range widely: dinosaurs, mammoths, book manufacturing, mythology, Shakespeare and growing up. Wikipedia lists *Digging Up Dinosaurs* among her titles used on Reading Rainbow, along with *Feelings* and *Mummies Made in Egypt*. Listeners who enjoy this book may like her other dinosaur titles *My Visit to the Dinosaurs* and *Dinosaur Bones*, or *How a Book Is Made*. In 1991 she won the Pennsylvania School Librarians' Association Award.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar walking a construction site: "There used to be dinosaurs right where I'm walking now, and they were no laughing matter." He then introduces the book. The earlier dinosaur jokes are cut, so the child hears this without them. The reading follows, read by Linda Gelman, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "See you at the museum sometime." Content note: the narrator says Tyrannosaurus used to be scary, then says he is not afraid anymore.

Not in the podcast audio: before the construction site, Jerry Stiller is the voice of a dinosaur comedian telling riddle jokes; after the book, LeVar talks about museums and a rhyming poem about animals that are dinosaur relatives; a visit to Dinosaur National Monument in Jensen, Utah, with a ranger and a paleontologist, a wall of fossil bones and a lab with an Allosaurus skull; children review books; and a time capsule.
