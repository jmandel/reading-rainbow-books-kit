## About the book
Glenda Feathers is a hen who delivers eggs to the Diamond Dog Diner, and she has just heard on the radio that diamond robbers are in town. She hurries to warn Lola, who likes to wear diamonds while she cooks, and comes up with a plan: blow out some eggs and tuck the diamonds inside. The plan has one weakness, which is that Glenda cannot keep a secret. Once the robbers arrive, the trouble moves fast, and a hen who talks too much has to think her way out of it.

This is a silly, quick-moving mystery for listeners who enjoy talking animals, a bit of suspense and a funny trick. Kirkus called the story "far fetched" but said "cartoony, colorful illustrations of anthropomorphic animals enliven" it. Publishers Weekly was less convinced, judging the story-line "jerky, slapdash" and saying the book would "probably work better as a comic strip." The tale is told largely through lively dialogue between the hen, the diner owners and the two robbers.

## Author and illustrator
Eileen Christelow wrote and illustrated the book. She was born in Washington, D.C., in 1943 and studied architecture at the University of Pennsylvania. She worked as a freelance photographer and graphic designer, then sent picture book ideas to publishers while her daughter was small. A children's writing course at the University of California extension led her to editor Jim Giblin at Clarion Books, who bought her first two books, *Henry and the Red Stripes* (1982) and *Mr. Murphy's Marvelous Invention* (1983). More than 30 books followed, and Giblin edited her work until his death in 2016. She lives in Vermont.

Encyclopedia.com lists *The Robbery at the Diamond Dog Diner* among her IRA/CBC Children's Choice selections, in 1987. Her list of Clarion titles also includes *Glenda Feathers Casts a Spell* (1990).

Young listeners who like her humor might try *Five Little Monkeys Jumping on the Bed*, her retelling of the nursery rhyme, which began a long series, or *Letters from a Desperate Dog*, one of the stories inspired by her dog, Emma.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with a welcome to an authentic roadside diner, then LeVar saying that "who drops by is what causes all the trouble in this book." Peter Falk then reads the whole book, from "There were diamond robbers in town" to the last line, "at least no robbers will ever find those diamonds."

Content note: the robbers tie everyone up with a rope, lock Glenda in the back of a truck and threaten to make her "a barbecue chicken." She cries out for help and a tear rolls down her cheek, and the story ends happily.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks after the book, then a visit to a fresh pasta kitchen where tortellini and linguine are made. The cooks at the diner also teach LeVar diner talk such as "cowboy on a raft" and "hounds on an island." Kid reviewers follow.
