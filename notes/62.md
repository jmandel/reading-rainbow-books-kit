## About the book
*Desert Giant: The World of the Saguaro Cactus* follows one tall saguaro in the Sonoran Desert through its long life. The book opens by introducing the cactus itself: it can reach 50 feet and live 200 years, and its pleated skin swells with rain to store water. Then the story turns to those who depend on it. A Gila woodpecker pecks out a nest hole, a tiny elf owl moves in after it, and Harris's hawks raise their young in the branches. In May the flowers open at night for bats, doves, a queen butterfly and a bee, and in June the red fruit ripens and the Tohono O'odham begin their harvest, and coyotes and javelinas come for the leftovers.

It suits young listeners who like animals and how living things depend on each other. Publishers Weekly (March 1990) recommended it for ages 6 to 10 and described the Sonoran desert's giants and the life they support as "depicted in vivid text and paintings." It was published by Sierra Club Books for Children.

## Author and illustrator
Barbara Bash is both author and illustrator. Her publisher describes her as the author and illustrator of many books for young readers about nature and animals, and also a calligrapher, teacher and performance artist.

Her path to picture books ran through lettering. She learned calligraphy in the Bay Area in the 1970s, moved to Boulder, Colorado, in 1979 to join a community of artists and study with Chögyam Trungpa, and later taught western pen calligraphy at Naropa Institute, where she created a Book Arts program with writer Susan Edwards. She also assisted Ed Young, the children's book illustrator, in his brush classes.

A bookseller listing for a later title calls her the author-illustrator of six award-winning titles for Sierra Club Books for Children. That title, *Tree of Life: The World of the African Baobab*, is a good next book. Kirkus's pointer review said "Dramatic watercolor illustrations . . . and an engaging text make this another outstanding nature title," and Booklist, in a starred review, called the book's watercolor illustrations "stunning."

## In this episode
The podcast opens with an on-location lead-in from the Sonoran Desert in Arizona: "What a shot. Sunrise in the desert is spectacular, but then so is every minute of the day out here." The lead-in explains that deserts get less than 10 inches of rain a year, then points out a saguaro well over 100 years old, and introduces *Desert Giant* by Barbara Bash, read by Philip Bosco. The audio ends on the book's last line, about the cactus giant towering quietly over the desert.

Not in the podcast audio: a closer look at the saguaro's spines and pleats, a woodpecker hole, and desert animals (jackrabbit, kangaroo rat, roadrunner, bobcat, Gila monster). A snake expert also shows a western diamondback rattlesnake. Three child reviewers then recommend books, and a song about the desert's seasons closes the episode.

Content note: near the end the book says the saguaro eventually dies.
