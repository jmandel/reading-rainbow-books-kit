## About the book
*Raccoons and Ripe Corn* is a quiet autumn story about one night on a farm. Leaves drift over the cornfield, the silk on the ears has turned brown, and the corn is plump and ripe. When it gets dark, a mother raccoon and her almost grown kits sneak into the corn, and the story follows what they do there until sunrise, when they hurry back into the woods.

The text is short and concrete: walking between the rows, climbing the tall stalks, pulling the ears down to the ground, peeling away the green husks. That suits toddlers and preschoolers who like to hear what animals do, and who enjoy a story that follows a single night from beginning to end.

Right after the reading, the episode (not the podcast audio) says the story is true, yet the author never saw a single raccoon in that cornfield. He found only clues from the night before, such as animal tracks, fallen corn stalks and teeth marks on the ears of corn, and he turned that evidence into the book.

## Author and illustrator
Jim Arnosky wrote *Raccoons and Ripe Corn*, which the episode credits to Lothrop, Lee and Shepard Books. In the cut part of the episode he is introduced as a naturalist, a scientist who studies nature, who lives with his family on a farm in Vermont. His laboratory is the woods, ponds and streams around his home, and he is also a writer and artist who turns his notes and sketches into books for children.

Two more of his books are featured in the same episode, both from the same publisher. *Deer at the Brook* grew out of the day he watched three deer come down to a brook to drink while he kept very quiet so he would not scare them off. *Come Out Muskrats* came from the hours he spent waiting and watching at a beaver pond after he found muskrats living there alongside the beavers. Either is a natural next choice for a child who likes this one, since each looks closely and patiently at animals in their own places.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in, beginning "For us, the woods are a great place to visit." He says that for many animals the woods are home, and that some animals hide from people while others only come out at night, then introduces the book as an adventure about nighttime creatures. The lead-in refers to the forest where he stood in the cut part of the episode, so a child hears it without that picture. Julia Barr then reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, about leaves sailing out over the field of ripe corn.

Not in the podcast audio: Jim Arnosky walks with LeVar near his Vermont farm. They read raccoon tracks beside a brook, find a red eft, visit a beaver dam, and watch him sketch a porcupine. Passages from *Deer at the Brook* and *Come Out Muskrats* are read, and kids review three books.
