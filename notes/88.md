## About the book
*Seashore Surprises* is an outdoor science book from Julian Messner, published in 1991 and listed in its Outdoor Science series. The book explains how waves, sand and pebbles form at the seashore, then turns to the animals, plants and shells that can be seen at low tide.

Along the way it suggests experiments and activities, so a listener can try things as well as hear about them. That makes it a good fit for a curious toddler or preschooler who likes collecting shells, poking at tide pools and asking where things come from. The subject is the beach itself and the many small creatures that live there, rather than a story with characters.

Parents should know that the podcast does not read this book straight through. The episode uses it as the field guide LeVar carries along on a Florida island, so the listener hears the book's facts alongside what LeVar finds on the sand.

## Author and illustrator
Rose Wyler taught elementary science, served on the faculty of Teachers College, Columbia University, and, with her husband Gerald Ames, wrote over fifty children's books, according to the Google Books jacket text. Besides *Seashore Surprises*, Google Books lists her *Puddles and Ponds* (Julian Messner, 1990), *The Wonderful Woods* (Simon & Schuster, 1990), *Raindrops and Rainbows* (Julian Messner, 1989) and *The Starry Sky* (Julian Messner, 1989). Those are natural next books for a child who enjoys this one. Her earlier titles include *Real Science Riddles* (1972) and *What Happens If..?* (Scholastic, 1976), and she wrote with Mary Elting on *A New Answer Book* (1977) and *Math Fun* (1992).

The pictures in *Seashore Surprises* are by Steven James Petruccio, according to the Google Books catalog record.

## In this episode
The podcast opens on LeVar's own words: "There's something so magical about seashore." He is on an island off the southwest coast of Florida, and the lead-in refers to "the seashore we're on". LeVar reads facts from *Seashore Surprises* as he goes, and finds coral, live and empty shells, a lightning whelk egg case and a beach worn away by the surf. The audio ends on his line "The wind, the sun, and the sea."

LeVar Burton is the voice throughout, in narration rather than a full reading of the book.

Not in the podcast audio: a walk through a mangrove forest with a naturalist, who points out prop roots, barnacles, oysters, a sea squirt and a mangrove crab; a seine-net trip in the island's grass beds with a marine scientist, who shows a spiny burrfish, a scallop, sea stars, a seahorse, a pipefish and a sea urchin; and children's reviews of other books.
