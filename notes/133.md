## About the book
Maria Lili loves Saturdays, when she makes chicken sancocho with her grandparents, Mama Ana and Papa Angelino. One Saturday there is no money for vegetables, let alone a chicken. All the family has is a dozen eggs, and Maria Lili is sure that eggs do not belong in sancocho. Her grandmother smiles, takes two baskets, and heads to the market.

What follows is a stall-by-stall lesson in bartering: eggs become plantains, plantains become cassava, and each swap brings the stew a little closer. It suits listeners who like a clear, step-by-step story and the friendly haggling that goes with it.

Publishers Weekly called the grandmother's method "a case study in bartering genius" and praised the "serene domesticity" of the watercolors. Kirkus, reviewing the 2022 paperback edition, found "sunny, warm watercolor illustrations" that capture "what could be any small Andean town in Colombia," and closed with "Family ties, cultural traditions, and a can-do spirit cooked into one sweet story."

## Author and illustrator
Leyla Torres wrote and illustrated the book herself. She was born in Bogota, Colombia, and now lives in Vermont. Spanish is her native language and English her adopted one. She describes herself as a visual artist and writer whose work includes watercolor painting and origami.

Saturday Sancocho was first published by Farrar, Straus and Giroux in 1995, and Square Fish issued a paperback in 2022. It was a commended title for the Americas Award for Children's and Young Adult Literature, and it is also available in Spanish as *El sancocho del sabado*. The 2022 edition ends with her own family recipe for sancocho.

Her other picture books to try: *Subway Sparrow* (1993), which Kirkus calls her first picture book, follows a girl and fellow passengers helping a bird that flies into a Brooklyn subway train. *Liliana's Grandmothers*, about a girl and her two very different grandmothers, was an Honor Book in the Social Studies K-6 category of the Society of School Librarians International Book Awards. *The Kite Festival* (2004) follows a family who find a town kite festival.

## In this episode
The podcast opens on location at the Oaks Neighborhood annual swap meet and flea market. The host welcomes viewers, explains that swapping is the same as trading, and says he has some great things to swap. He then introduces Saturday Sancocho as a story where a grandmother trades a dozen eggs for an entire feast.

The book is read by Jose de Guzman and runs to the last line, "The stew was delicious." Nothing in the audio is frightening or sad; the only tears are from chopping onions.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit with nine-year-old Jimmy, who collects baseball cards and learns to look up their values in a price guide, and a visit to a family auction house where twelve-year-old Sarah helps appraise items and explains how bidding works. Three children also review the books listed below.
