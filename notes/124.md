## About the book

*Someplace Else* (Simon & Schuster, 1995, 32 pages) introduces Mrs. Tillby, who has spent her whole life in one house by an apple orchard and has always wondered what it would be like to live somewhere else. One morning she tells her son she is ready to find out, climbs into her old green truck and sets off to stay with her grown children, one place after another: a big-city apartment house, a stilt house by the sea, a mountain home. Each visit begins happily.

The story repeats a simple pattern across very different settings, with a main character who knows her own mind. Kirkus praised the pictures, noting that "hazy golden light suffuses Root's tidy, comfortable gouaches." The same review was more reserved about the story, calling it "about, and perhaps for, adults" and saying it "may leave many readers unmoved," though it closed by calling the book "endearing, but also remote." Kirkus rated it for ages 7 to 9.

## Author and illustrator

Carol P. Saul wrote *Someplace Else*. Kirkus also lists her *Barn Cat* (Little, Brown, 1998), illustrated by Mary Azarian, and mentions an earlier book, *Peter's Song* (1992). *Barn Cat* is a counting book in which a curious cat watches the creatures of the garden, meadow and farmyard, counting up to ten, a natural next book for the same listeners.

Barry Root is a children's illustrator whose Kirkus listing runs from *The Saint and the Circus* (1991) to *The Barn* (2021), with thirty picture books in all. Among them is *Alvah and Arvilla* (Harcourt), written by Mary Lyn Ray, about a woman who longs to see the Pacific Ocean and a husband who says you can't have a farm and travel. Kirkus called that book "imaginative and humorous." Root also wrote and illustrated *Gumbrella* (Putnam, 2002), an elephant who plays doctor to small sick animals. Kirkus described it as his "faintly offbeat solo debut," with a light tone and bright pictures.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar in his "travel room," filled with reminders of places he has visited and the ways he got there. He says he loves maps, shows an antique flat map of the world and a globe, and says maps "can't tell you the whole story." To experience a new place, he says, you have to go there, like his friend Mrs. Tillby in this book, *Someplace Else*. Carol Kane reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line.

Not in the podcast audio: a pop-up United States map from a mystery box, and field segments on a New York City rooftop gardener, a rooftop pigeon flyer, a family crewing the schooner Heritage, and a Hawaiian potter working near the Kilauea volcano. Three more books were reviewed.
