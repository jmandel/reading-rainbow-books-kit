## About the book

Harry crosses the lawns every morning to the porch next door, where Mr. George Baker is already waiting for him. George is a hundred years old, a drummer whose crookedy fingers tap out rhythms on his knees, and he has one problem to fix: he never learned to read. Harry tells the story himself, in short present-tense sentences, as the two friends wait for the school bus and then go off to school, where George joins the grownups in room seven.

Kirkus Reviews called it "a gentle tale of intergenerational and interracial friendship" and noted that Jon J. Muth's soft watercolors show the two "completely at ease with each other," though it also judged that the book has "not much story." Booklist's Gillian Engberg described "a simple, sweet, moving portrait of a natural friendship between seniors and children." It suits listeners who like quiet, everyday stories about an older friend,. Its pleasures are small ones: a sweater with three buttons, a dance on the porch, leaves swooping off the trees.

## Author and illustrator

Amy Hest was born in New York City in 1950 and graduated from Hunter College. She worked as a children's librarian at the New York Public Library and as an assistant editor at Viking Press before becoming a full-time writer in 1977. Her picture books often center on children and grandparents. *In the Rain with Baby Duck* won the 1996 Boston Globe-Horn Book Award. *Mr. George Baker* received a Parents' Choice Silver Honor and a Teacher's Choice Award from the International Reading Association.

Jon J Muth was born in Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1960. He studied stone sculpture and brush calligraphy in Japan and painting and drawing in Europe, and he worked on comics before turning to picture books. His *Zen Shorts* won a Caldecott Honor in 2006, and *Come On, Rain!*, which he illustrated, won the Society of Illustrators Gold Medal in 1999. Young listeners who enjoy this book might try Hest's *In the Rain with Baby Duck* or Muth's own *Zen Shorts*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the lead-in: "Just because a thing is difficult doesn't mean you shouldn't learn it. In fact, according to Mr. George Baker, you shouldn't let anything stand in your way, not even a hundred years." The book is credited aloud to Amy Hest and Jon J. Muth, and it is read by Wayne Brady, from "See this man? This one here, sitting on the porch?" to the last line, about George's fingers flying across his knees "like a big old drum."

Not in the podcast audio: a short reflection saying Mr. George Baker was "100 years young" and that people are never too old to learn, then a field segment about The Steppers, a dance group for seniors aged 59 to 87.
