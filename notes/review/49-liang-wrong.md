## About the book

*Liang and the Magic Paintbrush* is a Chinese folktale about a poor boy who earns his living gathering firewood and cutting reeds. His one wish is to paint, but he cannot afford a brush, and an art teacher drives him away. He draws birds in the sand with a twig until, one night, an old man gives him a magic paintbrush. Whatever Liang paints comes to life.

Liang paints toys and lanterns for the children of his poor friends, and he learns to leave a detail out, such as a bird's eye, so that his pictures stay on the page. When word of the brush spreads, a greedy emperor takes an interest, and Liang has to think carefully about how to use his gift.

It suits children who love to draw, and anyone who has wished a picture could move. Kirkus Reviews (September 1980) was not won over, calling it an "old Chinese folktale" told "mundanely" with "faint, finicky, minute pictures."

## Author and illustrator

Demi was born Charlotte Dumaresq Hunt in Cambridge, Massachusetts, in 1942. Her father began calling her "demi" when she was small because she was half the size of her sister, and the name stuck. Her great-grandfather was the American painter William Morris Hunt. She studied art at the Instituto Allende in Mexico and with Sister Corita at Immaculate Heart College in Los Angeles, and she was a Fulbright scholar in India. Her publisher says she was drawn to art early, spending time in her mother's studio, and that her mother was her mentor. It describes her as the author and illustrator of over 130 children's books.

Her picture books include *The Empty Pot*, which former First Lady Barbara Bush chose in 1990 as one of the books for her radio program *Mrs. Bush's Story Time*, and *One Grain of Rice: A Mathematical Folktale*, which reached the Mathical Hall of Fame. Listeners who like this story may also enjoy *The Boy Who Painted Dragons*.

## In this episode

LeVar Burton's lead-in begins: "Making something come to life is part of the magic of being an artist of any kind." It refers to a cut segment of lion dancers coming to life, then introduces a boy who wants to be an artist more than anything. The audio goes straight into the reading, which ends on the last line, that Liang "roamed the earth, painting for the poor, wherever he went." The book is read by Lauren Tom.

Content note: the emperor and his family sink to the bottom of the sea.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to New York's Chinatown with a lion dance, a computer graphics artist painting the dragon boat from the book, a restaurant meal and children's book reviews.
