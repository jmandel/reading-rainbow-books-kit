## Findings

1. MAJOR. "Carol P. Saul wrote *Someplace Else* and, according to Kirkus, one other picture book, *Barn Cat*"
   Wrong: the Kirkus Someplace Else review says "Saul (Peter's Song, 1992, not reviewed)", so Kirkus names a second other book. The Kirkus author page lists only the two reviewed books, so "one other" overstates.
   Evidence: https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/carol-p-saul/someplace-else/ .

2. MAJOR. "with more than thirty picture books in between"
   The Kirkus Barry Root page lists exactly 30 titles (1991-2021) including the first and last, so "more than thirty in between" is not supported.
   Evidence: https://www.kirkusreviews.com/author/barry-root/ .

3. MINOR. "another good choice for children who enjoy his painted animals and settings"; "story of an elephant who loves caring for small sick animals"
   Kirkus calls it a tale with "a hint of Stephen King" in which the elephant will not release the animals she nurses; "painted" is not in the source. The summary and recommendation are softened beyond the source.
   Evidence: https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/barry-root/gumbrella/ .

4. MINOR. Spelling "Mrs. Tilby" (note body and the quote "like my friend Mrs. Tilby in this book") follows the auto-captions. Kirkus spells the character "Mrs. Tillby".
   Evidence: Kirkus Someplace Else review, "Mrs. Tillby".

5. MINOR. "Kirkus ... closed by calling the book 'endearing, but also remote'" is accurate, but "Young listeners get ... a kind main character who knows her own mind" and "suits children who enjoy road trips, houses and maps" are unsourced editorial claims. Kirkus rates the book ages 7-9 and "perhaps for adults"; wording should not imply wider suitability than sourced. Softened to what the review supports.
