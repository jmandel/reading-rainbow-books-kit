## About the book

*Tight Times* was first published by Viking in 1979, with pencil drawings by Trina Schart Hyman. A small boy tells the story in the first person. He asks his mother, and then his father, why he cannot have a dog, and each time he hears the same answer: the family is in tight times. Daddy explains what that means through a run of everyday examples, from breakfast cereal to Sunday dinner, and the boy comments on each one in his own voice.

Reviewers disagree. *Booklist* said the story "has a sweet, sad poignancy." Zena Sutherland, writing in the *Bulletin of the Center for Children's Books*, said "the first-person monologue is convincingly that of a child," which is the quality that lets a young listener follow the boy's point of view. *Kirkus Reviews* was negative, finding the book gloomy and its pencil drawings matched to that mood.

## Author and illustrator

Barbara Shook Hazen was born February 4, 1930, in Dayton, Ohio. She started as an editorial assistant at *Ladies' Home Journal* and became its poetry editor. From 1956 to 1960 she was a children's book editor at Western Publishing, and she has worked as a freelance writer since 1960. Her book *Even If I Did Something Awful* (Atheneum, 1981), illustrated by Nancy Kincaid, won a Christopher Award. *Tight Times* was among the first titles narrated on PBS's *Reading Rainbow*.

Trina Schart Hyman (1939 to 2004) was born in Philadelphia. She won the 1985 Caldecott Medal for *Saint George and the Dragon*, a retelling by Margaret Hodges. Her own retelling of *Little Red Riding Hood* earned one of her three Caldecott Honors. Young listeners who enjoy her art might like *Saint George and the Dragon* next, or *Hershel and the Hanukkah Goblins* by Eric Kimmel, which she illustrated.

## In this episode

The lead-in is LeVar Burton's, and the podcast opens with his line "Some kids just can't have pets." He adds that no matter the reason for the no, "it still hurts." Patrick McCaffrey reads the book. The reading runs to the last line, "I sure hope Dog likes lima beans."

Not in the podcast audio: a pet show on location, where children show off a turtle named Tim, a seagull named Sweet Pea, an imaginary hamster, and pet rocks, and a library segment in which Hillary and LeVar bet on free things to do.

Content note: a father loses his job, and parents cry.
