## About the book

Regina's class has been asked to draw a jungle or rainforest. Classmates are adding leafy trees and a fierce lion, but her own paper stays blank. When she finally touches crayon to paper, the second petal of her jungle flower comes out bigger than the first. Crayon doesn't erase, so she crumples the page before anyone can see, and her teacher, Mrs. Lee, reminds her that paper is not to be wasted.

The story is a good fit for any child who freezes in front of an empty page, worries about getting a drawing "right," or has been told "don't copy me" by a neighbor. The classroom setting will be familiar to children heading off to school.

In School Library Journal, reviewer Ruth Semrau wrote that the book gives "the reassurance that borrowed ideas can lead to something truly original." Marissa Moss's website also lists the book as a 1990 Notable Children's Trade Book in the Field of Social Studies (National Council of Teachers of Social Studies), and as a Reading Rainbow featured book.

## Author and illustrator

Marissa Moss wrote and illustrated this book herself. She was born in Pennsylvania in 1959, grew up mostly in southern California, and sent her first picture book to publishers at age nine. They did not publish it. After studying at UC Berkeley and at the California College of Arts and Crafts, she spent about five years sending out stories and revising them. Her first published book, *One, Two, Three & Four. No More?*, came from Houghton Mifflin in 1988, and *Regina's Big Mistake* followed from the same publisher in 1990.

Moss's website says the idea for her best-known series, *Amelia's Notebook*, came when she bought a composition book for herself while shopping for school supplies for her son. She wrote and drew what she remembered from being nine.

Other picture books she both wrote and illustrated include *Who Was It?*, *Want to Play?*, *After-School Monster*, *Mel's Diner* and *In America*. *In America* was named a Child Study Children's Book of the Year in 1995.

## In this episode

The podcast begins with LeVar's lead-in. He wonders what to make for his mother's birthday ("Well, let's see. I could make a collage, tie dye a shirt") and settles on a clay sculpture of the house he grew up in. He says the hardest part of being creative is getting started, which leads into the title.

The book is read by Anna Holbrooks, and the audio ends on the last line, "And even with all her mistakes, Regina thought hers was perfect." Content note: Regina wants to cry and cries out that she has ruined her picture.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar finishes his clay house; an artist builds a rodeo story box from junk found in his backyard; a sculptor bends a single piece of wire into an armadillo and paints it; a sidewalk chalk artist draws pictures in New York City; and three young reviewers recommend the books below.
