## About the book
A small boy wants a dog, and every grown-up answer comes back to the same two words: "tight times." His father explains it one household change at a time: bulk cereal instead of little boxes, the sprinkler instead of the lake, soupy lima beans instead of Sunday roast beef. Then an ordinary afternoon turns, and something turns up outside.

Told in the boy's own voice, it suits listeners from about four to eight, particularly families talking about money worries, a change in a parent's work, or not getting something wanted. Booklist said the story "has a sweet, sad poignancy," and Zena Sutherland wrote in the Bulletin of the Center for Children's Books that "the first-person monologue is convincingly that of a child." Kirkus was less taken with it, finding that Hyman's pencil drawings "partake of the drabness of the times."

## Author and illustrator
Barbara Shook Hazen was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1930 and studied at Smith College and Columbia University. She became poetry editor at *Ladies' Home Journal*, then a children's book editor at Western Publishing, before freelancing full time from 1960. Her first books, including *Animal Alphabet from A to Z*, appeared in 1958. Her *Even If I Did Something Awful* won a Christopher Award in 1981, and other picture books include *Fang* and *The Knight Who Was Afraid of the Dark*. According to Gale's *Something About the Author*, *Tight Times* was one of the first titles chosen for narration on *Reading Rainbow*.

Trina Schart Hyman (1939-2004) grew up near Philadelphia and went on to illustrate more than 150 books, many of them fairy tales and folklore retellings. She was art director of *Cricket* magazine. She won the 1985 Caldecott Medal for *Saint George and the Dragon*, retold by Margaret Hodges, and received Caldecott Honors for *Hershel and the Hanukkah Goblins* by Eric Kimmel and for John Updike's *A Child's Calendar*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "Some kids just can't have pets," a few lines about how it feels to want something badly and be told no, and then the title card. The book follows in full, read by Patrick McCaffrey, and the audio ends on its last line, "I sure hope Dog likes lima beans." The lead-in needs no outside context.

Content note: the story includes a father losing his job, the boy hearing a cat crying, and both parents crying.

Not in the podcast audio: the TV episode opens at a pet show where LeVar meets children with a turtle named Tim, a pet seagull, an imaginary hamster, and pet rocks, and makes pet prints. After the book, LeVar talks about grown-ups having hard days. Hillary then joins him for free games to make, including rag basketball, double Dutch, and a yogurt-lid toss, and for a song about the library. Three children review books.
