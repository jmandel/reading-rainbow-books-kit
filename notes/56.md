## About the book

*Humphrey the Lost Whale: A True Story* begins far out at sea, where a group of humpback whales is swimming south for the winter, just as whales always do. Then one whale named Humphrey makes a big mistake. He turns away from his friends, wanders under the Golden Gate Bridge into San Francisco Bay, and keeps going the wrong way up the Sacramento River. As the river narrows, crowds gather to watch, and scientists worry, because a whale cannot stay in fresh water for long.

The story has details to picture: Humphrey is described as long as a city bus and as big as seven elephants put together, and the story notes that humpbacks sing to each other. It fits children who like animals and rescue stories. It is a 32-page picture book, and a true story: the real Humphrey wandered into San Francisco Bay in October 1985.

## Author and illustrator

Wendy Tokuda is a television journalist. She was a reporter and anchor at KING-TV in Seattle from 1974 to 1977, then spent 14 years at KPIX in San Francisco as a reporter and co-anchor of the evening newscasts. She later anchored in Los Angeles and at KRON-TV, returned to KPIX in 2007, and retired in 2016. Her daughter, Maggie Tokuda-Hall, is a novelist and children's book writer.

Richard Hall is a television producer and the son of game show host Monty Hall.

Tokuda and Hall wrote this book together; it was published by Heian International in 1986 and illustrated by Hanako Wakiyama.

The same pair also wrote *Shiro in Love: A True Story* (1989), illustrated by Karen Okasaki Sasaki, which listeners who enjoy true animal stories may want to look for next. Tokuda later wrote *Sampson the Hot Tub Bear: A True Story* (1998), illustrated by Lokken Millis.

## In this episode

The podcast audio opens on location with LeVar Burton on a whale watch boat out of Gloucester, Massachusetts: "Camera and film. Film. Where's the film?" Aboard the Daunty 5, he sets out to look for humpback whales, talks about whales sometimes taking a wrong turn, and then introduces the book. Jane Pauley reads *Humphrey the Lost Whale*, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "Humphrey the whale had made it."

Content note: a whale in danger of dying in fresh water, who looks sick and gets stuck at a bridge, ending happily.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks after the book, a talk with naturalist Cindy Belt about humpback whale feeding, breathing and breaching, and a report on the rescue of three young pilot whales at the New England Aquarium (including whales stranded and dying on a beach). Also cut are three child book reviews, humpback whale song, and a plea to clean up beach litter.
