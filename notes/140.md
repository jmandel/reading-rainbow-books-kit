## About the book

*My America: A Poetry Atlas of the United States* is a poetry anthology that tours the country one region at a time. Lee Bennett Hopkins selected the poems, Stephen Alcorn painted the pictures, and Simon & Schuster published it in 2000. Kirkus counts 50 poems, organized in eight divisions based on geography. Each section opens with a map of its area and a short summary of basic facts for each state, and the book ends with author, title and first-line indexes.

Kirkus writes that Hopkins "has done a remarkable job of covering so much territory with only 50 poems, 20 of them commissioned for this collection." Of the art, the same review says Alcorn's "stylized paintings complement each poem with a palette of textured jewel tones." Kirkus lists the audience as ages 8 to 12, so toddlers are listening to something written for older children. It suits families who like short poems, geography, and a listen that jumps from a Hawaiian volcano to a lighthouse to a Tennessee summer.

## Author and illustrator

Lee Bennett Hopkins (1938-2019) was born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, and grew up in Newark, New Jersey. He graduated from Newark State Teachers College in 1960 and taught sixth grade in Fair Lawn, New Jersey, before working as a curriculum specialist at Scholastic Magazines. In 1976 he became a full-time writer and an advocate for poetry in classrooms. He wrote or edited more than 100 children's books, and Guinness World Records recognized him as the most prolific anthologist of poetry for children. His awards include the 2009 NCTE Award for Excellence in Poetry for Children. Among his other anthologies for young readers is *Good Rhymes, Good Times* (1995), illustrated by Frane Lessac.

Stephen Alcorn illustrated this book in 2000. He went on to illustrate more Hopkins anthologies, including *Hoofbeats, Claws & Rippled Fins* (2002), *Home to Me* (2002) and *Days to Celebrate* (2005). Kirkus's author page also lists *Let It Shine* by Andrea Davis Pinkney (2000), which he illustrated.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "We're going on a journey through one of the coolest places I know," followed by a tour of how big and varied the United States is, from deserts and rainforests to the volcanoes of Hawaii. Three poems are read aloud, each introduced briefly; the host says the first, "Mauna Loa," is from this book. Lea Salonga reads "Mauna Loa," Tom and Ray Magliozzi read "New England Lighthouse," and Nikki Giovanni reads her own "Knoxville, Tennessee," which ends on a summer of church picnics and gospel music. The lighthouse and Knoxville poems begin without the cut material that led into them.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar naming all 50 states, people naming their words for sneakers and sandwiches, a six-time-zone clock tour, visits with children in Helmville, Montana and Queens, New York, short state postcards, and kid reviews of the books below.
