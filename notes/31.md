## About the book
A visitor from outer space lands on Earth on Halloween night, hoping to meet the natives. Most of them turn out to be uncooperative, until a group of trick-or-treaters at the corner of Maple and Elm spot the beeping newcomer and think it is the new kid in a great costume. A library catalog summarizes the story as a creature who "is taken first for a trick-or-treater and then for a robot." A boy named Buddy takes the visitor home for the night, and the next morning it comes along to school and has firm views on the class's space projects.

The book suits young listeners who like gentle mix-ups. The text is brief and mostly dialogue, and the visitor's stiff, dictionary-learned way of talking is fun to hear read aloud. Kirkus, reviewing the 1986 sequel *Merry Christmas, Space Case*, wrote that the sequel "lacks the subtlety and drollness of the original," a plain verdict on the sequel, which Kirkus judged against the first book.

## Author and illustrator
Edward Marshall and James Marshall are the same person. James Marshall (born October 10, 1942, in San Antonio, Texas) illustrated under his own name and sometimes wrote under the name Edward Marshall. A viola scholarship took him to the New England Conservatory, but a hand injury ended his music career. He taught French and Spanish at Cathedral High School in Boston from 1968 to 1970, then moved to New York. His first picture book was *Plink, Plink, Plink* by Byrd Baylor (1971). The next year came *George and Martha*, his first book as author and illustrator; the idea came while he was sketching in a hammock, inspired by the play *Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf?*

He wrote and illustrated over 70 books. His illustrations for *Goldilocks and the Three Bears* made it a Caldecott Honor book in 1989, and in 2007 he received, posthumously, the Children's Literature Legacy Award.

Next, try the *George and Martha* books, the Fox books he wrote as Edward Marshall, or *Miss Nelson Is Missing*, which he illustrated for Harry Allard.

## In this episode
The podcast audio begins with LeVar Burton at Lick Observatory, asking, "Did you ever wonder who's out here, all the way out here?" He talks with an astronomer there about the huge telescope, the stars it shows, and whether anyone might be out there. He then introduces the book as one about a boy who got the chance to meet a being from another planet on a spooky Halloween night. Michael Winslow reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, "Wow! Goodbye."

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's closing remarks, and a "space edition" message to aliens, with a tour of Earth, three kids reviewing books, and a visit to a giant radio telescope in Puerto Rico.
