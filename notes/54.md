## About the book
*Mummies Made in Egypt* is a nonfiction picture book that walks through how ancient Egyptians preserved their dead and why. The text turns to the Nile and the desert, then follows one mummy step by step: the embalmers' 70 days of work, the natron that dries the body, the long strips of linen, the painted coffins and the sealed tomb. It explains words like sarcophagus and pharaoh in plain sentences, so it suits curious listeners.

Kirkus Reviews (1979) found both the "Egyptian-look pictures" and the "simple text" "as crisp and spiffy as one could ask a description of embalming and burial to be," and said the author does not shy away from the "less savory procedures," such as removing the brain through the nostrils. The same review judged that the subject of mummies "doesn't allow for the brisk high spirits" of her earlier natural history books, but closed with the question, "Given the climate of interest, who could pass it up?" Thomas Y. Crowell published it in 1979.

## Author and illustrator
Aliki, who wrote and illustrated this book, was born Aliki Liacouras in Wildwood Crest, New Jersey, in 1929. Her parents were from Greece and taught her Greek as her first language. She graduated from the Philadelphia Museum School of Art in 1951, then freelanced as an artist and painted murals.

In 1956 she travelled in Greece and elsewhere in Europe. She met Franz Brandenberg there and married him the next year. Living in his native Switzerland, she wrote her first book, *The Story of William Tell*, published in 1960. The couple later moved to New York, then to London in 1977, and eventually back to New York City.

Her nonfiction covers dinosaurs, mammoths, how a book is made, mythology and Shakespeare, while her fiction looks at family and friendship. She won the Pennsylvania School Librarians' Association Award in 1991.

Listeners who enjoy this one might try her *Fossils Tell of Long Ago*, *Digging Up Dinosaurs* or *Feelings*; the last two were also featured on Reading Rainbow.

## In this episode
The podcast audio begins with LeVar's lead-in: "People have lots of ideas about what mummies are. Some are more fantasy than reality, but this book has all the facts wrapped up." He names the book and its author-illustrator, and Corinne Orr reads it, from the Nile and the desert through to the sealed tomb. The audio ends on the book's last line, "At last, the mummy was in its eternal resting place."

Content note: the reading describes death, burial, and the removal of the organs and brain during mummification.

Not in the podcast audio: a cold open aboard a camel taxi in Boston; a visit to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston to see real mummies; a trip to Kalamazoo, Michigan, where a mummy gets a CT scan and a forensic artist builds a model of her face; the museum's conservation lab, where a conservator cleans a mummified cat; and three young reviewers.
