## About the book

A boy's perfect summer, with a new tree house and a spot on the best baseball team, goes sour when Jeremy Ross moves into the neighborhood. Jeremy strikes him out, throws a trampoline party without inviting him, and lands at the top of the boy's enemy list. Dad has a cure: Enemy Pie, a secret recipe that is the "fastest known way" to get rid of enemies. The boy offers weeds, earthworms and chewed gum, but the recipe has one part he cannot skip.

The book is a Reading Rainbow selection, published by Chronicle Books in 2000. It suits young listeners who are starting to sort out friends, rivals and hurt feelings, and the pie gives the lesson a funny, kitchen-table shape. The Ripple Kindness Project called it "an impressive picture story book about friendship, judgment, and bullying." Kirkus Reviews was critical, finding the illustrations bland and the presentation superficial, and noting that the gross worm-and-weed dishes shown on the cover never appear in the illustrations inside. In the podcast, the story rests on the reading.

## Author and illustrator

According to Chronicle Books, Derek Munson lives in Washington state. *Enemy Pie* is his first children's book.

Tara Calahan King studied at the Art Academy of Cincinnati and lives in Ohio with her husband, Rick. *Enemy Pie* was also her first children's book, so the pairing was a first for both of them.

According to its publisher, the book collected a run of awards and nominations. It won the 2003 Peace Education Fund Children's Book Award, and it placed second for both the 2003 Nebraska Golden Sower Award and the 2003 Missouri Show Me Award. It was also a nominee for the Utah Children's Picture Book Award (2002-2003), the Tennessee Volunteer State Book Award (2004-2005) and the Nevada Young Readers' Award (2004).

## In this episode

The podcast opens in the middle of the host's lead-in: the park is full of kids, and a basketball seems to have come from nowhere. Two boys argue over whose ball it is, and the host says they are jumping to big conclusions. The lead-in then turns to a new kid moving into a boy's neighborhood and a rocky start, and introduces the book. Ed Harris reads *Enemy Pie*, and the audio ends on the boy's last line about having just lost his best enemy.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's reflection after the story, on spending a day with someone to learn what is true. The episode also followed two girls, one from an Italian family and one from an Indian family, as they visited each other's homes, shared a dinner eaten by hand and tried on dance costumes. Other children talked about how they became friends, and young reviewers recommended the books below.
