## About the book
On Monday, a girl's math teacher, Mrs. Fibonacci, remarks that almost everything can be thought of as a math problem. By Tuesday morning the girl cannot stop. Getting dressed, catching the bus, counting classmates, sharing pizza and apple pie: each turns into a question with numbers in it, and she decides she has been put under a math curse. The questions run along the page for listeners to try, so the book suits children who like to count, guess and call out answers. Some of the questions are not really math at all.

Kirkus Reviews called it "another calculated triumph" from the team behind *The Stinky Cheese Man*, with "a bit of brainwork deftly woven in," and described Smith's collage-style illustrations as "way strange." Kirkus recommended it for ages 7 to 9. The answers are printed on the back cover.

## Author and illustrator
Jon Scieszka (pronounced SHESH-kuh) was born in Flint, Michigan, on September 8, 1954, the second oldest of six sons. He earned an M.F.A. in fiction writing from Columbia University, then taught at Trevor Day School in New York City for ten years, mostly second graders. He has said he writes because he loves to make kids laugh. In 2008 the Library of Congress named him the first National Ambassador for Young People's Literature, and he founded Guys Read, a web-based program to encourage boys to become readers.

Lane Smith was born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, on August 25, 1959, and moved as a child to Corona, California. He graduated from the Art Center in 1983 with a B.F.A. in illustration, then moved to New York City.

Scieszka and Smith made picture books together from 1989 to 2007. *The Stinky Cheese Man and Other Fairly Stupid Tales* received a Caldecott Honor in 1992, and Booklist gave *Math Curse* a Top-of-the-List citation in 1995. Listeners who like this pair might try *Science Verse*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens mid-scene: LeVar says he is determined to use math to get "this place" in order, starting by sorting gifts into groups by size. "This place" is a friend's warehouse, (cut). He then says math is a way of thinking that helps find solutions, unless you only focus on the problem and go in circles, which is what happens at first to the girl in this book.

Michelle Trachtenberg reads the book, which ends as the science teacher, Mr. Newton, says almost everything can be thought of as a science experiment.

Not in the podcast audio: children describing how they use math, from elevator buttons to measuring a floor before buying carpet; an artist making fish-themed clay tile murals for New York subway stations; and a tour of a FedEx package-sorting center.

Content note: the girl is worried, becomes a "math zombie," and says in a dream that she is about to give up and die.
