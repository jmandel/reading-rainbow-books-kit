## About the book

R. R. Pottle III loves hats: firemen's helmets, fur hats, berets, bonnets, bathing caps and bowlers. He is the last of a long line of Pottles, whose father collected canes and whose mother liked umbrellas, and he lives alone in the family mansion. He is rather lonely, and he dreams of meeting his future wife in the rain, wearing the perfect hat.

Every morning he picks a hat before he does anything else, and when he feels sad he piles on a second one. Then comes a morning so glum that he wears three, and heads for the biggest hat store in town.

The text is full of hat names to listen for, from fezes and tam o'shanters to beanies with propellers, so it suits listeners who enjoy lists and silly sounds, with pictures by Arnold Lobel. The book was named one of the American Library Association's Notable Children's Books of 1985, according to the Eric Carle Museum shop listing.

## Author and illustrator

Laura Geringer is a graduate of Barnard College and Yale University. An older biography on a bookseller's page says she has worked as a newspaper reporter, an art teacher and a book reviewer, and that she was an editor at a major New York publishing house. She lives in New York City and serves on the National Advisory Board of First Book, a charity that gets books to children in need. Her other books include the picture book *Boom, Boom Go Away!*, illustrated by Bagram Ibatoulline, and *Sign of the Qin*, named an ALA Best Book for Young Adults.

Arnold Lobel was born in Los Angeles on May 22, 1933, grew up in Schenectady, New York, and graduated from the Pratt Institute in 1955. He won the 1981 Caldecott Medal for *Fables*, and *Frog and Toad Are Friends* earned a Caldecott Honor. *Frog and Toad Together* received a Newbery Honor. Young listeners who like this book may enjoy his *Frog and Toad Are Friends*, *Mouse Soup* and *Owl at Home*.

## In this episode

The audio opens with LeVar reading a hat store's sign aloud, "Hats to where? Hats to where? Strange name for a hat store." He remarks on the straw, top and cowboy hats and the books about hats, then picks out *A Three Hat Day*. The arrival that led him to the store is cut.

Zelda Rubinstein reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, "R. R. Pottle IV loved shoes."

Content note: the reading mentions that R. R.'s mother and father died, that he is lonely and sad, and that tears come to his eyes when a saleswoman scolds him.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar tries on hats that carry him away. He rides in a race with a jockey at Turf Paradise racetrack, visits a huge model railroad in Three Bridges, New Jersey, with hand-built bridges, and learns goaltending gear and saves at the New York Islanders' practice at Nassau Coliseum.
