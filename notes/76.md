## About the book
*Opt: An Illusionary Tale* is a picture book about a place where optical illusions are everywhere. A catalog description calls it a land of "optical illusions" where objects appear and disappear and readers "can discover just how often their eyes can deceive".

On the show, the book is presented as one a child can really get into. The visitor to the land of Opt starts with an envelope holding a red line and a blue line. One looks longer, but the two are the same length, and the big and little boxes of the envelope are what trick the eye. Next come two lines with arrowheads, a castle guard's trident with a puzzling number of prongs, and a pair of flowers whose centers look different in size because of the circles around them. In each example LeVar asks which is bigger or longer, then shows why the eyes are fooled.

## Author and illustrator
The book is credited to Arline Baum and Joseph Baum, listed together as its creators in library records. Library records list a first edition from Viking Kestrel dated 1987, and a Puffin Books edition dated 1989. The Reading Rainbow credits give the publisher as Viking Penguin, a division of Penguin Books USA.

A library description of the Puffin edition says a royal family and court present a variety of optical illusions, which are explained at the end of the book, and that it includes suggestions for making your own illusions. A 1998 annotated list, *Literature for Science and Mathematics*, files the book under nonfiction for grades 3 to 6 and says "a reader cannot resist solving these puzzles and illusions."

The episode captions spell the first author's name "Arlene"; the catalog listings spell it Arline.

## In this episode
The podcast opens inside a mock mountain-climbing scene. LeVar Burton, in a studio, plays at being caught in a blizzard, the scene is stopped, and he explains how the snow, wind, foam rock and blue-wall backdrop were made. He ends with the lesson that "you can't always believe what you see," and moves on to optical illusions and the book. 

LeVar Burton is the reader, walking through the book's illusions. The audio also includes a second, shorter stretch from the book's art gallery, where one picture shows two faces, an old woman and a young woman, and a pattern of white lines makes gray spots appear. It ends on a count of boxes that changes from six to seven.

Not in the podcast audio: demonstrations of optical illusions to make at home, a visit with an artist who paints scenes meant to fool the eye, a segment on animal camouflage, and the children's book reviews.
