## About the book

Sebastian is playing in the garage with Egbert, his wooden pull-toy duck, when his father calls him in for lunch. Sebastian ties the duck to the bumper of his father's car so it cannot roll away, and then forgets all about it. Before long Egbert is speeding down the highway, and the first turn of the story is that the string snaps and the duck goes sailing off into the air.

From there the tale is a long, silly, farfetched journey over water and across an ocean, with poems written about the little duck along the way. It suits listeners who enjoy a funny adventure and who have ever lost a favorite toy. The Christian Science Monitor, reviewing it in 1985, found "many levels of participation," naming adventure, poetry, romance and "an introduction to map reading."

## Author and illustrator

David Lyon both wrote and illustrated *The Runaway Duck*, so the funny story and the thickly painted pictures come from one pair of hands. The book was published in New York in 1985 by Lothrop, Lee & Shepard and runs 33 pages.

The Christian Science Monitor singled out the pictures in May 1985, in a roundup titled "Four stories to catch a toddler's eye." Its critic wrote that the colors are so rich and thickly applied that an almost three-dimensional effect is created. The reviewer describes Egbert as a creamy white wooden duck with a bright orange bill, and notes that both of Egbert's owners "pray and write poems" for him, an earnest affection it says will inspire young readers with "visions of friendship and loyalty."

The book became a featured book on Reading Rainbow, and Google Books lists it as volume 40 of the Reading Rainbow series, which matches this episode's number.

## In this episode

The podcast opens on location in a duck blind, a tent hidden among bushes. LeVar says, "this is a duck blind," and a wildlife worker answers that the tent blends in with the bushes. The two wait for ducks while LeVar introduces the book. The trip to Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge on the Chesapeake Bay that led up to it is cut, so a child hears the blind without its setup.

Orson Bean then reads *The Runaway Duck* through to its last line, about a little girl in Portugal who wonders who Sebastian is.

Content note: the story includes a lost toy, a shark biting off one of its wheels, and its owner missing it.

Not in the podcast audio: catching and examining ducks up close, children saying how they would call a duck, a duck call being played, a couple carving and painting wooden duck decoys, and children reviewing the books below.
