## About the book
A mouse is given a cookie, and that small kindness sets off a long run of requests. First comes a glass of milk, then a straw, then a napkin, then a mirror to check for a milk mustache. Each request leads to the next.

The book is told in the second person, so a listener is the one doing the giving. That makes it easy to join in and guess what the mouse will want next. The cause-and-effect pattern is clear and predictable, which suits toddlers and preschoolers who enjoy anticipating the next step.

Published in 1985 by Harper & Row, it is a circular tale: the last page returns to where the first began. Felicia Bond's blended-pencil pictures show the mouse's rising energy and the boy's growing tiredness. The book started a series of "If You Give..." titles that Numeroff and Bond made together.

## Author and illustrator
Laura Numeroff was born in Brooklyn on July 14, 1953, and knew from about age nine that she wanted to be a writer. She studied at the Pratt Institute, where she moved from fashion to writing and illustrating children's books. Her first book, *Amy for Short*, was published by Macmillan before she graduated in 1975. According to Wikipedia, the manuscript for *If You Give a Mouse a Cookie* was turned down nine times before HarperCollins accepted it. Later titles in the series include *If You Give a Moose a Muffin* (1991).

Felicia Bond was born in Yokohama, Japan, on July 18, 1954, and grew up in Bronxville, New York, and Houston, Texas. She earned a painting degree at the University of Texas at Austin and moved to New York City at 22. Her first book as both author and illustrator, *Poinsettia and Her Family*, came out when she was 26. Wikipedia says the boy in this book was modeled on her boyfriend as a child. She also illustrated *Big Red Barn* by Margaret Wise Brown.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar at a bowling center explaining that when you bowl, one thing leads to another: you throw the ball, it knocks down the pins, the pins are reset, and the ball comes back for another turn. He calls that a chain reaction, and says a chain reaction can start with anything, in this book a single cookie. The bowling lanes are not shown in the audio, so the child hears the opening without that setting.

The book is read by Beth Howland, and the audio ends on the book's last line about the cookie to go with the milk.

Not in the podcast audio: after the reading, LeVar visits the Ebonite bowling ball factory in Hopkinsville, Kentucky, where bowling balls are molded, polished and stamped. A domino artist then sets up and topples rows of dominoes in patterns. Three children review *The House That Jack Built*, *This Is the Key to the Kingdom* and *Smart Dog*.
