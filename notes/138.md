## About the book
The Elm Street Kids Club has a falling-down clubhouse and an empty piggy bank, so Meg, Matthew, Danny and Sherry open a lemonade stand. Sherry keeps a bar graph of the cups sold each day, and the numbers climb through Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday. Then Thursday's bar drops, and the club has to work out why. Meg's pet parrot, Petey, squawks the sales pitch and repeats the kids' lines.

Children who like counting, patterns and neighborhood stories will find plenty to enjoy. The bar graph is built up one day at a time, so the math arrives inside the story. *Lemonade for Sale* belongs to Stuart J. Murphy's MathStart series, which uses stories to teach math ideas.

Kirkus Reviews described Tricia Tusa's pictures as "vigorously drawn watercolors" with "loads of funny detail," and called the book "a winning way to make some basic concepts and techniques less intimidating."

## Author and illustrator
Stuart J. Murphy was born in 1942 and grew up in Rockville, Connecticut. He studied illustration at the Rhode Island School of Design and later worked as art director at the publisher Ginn and Company. In 1980 he co-founded Ligature, a Chicago design firm that developed textbook programs. His own site says a fourth-grade teacher encouraged him to write, and that his first published book was *The Best Bug Parade*. The MathStart series began in 1995 with three books and grew to 63; Wikipedia reports sales above 10 million by 2013. Two MathStart books, *Jack the Builder* and *Mighty Maddie*, are named for his grandchildren.

Tricia Tusa was born in 1960 in Texas and has lived in New York City and New Mexico. Her website says she has illustrated more than 40 children's books and written and illustrated ten of them. She also illustrated Murphy's *Treasure Map* (2004), and her other picture books include *The Magic Hat*, written by Mem Fox, and *The Sandwich Swap*, by Rania Al Abdullah and Kelly DiPucchio.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar on Wall Street in New York City, explaining that many big companies were started by a few people with a great idea and a willingness to work hard. He calls such people entrepreneurs and introduces the story of four young ones and their thirst to succeed. The story starts right after that introduction, with no cut material the child needs to know about.

Andrea McArdle reads the book, and the audio ends with Jed's answer when the club asks him to join: "You bet! You bet!"

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to the American Stock Exchange, where LeVar looks at the histories of TWA, Hewlett-Packard and Goya Foods; a trip to Nantucket Nectars, where founders Tom First and Tom Scott show how a juice flavor is made and bottled; children sharing their own business ideas; and Dress for Success, a not-for-profit that gives interview suits to women. Three kids then review books.
