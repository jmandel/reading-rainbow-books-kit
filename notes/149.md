## About the book
*Beegu* opens with a plain line: "Beegu was not supposed to be here." A small, banana-yellow alien with floppy ears crash-lands on Earth, lost and looking for her mother. The text is minimal, and the illustrations carry much of the story. The first people she meets are the big ones, and they are not welcoming. Her luck with the little ones is a different matter.

Publishers Weekly described Deacon's "simple text" and said Beegu's black outline and solid yellow center "evoke a celestial simplicity," in contrast with the solidly inked human figures and the city's gray gutters and stark fences. It also said the artwork "highlights the book's uplifting message that acts of kindness have lasting effects." Julia Eccleshare of the Guardian wrote that the "atmospheric illustrations capture the pathos of being an outsider," and the Financial Times called it "Moving and heart-warming."

It suits preschoolers and kindergartners (Publishers Weekly gave ages 3 to 6) who like gentle stories about feeling lost, being new somewhere, or making a friend.

## Author and illustrator
Alexis Deacon wrote and illustrated *Beegu*. He studied illustration at the University of Brighton, graduating with first-class honours, and was named one of Booktrust's ten Best New Illustrators in 2008. He lives in London.

*Beegu* was a New York Times Book Review Best Illustrated Book of the Year and was shortlisted for the Kate Greenaway Medal. It was published in the United States by Farrar, Straus and Giroux. Penguin's description of the book calls it his second picture book, "as wonderfully fresh and inventive as his first."

Deacon has described Beegu as "a little shiny jewel" set against his dark, stark landscapes, and the blogger Tim Warnes notes that she shines out from that darkness. In an appreciation of the book, the picture-book creator Tim Warnes recommends it for teaching empathy and modelling kindness.

Other books by Deacon that a young listener might enjoy next include *Slow Loris*, *While You Are Sleeping* and *I Am Henry Finch*, which he wrote and Viviane Schwarz illustrated.

## In this episode
The podcast begins with the lead-in about astronaut Charles Walker, who described his first view of Earth from space, a deep blue ocean with green, gray and white atolls and clouds. The lead-in goes on to astronaut Edgar Mitchell's description of Earth as a small pearl in a sea of black, wonders whether creatures out there are looking back at us, and introduces Beegu as a friendly little space creature who lands on Earth by accident.

The book is read by Freddie Prinze Jr. The audio ends on the book's last line. Content note: Beegu is lost and alone, hears what she thinks is her mother calling, and says goodbye to her new friends.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks after the book, a Moon landing segment, kids inventing alien creatures, a young writer's story about Mars, and a report on NASA's Spirit and Opportunity rover missions, followed by children's book reviews.
