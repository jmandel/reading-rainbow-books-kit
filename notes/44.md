## About the book

Rancher Hicks lives so far out west that, as far as the eye can see, there is nothing, so he drives 84 miles into the town of Sleepy Gulch to find some excitement. His wife, Elna, stays home to dig potatoes. The story then flips back and forth between two places. In town, the post office still displays the same wanted posters it had twelve years ago, and the biggest news at the barber shop is a rainstorm from long ago. Meanwhile, back at the ranch, one amazing visitor after another turns up at Elna's door.

The publisher's description calls it a "deadpan tall tale" and says Tony Ross answered it with a "zany, on-target vision." The joke builds on the contrast between a dull town and a wild ranch, and the repeated phrase "Meanwhile, back at the ranch" gives young listeners a pattern to anticipate. It suits children who enjoy silly surprises.

## Author and illustrator

Trinka Hakes Noble wrote picture books for Dial, Sleeping Bear Press and other publishers. Her books include *The Day Jimmy's Boa Ate the Wash* (Dial, 1980, illustrated by Steven Kellogg), a chaotic class trip to a farm told backward by a child to her mother. Later books include *Jimmy's Boa and the Bungee Jump Slam Dunk* (2003, also illustrated by Kellogg) and *The Orange Shoes* (2007, illustrated by Doris Ettlinger), about a barefoot country schoolgirl.

Tony Ross, born in London in 1938, studied at the Liverpool School of Art and Design and worked as a cartoonist, graphic designer and advertising art director. He later led the illustration course at Manchester Polytechnic. His long link with Andersen Press began in 1976 with his retelling of *Goldilocks and the Three Bears*. He won Germany's Deutscher Jugendliteraturpreis in 1986 for the German edition of *I'm Coming to Get You!* Young listeners might also like his *Little Princess* books or *I Want Two Birthdays*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar on a stagecoach heading for Tucson, Arizona: "Howdy, partner. Hitch up your britches and climb aboard." He remarks that the West is "still full of surprises" and introduces the book. Hoyt Axton reads *Meanwhile Back at the Ranch*, and the audio ends on the book's last line. The lead-in describes a stagecoach ride that was shown on screen, so a child hears it without the pictures.

Cut from the podcast audio, because it comes after the book:
- A visit to Old Tucson, a western town where movies are filmed; LeVar dresses as a cowboy and explains why cowboys wore long sleeves, bandanas, chaps and sturdy hats.
- A song, "Everyone Says Howdy to a Cowboy."
- A stunt rider showing trick riding and standing on two horses at once.
- Children's reviews of *Bossy Boots*, *Dakota Dugout* and *Rodeo*.
