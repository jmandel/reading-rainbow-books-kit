## About the book

Brian finds a little orange salamander on the forest floor and carries it home, saying, "Come live with me." His mother asks gentle questions: where will it sleep, where will it play, what will it eat? For each one Brian has an answer, and each answer brings a little more of the woods into his bedroom. Moss, leaves, crickets and bullfrogs come first, and the questions keep going from there.

Kirkus described the pictures as "luminous, carefully imagined paintings" that show the room being transformed, and called the book "a valid lesson, delivered with sensitivity to both the child and his captive." Publishers Weekly, reviewing the paperback in 1994, called it an "atmospherically illustrated" tale and a "fitting tribute to a child's perseverance and imagination," for ages 4 to 8.

The question-and-answer pattern makes it easy to follow by ear. Animal lovers will have plenty to picture. Knopf first published it in 1991.

## Author and illustrator

Anne Mazer was born in 1953 in Schenectady, New York, the daughter of novelists Harry Mazer and Norma Fox Mazer. She studied art at Syracuse University and French language and literature at the Sorbonne in Paris, where she began writing. She did not think of picture books until after her first child was born. *Watch Me* was her first published work. She has written forty-five books, including the twenty-two-book Amazing Days of Abby Hayes series and *The No-Nothings and Their Baby*. *The Salamander Room* was a 1993 ABC Children's Choice book and a Reading Rainbow Feature selection.

Steve Johnson and Lou Fancher illustrate books together. Kirkus credited the paintings in the first edition to Johnson. The pair also illustrated *My Many Colored Days*, written by Dr. Seuss and published by Knopf in 1996. Their picture book *The Dancing Tiger*, written by Malachy Doyle, won the Nestlé Children's Book Prize Silver Award and was longlisted for the Kate Greenaway Medal. They also illustrated Kathleen Krull's *The Boy on Fairfield Street*, about Dr. Seuss as a child.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in: "As you can see, making a home for animals is complicated. You have to create a whole environment to give them what they need. And that's what Brian found out in this book." The lead-in follows a visit to a zoo habitat that was cut, so children hear "as you can see" without having seen anything.

The book is read aloud by Lynne Thigpen, from "Brian found a salamander in the woods" to Brian's bed under the stars, with the salamander asleep beside him. The audio ends there. Nothing in it is frightening or sad.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar tours Jungle World, an indoor rainforest at the Bronx Zoo, where a science teacher explains habitats for proboscis monkeys, Malayan tapirs and langurs. A film shows artists building fake trees, vines and rocks, and another follows scientists on a blimp-carried raft studying the rainforest canopy. Child reviewers then discuss the review books.
