## About the book

A small boy asks to tell the story himself this time: how he came to his mama and papa. Before he ever met them, they were pictures he held in his hand, a new mother and father, a red dog, a white house with a green tree out front, and a bed with a teddy bear quilt waiting just for him. To reach them he has to fly for a day and a night, through blue skies, clouds, moon and stars, too frightened of the roaring plane to sleep. Then he arrives in a house full of strange things and dark corners.

The book suits children who were adopted and any child who likes hearing the story of how they joined their family. Kirkus called it a "beloved family story" told by "an adopted preschooler," with "quiet, gracefully cadenced text." Publishers Weekly praised its "quiet charm," and in the Horn Book Ellen Fader wrote that it "illuminates in a lyrical and compassionate way" a faraway child's adjustment to a new home.

## Author and illustrator

Ann Warren Turner was born in 1945 in Northampton, Massachusetts, to a printer and an artist. She studied at Oxford, Bates College and the University of Massachusetts, and taught high school English before turning to writing. Her first book, *Vultures*, was illustrated by her mother. She has written more than 40 books, many of them historical picture books. Her novel *A Hunter Comes Home* and her historical poem *Dakota Dugout* were both named ALA Notable Children's Books. The National Council for the Social Studies gave *Through Moon and Stars and Night Skies* a 1990 Notable Book citation. Listeners who like this book's quiet, first-person voice could try *Dakota Dugout* next.

James Graham Hale was new to picture books here: Kirkus called him a "new illustrator" with "warm, understated illustrations," and Publishers Weekly called him a "newcomer" with "gentle watercolors." He went on to illustrate science picture books for young children, including *From Seed to Pumpkin* by Wendy Pfeffer and *How Mountains Are Made* by Kathleen Weidner Zoehfeld.

## In this episode

LeVar opens at family skate day in Prospect Park, where parents and kids admit what bugs them about each other (a mom who calls everyone by the wrong name, clothes left on the bathroom floor) and what they love (bike rides together, a dad who is "my best friend"). He notes that families are created in many ways, and that this story tells how one began when parents who wanted a child found a child who needed parents. Andrew Lei reads the book.

Not in the podcast audio: visits with three adoptive families. The Harris family retell how they adopted Andrew and then baby Gavin. Cindy Peek talks with some of her nine adopted children, and the Abney family welcome baby Tess.
