# Tight Times

## About the book

A little boy wants a dog. His mother says "not now," and his father explains that the family is living through "tight times": bulk cereal instead of little boxes, the sprinkler instead of the lake, soupy lima beans instead of Sunday roast beef, and a sitter after school because Mommy has a job. The boy is not convinced. Then one afternoon his father comes home in the middle of the day, and the reason turns out to be bigger than anything on that list.

The whole book is told in the boy's own voice. Zena Sutherland, in the *Bulletin of the Center for Children's Books*, wrote that "the first-person monologue is convincingly that of a child," and *Booklist* said the story "has a sweet, sad poignancy." It was first published by Viking in 1979, in 32 pages. It suits children who have heard "we can't afford it" and gives parents a gentle way into talking about money worries.

## Author and illustrator

Barbara Shook Hazen was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1930 and studied at Smith College and Columbia University. She was a poetry editor at *Ladies' Home Journal* and then a children's book editor at Western Publishing, the home of Golden Books, before turning freelance in 1960. Her publisher describes her as "the author of more than 80 books for young readers." *Even If I Did Something Awful* won a Christopher Award in 1981, and *The Knight Who Was Afraid of the Dark* received a Children's Choice Award in 1990; the knight book makes a good next pick.

Trina Schart Hyman (1939–2004) grew up near Philadelphia and studied art in Philadelphia, Boston and Stockholm. Her favorite childhood story was "Little Red Riding Hood," and she once spent a whole year wearing a red cape. She illustrated more than 150 books, was the first art director of *Cricket* magazine, and won the Caldecott Medal for *Saint George and the Dragon*. Her own *Little Red Riding Hood* was a Caldecott Honor book and is a fine follow-up here.

## In this episode

LeVar Burton opens by talking about wanting something badly and being told no: "Some kids just can't have pets," and no matter the reason, "it still hurts." Patrick McCaffrey reads the book; a snatch of the sitter's TV soap opera plays partway through. Parents should know the story includes a father losing his job and both parents crying.

Not in the podcast audio: a children's pet show with a turtle named Tim, a baby seagull named Sweet Pea, an imaginary hamster and two pet rocks; LeVar and a girl named Hillary trying games that cost nothing, like rag basketball and Double Dutch; a song about the free public library; LeVar's closing talk about the book; and the kids' book reviews.
