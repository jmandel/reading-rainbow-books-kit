## About the book

Wendell and Floyd are late for school again. Monday it was space creatures, Tuesday pirates, Wednesday a plague of frogs, and their teacher has had enough of the crazy excuses. When Wendell offers a secret shortcut he invented himself, the boys leave the alley behind and tumble toward school through blackberry thickets, a steep bank and a jungle that keeps growing wilder. Whether they arrive on time is for the listener to find out.

Kirkus Reviews called it "an invigorating massage to the imagination" and said it takes "the sidelong view of life", with "tactile acrylic illustrations" painted from worm's-eye and bird's-eye angles. The same review predicted that readers "who have found themselves tardy and lacking suitable excuses will prize the brio of Wendell and Floyd." Kirkus lists it for ages 5 and up.

## Author and illustrator

Mark Teague wrote and illustrated *The Secret Shortcut*, published by Scholastic in 1996, so one person covers both jobs. According to Encyclopedia.com's biography, he was born in 1963 in La Mesa, California, and earned a history degree at the University of California, Santa Cruz in 1985. He moved to New York City the next year and worked in the display department of the Barnes and Noble at Rockefeller Center, where he saw a great many new children's books. He has worked as a freelance illustrator and writer since 1989. His first picture book, *The Trouble with the Johnsons*, was published by Scholastic that same year and drew on his time in Brooklyn.

He won a Christopher Award in 2003 for *Dear Mrs. LaRue: Letters from Obedience School*, and it makes a good next book. He also illustrated Cynthia Rylant's Poppleton books, Jane Yolen's "How Do Dinosaurs" books and Shana Corey's "First Graders from Mars" books. Among his other self-illustrated picture books are *Moog-Moog, Space Barber*, *Frog Medicine* and *Baby Tamer*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar in Central Park, saying he is "in one of my favorite places in the world" and is supposed to meet friends for a picnic across the park. A shortcut, he says, is what the kids in this book tried. Tim Conway reads *The Secret Shortcut* in full, and the audio ends on its last line, "Even so, they never did find a really good shortcut."

Content note: the story has playful peril only, such as a jungle, quicksand and crocodiles, plus a teacher's repeated warnings about being late.

Not in the podcast audio: kids on an orienteering course in Central Park's Ramble, finding flags with maps and compasses; a passer-by giving walking directions, and kids describing the way to a friend's house; a tour of the Pico Primate Reserve at the Philadelphia Zoo, and how architects use blueprints and models; and three kid book reviews.
