## About the book

On a night with a full moon, the farm is quiet. The hound dog is dreaming of rabbits, the farmer and his family are asleep, and only a skinny kid with a head full of questions is awake. An owl tells him to come closer, and he hears a fiddle tuning up in the cornfield, played by the scarecrow. Soon the critters of the farm are gathering for a dance.

The verse is full of rhyme and rhythm, which suits toddlers who like to bounce, clap and hear a line again. Kirkus Reviews called it "a cheery picture book that's a winning combination of magic and humor" that "should be useful for bedtime and library story-time." The review noted that midnight blue and moonlight yellow dominate the pictures, for "a nice, mysterious mood with no threatening overtones," and that "the text has the rhythm, bounce and rhyme of a good square-dance call." Henry Holt published it in 1986.

## Author and illustrator

Bill Martin Jr. (1916-2004) was born in Hiawatha, Kansas, and had difficulty reading until college, where he memorized poems a teacher read aloud. He earned a doctorate in early childhood education in 1961 and wrote more than 300 children's books, including *Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?* with Eric Carle. He met John Archambault in 1983 at UC Riverside, and the two collaborated on more than a dozen books.

John Archambault grew up in Pasadena, California, studied writing and journalism at UC Riverside, and edited the campus newspaper. He is a children's author, poet, storyteller and musician, and he once taught first grade in the Bronx. His best-known book with Martin is *Chicka Chicka Boom Boom* (1989).

Ted Rand (1916-2005) was born on Mercer Island, Washington. He taught at the University of Washington for 20 years, began illustrating children's books in his sixties, illustrated 78 of them, and received the Kerlan Award posthumously in 2005.

Martin, Archambault and Rand also made *The Ghost-Eye Tree* (1988) and *Knots on a Counting Rope*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "if you like good music and great dancing," he says, today is a hay ride down to Smiley Hollow for a good old-fashioned Tennessee barn dance, with Bill and Jim coming along. Bill and Jim, the hay ride and the wagon are on screen only, so a child hears the invitation without the pictures. He then introduces the book, which is read by Roy Clark. The audio ends on the book's last line, with the wonders of the barn dance dancing in the boy's head.

Not in the podcast audio: a bluegrass band showing its instruments, a fiddle maker building a fiddle from maple, a clogging lesson with caller's calls such as "birdie in the cage," and a live barn dance. Young reviewers recommend three more books.
