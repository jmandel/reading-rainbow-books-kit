## About the book
Helen's dog Martha eats a bowl of alphabet soup, and the letters travel to her brain instead of her stomach. That evening she speaks up: "Isn't it time for my dinner?" The family is delighted and shows Martha off to friends. Then she says exactly what is on her mind, remarking on a guest's size and a gift fruitcake, and the family loses patience. Told to be quiet, Martha stops talking.

The jokes land on both levels. Children hear a dog say what dogs might think ("Come, sit, stay. You never say please."). The book is a gentle way to talk about saying the right thing at the right time, and it is a good fit for listeners who like animals, silly voices and letters.

The Horn Book Magazine called it "Good-natured and amusing, with cheerful illustrations." Common Sense Media rates the book for ages 4 and up. It was published by Houghton Mifflin in 1992 and is the first of six Martha books.

## Author and illustrator
Susan Meddaugh wrote and illustrated *Martha Speaks* herself. She grew up in Montclair, New Jersey, and, painfully shy as a child, found an outlet in art. She studied French at Wheaton College, took studio art classes, and afterward worked as a children's book designer at Houghton Mifflin in Boston under the editor Walter Lorraine. After about a decade of designing other people's books, she began illustrating and then writing her own.

According to Wheaton Quarterly, the idea came when her son Niko, then seven, asked at lunch: "Mom, if Martha ate alphabet soup, would she speak?" The character was based on Meddaugh's own stray dog, also named Martha. The Quarterly reports that *Martha Speaks* sold very well and that The New York Times named it one of the Best Illustrated Children's Books of the Year. By 2009 she had written and illustrated 20 of her own picture books.

Listeners who enjoy Martha can continue with *Martha Calling*, the next book in the series. She was also a creative producer on the animated *Martha Speaks* series on PBS Kids.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with the host, at home with his dog Luke, getting ready for a barbecue: "Well, Luke, we've got a perfect day for a barbecue, don't we?" He starts to introduce a book about a cat, is called away, and turns to the dog book instead. Jo Hayden reads *Martha Speaks* aloud and the audio ends on the book's last line about Martha not saying anything "at least for a few minutes."

Content note: in the story, the family tells Martha to be quiet and she is hurt, and a burglar breaks into the house and shuts her in the kitchen.

Not in the podcast audio: a professional trainer of movie dogs demonstrates basic tricks such as speak, sit up and take a bow; a dog's-eye walk with Luke, who meets a frog and a neighbor; three young reviewers describe the review books below.
