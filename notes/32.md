## About the book
*The Milk Makers* (Macmillan, 1985) follows milk from pasture to refrigerator case. It opens on dairy cows grazing in a meadow and explains that a cow makes milk once she is two years old and has had a calf, whose food it first is. The book then covers the five common dairy breeds, what cows eat in summer and winter, milking by hand and by machine, and the tank truck's trip to the dairy, where the milk is pasteurized, homogenized and packed.

It suits curious listeners who like to know how everyday things work. The *Christian Science Monitor* (1985) said it "takes milk off the supermarket shelf and back to the pasture" and called it "interesting and informative for adults as well as children." *School Library Journal* wrote, "At last! Here is an interesting, informative book on milk production for young readers." *Booklist* said that "Gibbons' sturdy artwork and concise writing style are used to best advantage."

## Author and illustrator
Gail Gibbons wrote and illustrated the book herself. She was born in Oak Park, Illinois, and earned a B.F.A. from the University of Illinois in 1967. She worked as a television artist in Champaign, Chicago and New York, and her website says children on a children's show asked whether she had thought of writing children's books. Her first, *Willy and His Wheel Wagon*, appeared in 1975. Her site says more than 170 of her books have been published, and that she writes nonfiction because she loves researching and asking questions.

Encyclopedia.com's Gale entry says she spends several months on research, using libraries, personal experience and interviews with experts. It lists *The Milk Makers* among her American Library Association Notable Book citations (1985), and names *Clocks and How They Go* and *The Honey Makers*, which follows a worker bee through its jobs in a hive, among her other books.

## In this episode
The podcast opens inside LeVar's lead-in, at "Good. That good," and the line that the bottle is all gone, which refers to the cut opening. He says one of the cows over there will give more milk, then explains that elephants, armadillos, porcupines, bats and whales also make milk and are mammals, and so are cows. Alaina Reed reads the book, which ends on milk in store coolers, ready to buy and enjoy.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar tries hand milking with a dairy farmer, then a milking machine at a neighboring farm. A basketball broadcast turns milk's nutrients into a team. LeVar visits a cheese factory in Sonoma, California, and a farm feeding 600 cows. Young reviewers discuss *Whales and Other Sea Mammals*, *Baby Animals on the Farm* and *From Blossom to Honey*, and the episode ends with newborn calves.
