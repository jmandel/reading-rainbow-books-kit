## About the book

Badger is dependable, reliable and very old, and he knows he will die soon. He is not afraid of it. His only worry is how his friends will feel once he is gone, so he has told Mole, Frog and Fox that someday he will go down the long tunnel and that he hopes they will not be too sad. Then one morning he does not come out to say good morning.

A gentle picture book about a first encounter with death, aimed by *Publishers Weekly* at listeners aged four and up.

Reviews differ. *Publishers Weekly* (1992) wrote that "warm and sensitive illustrations reflect the hopeful mood of this tale" and suggested ages four and up. Kirkus Reviews (1984) was unimpressed, calling it "Hollow echoes of The Wind in the Willows" and finding the sentiment "Facile, trite emotionalism." Judges of the 1985 Mother Goose Award thought the treatment of grief "just distanced enough" for young children "and in no way mawkish."

## Author and illustrator

Susan Varley wrote and illustrated *Badger's Parting Gifts*. She was born in 1961 in Blackpool, England, and studied graphic design and illustration at Manchester Polytechnic. The book was her first, published by Andersen Press in 1984, and it won the 1985 Mother Goose Award, given to the "most exciting newcomer to British children's book illustration." Wikipedia reports that some booksellers refused to stock it at the time because of its subject.

The book is still in print. In 2019 a 35th anniversary edition appeared in partnership with the charity Child Bereavement UK, and Wikipedia notes that grief counsellors and teachers continue to cite it for talking with children about death.

Varley has also illustrated books by other authors, all published by Andersen. They include *The Monster Bed* by Jeanne Willis, *Lovely Old Lion* and *Two Small Pandas* by Julia Jarman, and *Captain Small Pig* by Martin Waddell, which may suit listeners looking for another book with her animal characters.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton saying he finally has enough firewood to last the weekend. He loves being there because it reminds him of his grandparents' farm in Missouri, and he explains how his big sister's trouble pronouncing "grandmother" and "grandfather" gave them the names ah-mami and poppy. He then introduces a story of friends who each find a gift left behind.

Ruby Dee reads the book; the audio ends on its last line, "And somehow, Badger did."

Content note: the audio includes a death, the friends' sadness, and Mole crying in bed.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's talk about what to do when someone dies, a boy performing a monologue in the voice of his late uncle, and Staten Island Academy students making remembrance projects such as a gavel, a memory box and a snow globe. Also cut: a dancer from the Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater, LeVar's sweet potato pie recipe, and children's book reviews.
