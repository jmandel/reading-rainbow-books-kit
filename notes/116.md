## About the book

A boy and his father live in an airport. They have no home, and the airport is better than the streets, so they follow one rule: don't get noticed. They wear matching blue clothes, wash up in the terminal bathrooms, and keep moving like everyone else. A small bird trapped inside the terminal gives the boy something to watch and hope for.

Kirkus Reviews wrote favorably of the 1991 Clarion Books edition and singled out the illustrations: quiet browns and blues for the airport, and homeless figures drawn with undefined faces and averted eyes. Kirkus called it a "poignant narrative" and said Himler "matches Bunting's understated text with gentle sensibility." Publishers Weekly described it as "a naturalistic look at the plight of the homeless" and recommended it for ages 5 to 8.

It suits children ready for a gentle, honest story about families without housing, and parents who want to start that conversation. The text is spare and the pictures are calm.

## Author and illustrator

Eve Bunting was born on December 19, 1928, in Maghera, Northern Ireland, and moved to the United States in 1958 with her husband and three children. Her first published book, *The Two Giants*, appeared in 1971. She wrote more than 250 books, mostly for children and young people, and won the Golden Kite Award in 1976 for *One More Flight*. She died on October 1, 2023, at age 94. Young listeners might also like her picture books *Dandelions*, *A Turkey for Thanksgiving* and *Night Tree*.

Ronald Himler was born on November 16, 1937, in Cleveland, Ohio, and earned a diploma from the Cleveland Institute of Art in 1960. He worked as a technical sculptor at General Motors and as a toy designer before working as an artist and illustrator from 1963. He has illustrated more than 100 books, often for Eve Bunting, and has also written and illustrated his own, including *The Girl on the Yellow Giraffe*. He received a Society of Illustrators Silver Medal in 1992.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with a lead-in that begins, "Most of us take having a home for granted." It explains what homeless means, why most homeless families are poor, and why homeless people try hard not to get caught, then points to a boy and his father in this book.

Daniel Saltzman reads the book, from "My dad and I live in an airport" to the last line about the bird singing. The audio ends there.

Content note: the story mentions a mother who has died, a father who is sad and angry after calling about apartments, a boy who sometimes wants to cry, and people caught or moved along by security.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's closing remarks about homeless families after the story, and the rest of the television episode.
