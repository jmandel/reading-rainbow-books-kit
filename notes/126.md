## About the book
*On the Day You Were Born* is a lyrical welcome for a new baby, written and illustrated by Debra Frasier with collage art. It opens before the birth, with word of the coming baby passing from animal to animal: reindeer, humpback whales, Pacific salmon, monarch butterflies. Then the whole world gets ready. The Earth turns toward the baby's morning sky, gravity holds the baby to the ground, and the moon pulls the tide to wash the beaches clean.

The text is simple and rhythmic, with the repeated phrase "On the day you were born" giving young listeners something to wait for. The book's page describes use for newborns and birthdays, and a glossary explains gravity, tides and migration. It quotes School Library Journal: "A book filled with reverence for the natural order of the world and the place of the individual in it." It lists a Gold Parents' Choice Award among its honors.

## Author and illustrator
Debra Frasier was born on April 3, 1953, in Vero Beach, Florida. She earned a design degree at Florida State University in 1976 and studied textiles at the Penland School of Crafts in North Carolina. Before picture books she made large costume puppets and wind sculptures. She married photography professor Jim Henkel in 1984, and they lived in Minneapolis before moving to Asheville, North Carolina, in 2015.

Their daughter, Calla, inspired this book. During a difficult pregnancy Frasier was in the hospital for many weeks, unable to keep food or water down. At her lowest point she asked a nurse for paper and wrote down all the things on earth that would welcome the child. After Calla was born safely, she shaped those notes and drawings into the book, published in 1991.

Other Frasier picture books to look for are *Out of the Ocean* (1998), which won a Minnesota Book Award for illustration, *Miss Alaineus: A Vocabulary Disaster* (2000), which also drew on her daughter, and *The Incredible Water Show* (2004).

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "There is nothing more wonderful, more exciting, more marvelous than the birth of a baby." He adds that the day you are born is really your first birthday and introduces a story about how the whole world celebrates every new baby. The first words heard, "here to say welcome," begin mid-sentence and refer to cut material, so a child hears them without context. Patrick Stewart reads the book, and the audio ends mid-line on the whispered welcome, "We are so glad you've."

Not in the podcast audio, the rest of the episode follows a real family expecting a fourth daughter, Jessica:
- A doctor's visit with a heartbeat check and ultrasound.
- The three older sisters in a class on holding, feeding and diapering a baby, then a tour of the hospital room and nursery window.
- Labor and Jessica's birth, with the sisters meeting her afterward.
- Three children reviewing the books below.
