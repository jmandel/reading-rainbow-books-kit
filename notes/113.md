## About the book

Alejandro lives in a small adobe house beside a lonely desert road, with a well, a windmill and one companion, a burro. He plants a garden to pass the quiet hours. Then a ground squirrel slips out of the underbrush for a drink, and for a moment the loneliness lifts. More small visitors follow, and Alejandro begins to wonder about the larger animals of the desert and what he could do for them.

The book suits young listeners who like gentle stories and animal names: roadrunners, Gila woodpeckers, a desert tortoise, coyotes, bobcats and peccaries. Kirkus called it "A likable, beautifully presented picture of a human being responding to his natural surroundings with sensitivity and imagination," and singled out the pen-and-watercolor spreads and a closing appendix identifying 26 species. Publishers Weekly described it as using "the appealing story of a lonely man as the framework for an edifying lesson on desert wildlife." Chronicle Books published it in 1994.

## Author and illustrator

Richard E. Albert wrote *Alejandro's Gift*, which Kirkus described as his first children's book. Kirkus's author page lists only this title, and its review says he was 84 when the book appeared.

Sylvia Long is the illustrator. Chronicle Books describes her as the illustrator of many award-winning books for children that have sold more than 2.5 million copies, with detailed paintings inspired by her love of animals and the outdoors. She lives in Scottsdale, Arizona.

Her other picture books include *Fire Race* (1993, adapted by Jonathan London), *Snug as a Bug* (2004), *Waddle, Waddle, Quack, Quack, Quack* (2005) and *Sylvia Long's Thumbelina* (2010). For nature-loving listeners, there is *An Egg Is Quiet* (2006, written by Dianna Hutts Aston), which received a starred Kirkus review calling it a "delight for budding naturalists of all stripes, flecks, dots, and textures." Chronicle also lists it among *Scholastic Parent & Child* magazine's 100 Greatest Books for Kids. Kirkus noted that nearly all the eggs in it are shown at actual size.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in. His car is overheating in the desert, he has not far to go, and he will finish the trip on foot to get water for his Jeep. He adds that a friend can help with finding water, which is what happens to the animals in this book. The Jeep comes from a cut opening, so a child hears it without context.

The book is read by Francisco Rivela, from the title card to the final line about a gift received rather than given. Content note: the story is about loneliness, and the animals are at first afraid and stay away.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks on water after the book, a montage of animals drinking, a visit to an oasis at Indian Canyons near Palm Springs, California, where a naturalist shows him mesquite beans and stone mortar holes, a look at Niagara Falls, and three child book reviews.
