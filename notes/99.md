## About the book

On a bluff at the bend in a river stands a turtle carved of stone. An old man shapes him so that he can be the eyes of Manitou, the All-Father, watching over the Delaware people who come to the rock each summer and speaking for them. Season follows season, and the turtle is happiest in summer, when children and then their children visit. Then fewer and fewer come, and strangers arrive who do not greet him. From there the story follows what the turtle sees and feels as the land around him changes.

It suits children who like quiet, serious stories with a strong sense of place. Kirkus Reviews, in a starred review, wrote that "Moser's beautifully balanced book design and powerful paintings ably contribute to the story's dignity and purpose." Publishers Weekly described "thoughtful prose frequently imbued with a poetic tone" and said "the text's quiet wisdom is reflected in Moser's exquisite watercolors." Kirkus listed the book for ages 6 to 10.

## Author and illustrator

Sheila MacGill-Callahan wrote the text. The book appeared in 1991, and Puffin issued a 32-page paperback in 1996.

Barry Moser, born in 1940 in Chattanooga, Tennessee, is an American illustrator, printmaker and educator known for wood engravings. He studied printmaking with Leonard Baskin and founded the Pennyroyal Press, a small engraving and book press, in 1970. He was on the faculty of the Department of Illustration Studies at the Rhode Island School of Design and was Professor in Residence and Printer to the College at Smith College until he retired in 2025.

His best-known work is a pair of illustrated editions of Lewis Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* and *Through the Looking-Glass*, each with more than a hundred prints. His *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* won the National Book Award for design and illustration in 1983. He has illustrated nearly 300 other works.

Young listeners who enjoy his paintings may like other children's books he illustrated, including *Polly Vaughn* (1992), *Hummingbird Nest* (2004) and *The Cheshire Cheese Cat* (2011).

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "These are the deep woods of western America." He explains that great Native American civilizations once lived across the country, that newcomers took the land and used more than they needed, and that the forest's trees and animals have watched their world change. He then introduces the story of one of those creatures. "These deep woods" refers to footage that is not included, so the opening arrives without context.

The book is read by Michael Ansara, and the audio ends on the last line, "and he will speak of them to Manitou."

Content note: the turtle weeps, is blinded by spray paint and feels his heart crack, before he is rescued.

Not in the podcast audio: a talk about what turtle, wolf and bear mean to Native American tribes; a Florida team hatching and raising bald eagles for release; city children planting a linden tree; and kid book reviews.
