## About the book
Every fall, Gabrielle and her mother ride two trains into the city to Grandpa's tailor shop, where Gabby gets a new navy blue coat. This year Gabby has other ideas. She wants a purple coat with purple buttons, a purple hood and a big pocket, and she is a little nervous about saying so. Grandpa has a way of nudging people toward something new, and the question is how Mama will take it.

Young listeners get a gentle story about wanting to choose for yourself, told with warm, believable characters and the small pleasures of a tailor's shop: bolts of fabric, scraps of paper, and sandwiches from the corner deli. Kirkus (August 15, 1986) praised the book's "glowing watercolors." In the New York Times Book Review, Ellen Feldman called it a "triumph of imagination, resourcefulness and hope," as quoted by Encyclopedia.com. It suits listeners who like family stories, and the refrain "Once in a while it's good to try something new" is easy for small ears to catch.

## Author and illustrator
Amy Hest was born in New York City on April 28, 1950, and earned a B.A. from Hunter College in 1971 and a library degree in 1972. She was a children's librarian at the New York Public Library from 1972 to 1975, and became a full-time writer in 1977. Her picture books often concern family bonds, especially with grandparents, and several are set in New York City. She won the 1996 Boston Globe/Horn Book Award for *In the Rain with Baby Duck*. Younger listeners may enjoy her *Kiss Good Night*, illustrated by Anita Jeram.

Amy Schwartz (1954-2023) was born in San Diego and earned a Bachelor of Fine Arts in drawing from the California College of Arts and Crafts in 1976. She moved to New York, had her first publication in 1982, and published about sixty children's books. Her work won the Charlotte Zolotow Award in 2004 for *What James Likes Best*. Hest and Schwartz also made *Nana's Birthday Party* (1993) and *Gabby Growing Up* (1998).

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar in New York City: "Hi, I'm in the Big Apple, New York City, and I'm on my way to a special neighborhood where they create today the fashions that you and I will be wearing tomorrow." He rides the subway and introduces the book as a girl's trip into the city on an important errand. The neighborhood he mentions is never shown, since that part of the episode is cut. Jack Gilford then reads the whole book, and the audio ends on its last line, "And that is just what she decides to do."

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to New York's garment district, students at the Fashion Institute of Technology preparing a final-exam fashion show, a sculptor who builds papier-mache figures, a stop at a custom coat shop, and children's reviews of the other featured books.

Content note: nothing beyond a girl's nervous hesitation before asking for what she wants.
