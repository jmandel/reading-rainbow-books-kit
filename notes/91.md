## About the book

Grace loves stories. She likes them read to her, told to her, or made up in her own head, and she acts them out in the backyard, always giving herself the most exciting part. When her teacher announces a class play of *Peter Pan*, Grace raises her hand for the title role. Two classmates tell her she cannot be Peter, one because she is a girl and one because she is black. Grace goes home deflated, and her mother and her Nana have something to say about it.

The book suits children who love dressing up and pretending, and it gives parents an easy way to talk about fairness and about believing in yourself.

Publishers Weekly (1991) called Hoffman's tale "truly inspiring" and described the illustrations as "evocative, carefully detailed watercolor paintings," recommending the book for ages 4 to 8.

## Author and illustrator

Mary Hoffman was born in 1945 in Eastleigh, Hampshire, England, and studied English at Newnham College, Cambridge. She spent eighteen years as reading consultant to the BBC schools series *Look and Read*. Her first book, *White Magic*, appeared in 1975, and she has written more than 90 books, including 24 picture books and the *Stravaganza* series for teenagers. *Amazing Grace* is among her best known works and is part of a series of Grace picture books. A finalist for the 1991 Kate Greenaway Medal, it brought her an award from Waldenbooks when it was published. A 2015 anniversary edition includes an afterword by LeVar Burton.

Caroline Binch, born in 1947, is an English illustrator who studied graphic design at Salford Technical College. For *Amazing Grace* she hired members of a family in Gambia as models for Grace and the other characters. She has also illustrated *Hue Boy*, which shared the 1993 Nestlé Smarties Book Prize in the youngest age category, and *Gregory Cool*, which the Greenaway judges commended.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar in a theater: "welcome to one of my favorite places, the theater." He talks about the spotlight and how scary it can be. Tyne Daly reads the book, and the audio ends on Nana's last line, that Grace can do anything she wants if she puts her mind to it.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's reflection after the reading, then Whoopi Goldberg, who talks about wanting to act and about how her hair and look shaped her career. A teenage girl who plays on her high school's boys' ice hockey team follows, and then a woman who works as a heating plant technician in a housing project.

Content note: in the audio, classmates say Grace cannot play Peter Pan because she is a girl and because she is black. Her family answers that she can.
