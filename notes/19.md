## About the book

Ralph is an opera-loving mouse who works as the short-order cook at a diner on a pier. One foggy night a dark ship glides up to the dock, and two rough customers rush out without paying for their fish and fries. The same newspaper Ralph is reading reports that his favorite singer has vanished, and when he follows the rats he lands in more trouble than he bargained for.

Kirkus Reviews called it "hoary but dateless hijinks in the Don Freeman mode," a mixed verdict on the story's familiarity, but found the "broadly stroked, lightly sketched full-color pictures, strong on mood and spontaneity," along with "a teasing refrain" of "Or was it?" that adds to the "brisk, exuberant (but unfrenzied, unmalicious) derring-do."

It is a pretend-scary adventure full of fog, foghorns and sound effects, and suits listeners who like a clear hero, a clear villain and a lot of noise. Bookroo lists it for ages 4 to 8.

## Author and illustrator

Thacher Hurd wrote and illustrated the book. Born March 6, 1949, in Burlington, Vermont, he is the son of the children's book creators Clement Hurd and Edith Thacher Hurd. Margaret Wise Brown dedicated her 1949 book *My World* to him as a newborn. He attended the California College of Arts and Crafts, and his first book, *The Old Chair*, appeared in 1978.

*Mystery on the Docks* was published by Harper & Row in 1983. A second Hurd title, *Mama Don't Allow* (1984), was also a Reading Rainbow book. Other books to look for include *Art Dog* (1996), *Little Mouse's Birthday Cake* and *Moo Cow Kaboom!* (2003).

In 2008 he used his father's artwork from *Goodnight Moon* to make *Goodnight Moon 123: A Counting Book*. His book *Zoom City* was named a New York Times Best Illustrated Book of the Year, and inspired the name of Zoom Video Communications. In 1983 he and his wife, Olivia, founded Peaceable Kingdom Press, which prints cards and posters from children's books.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the lead-in: "The night was dark and cold. The fog was thick as homemade soup." The speaker is in Charleston Harbor, hot on the trail of someone called Big Mama Blue, and then turns to *Mystery on the Docks* for a clue. Big Mama Blue is a crane from the cut field segment, so the name arrives unexplained. Raul Julia reads the book, and the audio ends on the last line about Ralph and his friends toasting the hero who saved Eduardo from Big Al and his gang of nasty rats.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks after the story, a visit to the crane Big Mama Blue at the Port of Charleston, a tugboat ride guiding a freighter, children singing about what they love, and child reviews of the other books.

Content note: Ralph is chased, captured and shut in a ship's hold, and Eduardo has been kidnapped for ransom. It ends happily.
