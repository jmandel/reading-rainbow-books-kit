## About the book

*Paul Bunyan* is a tall tale, the kind of story where each teller stretches the facts a little further. Steven Kellogg's version begins with Paul as "the largest, smartest, and strongest baby ever born in the state of Maine," a child who wanders the neighborhood hauling off trees in a lumber wagon. The first turn comes on a morning of blue snow, when Paul finds a shivering ox calf in a drift and adopts him as Babe. From there Paul gathers a crew of legendary lumbermen and sets out to clear a path across the country, meeting underground ogres, a griddle big enough to feed an army and a blizzard that lasts for years along the way.

It suits listeners who enjoy big, silly exaggeration and a cheerful, rollicking pace. The publisher's description calls Paul "America's finest, fastest, funniest lumberman." The character grew out of stories told in lumber camps, and by 1916 advertising writer William B. Laughead was using Paul in a pamphlet for the Red River Lumber Company, where the blue ox got the name Babe.

## Author and illustrator

Steven Kellogg retold and illustrated this book himself. He was born in 1941 in Norwalk, Connecticut, and grew up in Darien, where he loved "telling stories on paper" for his younger sisters. He won a scholarship to study in Italy for a year, graduated from the Rhode Island School of Design in 1963, and illustrated his first book, *Gwot! and Other Horribly Funny Hair Ticklers*, in 1967. He has created more than 90 children's books.

His Great Dane puppy inspired the Pinkerton books, beginning with *Pinkerton, Behave!* in 1979, a good next stop for young listeners who like his comic style. He kept returning to American tall tales: *Paul Bunyan* came out in 1984, followed by *Pecos Bill* in 1986 and *Johnny Appleseed* in 1988. The Catholic Library Association gave him its Regina Medal in 1989 for the body of his work, and his books have been named Reading Rainbow featured selections.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the host in the woods of Maine: "Hey, you'd better put on something warm because today we're going to be in the great outdoors in the state of Maine to celebrate trees." He explains that loggers harvest trees the way farmers harvest a crop, then introduces tall tales and Paul Bunyan, "the legendary logger who cleared a path across our country."

Buddy Ebsen reads the book. The audio ends on its last line, about Paul and Babe still roaming the Alaskan mountains.

Content note: in the story, underground ogres snatch Paul's crew, a years-long blizzard buries the mountains, and Babe becomes depressed.

Not in the podcast audio: children saying what they would do if they were as big as Paul, a forest fire drill in Maine with rangers and water-dropping helicopters, seedlings being planted, a logging contest at the University of Maine, and child reviews of the books below.
