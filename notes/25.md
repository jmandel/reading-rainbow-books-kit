## About the book

Perfect is a pig so small that his own mother does not notice him. While his littermates shove and squeal for food, he stays gentle and quiet, lying under a favorite tree and wishing for wings. When he helps a fallen sow back onto her feet, she offers him a wish, and the wings arrive. They do not make him welcome with the other pigs or the birds, so he flies on until he lands on a fire escape in a big city, where an artist named Olive takes him in and names him. Then a heavy fog rolls in.

This is a book for listeners who like an animal hero who is kind rather than clever, and a story that carries a little worry before it settles. Booklist wrote that "Jeschke's pencil drawings are rough-hewn and pithy, their homely figures at once distinctive and endearing," and Publishers Weekly said "Jeschke's tender original comedies deserve to be saluted by a big brass band." Publishers Weekly lists it for ages 3 to 7.

## Author and illustrator

Susan Jeschke wrote and illustrated *Perfect the Pig*, which Holt, Rinehart and Winston published in New York in 1980.

She is described as a self-taught artist who has worked as an industrial designer, fine artist, illustrator and children's book author. Her murals appear in places including Madison Square Garden in New York and the Taj Mahal casino in Atlantic City. She has also designed holiday window displays for Marshall Field's and Saks Fifth Avenue.

Listeners who enjoy her drawings can look for her other picture books. *Lucky's Choice*, a Scholastic title, is about a sheltered house cat who befriends a mouse, according to Publishers Weekly. She also illustrated books by other authors, among them *Busybody Nora* and *New Neighbors for Nora* by Johanna Hurwitz and *Wild Boy* by Joan Tate.

## In this episode

The podcast opens in the middle of the television episode, with LeVar's lead-in at a farm: "It's dinner time" and the busiest time of day for everybody, "including mama." Voices crack pig puns and a pigarick (a limerick about pigs) about a Tennessee pig is read. The farm footage was cut, so a child hears the jokes without seeing the animals. LeVar then introduces a tiny pig who gets to make a wish.

James Coco reads the whole book, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "where she and Perfect lived in peace and happiness."

Content note: Perfect is lost in fog, taken by a man who ties him up and cages him, and cries himself to sleep missing Olive. The man threatens the butcher.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's pig facts, a pig-themed gift shop, a puppet sketch in a library, more pigaricks, a pig song, and children's reviews of three other books.
