## About the book
Florence and her little brother Eric are spending summer vacation at Rosemary Cottage with Granny and Grandpa Mutton. Granny is tidying up for the Little Nibbling Knitting Circle when the telephone rings. Miss Lavinia Bleating has baked an angel food cake for the afternoon tea but has caught a frightful cold, so Florence and Eric are sent to collect it from her hall. Meanwhile Lavinia's sister Muriel comes home from buying a new hat for an evening at the opera.

This is a picture book built on a mix-up that a young listener can see coming well before the characters do, which is much of the fun. The names are sheep puns, from Mrs. Wooly-Jumper to the opera called the "Baa Baa of Seville". It suits listeners of about three to seven, the age range Publishers Weekly gave the book. That review found the premise somewhat improbable, but added that "Wild's droll illustrations carry the day." Dial Books for Young Readers published it in 1987.

## Author and illustrator
Jocelyn Wild is the author of *Florence and Eric Take the Cake*, and Publishers Weekly refers to "Wild's droll illustrations". Dial Books for Young Readers published it in New York in 1987, at 28 pages, according to Publishers Weekly's listing. The Internet Archive's catalogue record describes it as a story in which "a brother and sister lamb accidentally cause a major mix-up between a delicious cake and a beautiful hat."

Another picture book catalogued under the name Jocelyn Wild is *The Bears' Book of Colors*, published by Ideals Children's Books in 1989. It follows three bears named Griff, Snuff and Pawpaw as they play "I Spy" and learn about colors, a next step for listeners who enjoyed the animal cast here.

## In this episode
The lead-in is LeVar's, and it starts in the Movieland Wax Museum: "If you want to get a close up look at some of your favorite Hollywood stars, then this is the place to be. But these stars are special. Every single one of them is made out of wax." He then says that things that look alike can lead to hilarious situations, as in the book. The rest of the museum visit is cut.

Julia Child reads the book. The audio ends on the story's last line, that Florence thinks the ruined cake makes a lovely hat, and Eric agrees.

Content note: a woman screams, and a guest breaks a tooth on a cherry on the tough, uneatable cake.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar visits a bakery where cakes are sculpted to look like a cheeseburger and a race car, and then watches a sculptor make a wax figure of LeVar himself.
