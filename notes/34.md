## About the book
*Germs Make Me Sick!* starts with something every child knows: waking up with aching arms and legs, a sore head and a fever, and guessing that a germ is to blame. From there the book explains what germs are, how many fit across the top of a pencil, and how bacteria and viruses differ under a microscope. It follows the ways germs get past the body's defenses, such as a friend's sneeze, a shared straw or a scraped knee, and what a doctor does when a child stays sick. It suits curious preschoolers and early primary listeners, particularly any child who is sick, or about to visit the doctor.

Kirkus Reviews (1985) wrote that "Hafner's breezy drawings of sick kids and medical workers set a nice, cheery tone," and that "this is no sugar-coated placebo." The same review called it "a non-threatening first exposure, administered with a pleasant bedside manner." Crowell published the book in 1985.

## Author and illustrator
Melvin Berger was born in Brooklyn on August 23, 1927. He studied at City College of New York, earned a B.M. at the Eastman School of Music and an M.A. at Columbia University and played viola professionally. He taught music until 1979, then wrote full time. He has written over two hundred books for children and young adults on science and music, often with his wife, the author Gilda Berger. His titles include *Buzz! A Book About Insects*, *Chomp! A Book About Sharks* and *Why Do Volcanoes Blow Their Tops?*

Marylin Hafner was born in Brooklyn on December 14, 1925, and graduated from the Pratt Institute in 1947. She won a 1949 New York Herald Tribune Children's Spring Book Festival award for illustrating *Bonnie Bess: The Weathervane Horse*. She has illustrated dozens of books for other authors and wrote her own, including *Mommies Don't Get Sick*. About her approach she told *Something About the Author*: "My feeling about picture books is that the pictures can add another dimension without overpowering the author's intention."

## In this episode
The podcast opens with the host's lead-in: "There are all kinds of germs in the world. Some make you sneeze or feel sick to your stomach. Others give you the blahs." Molly Mandlin reads the whole book, which ends on the line about eating right, exercising and keeping clean.

Everything below was cut from the podcast audio. The TV episode opens with LeVar as a pretend detective hunting an invisible gang, which turns out to be pond water under a microscope (amoeba, hydra, rotifers and water bears), then asks children what they think a germ is. After the book, LeVar visits a medical technologist named Beverly, who grows bacteria in a dish and shows him slides and moldy fruit. A cartoon shows blood cells fighting germs, and an organic farmer explains how microbes make compost. Three young reviewers then recommend books.

Content note: the book mentions fever, aches, a rash, blood drops taken at the doctor and getting sick.
