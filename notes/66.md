## About the book
Ms. Frizzle's class has spent almost a month on animals' homes when she announces a new topic, the Earth, and a homework assignment: bring a rock to school. When the next day brings mostly excuses, the class heads out to collect rocks. With Ms. Frizzle, that never stays an ordinary trip. The bus spins like a top, turns into a steam shovel, and the children start digging through stripes of sandstone, shale and limestone toward a limestone cave and deeper layers of rock.

One of the students tells the story, and short student reports at the end pull the facts together. It suits curious children who like rocks, digging and caves, and the mix of story and science sets it apart.

The New York Times reviewer Katherine Bouton called the team's approach "the freshest, most amusing approach to science for children that I've seen." Kirkus Reviews was more mixed: with so much byplay on the pages, "the facts may be submerged by the fun," though readers who take it in will find "a good dose of basic geology here."

## Author and illustrator
Joanna Cole (1944-2020) grew up in East Orange, New Jersey, and loved science as a child. She worked as a librarian in a Brooklyn elementary school and as an editor before going freelance in 1980 to write children's books. Her first, about cockroaches, came out in 1971. She wrote more than 250 books and is best known for The Magic School Bus series, whose first book, *The Magic School Bus at the Waterworks*, was published in 1986. Cole named *Inside the Earth* her favorite of the series.

Bruce Degen (1945-2024) was born in Brooklyn and studied at Cooper Union and Pratt Institute. He taught art in New York City schools and began making children's books in 1975. He became the series illustrator in the mid-1980s. He and Cole made 14 books together.

Young listeners who enjoy this one might like Degen's own *Jamberry*, in print since 1983, or the *Jesse Bear* books he illustrated for Nancy White Carlstrom, starting with *Jesse Bear, What Will You Wear?*

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton at California Caverns, saying "Hi there. I hope you're ready for an unusual adventure." He notes that many people explore the earth on foot, unless they are students in Ms. Frizzle's class, and introduces the book. The audio then runs through the full reading and ends on the line "It had been a weird trip, but we got a great rock collection for our classroom."

Keshia Knight Pulliam reads the book.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's cave tour with a guide, past stalactites, stalagmites, flowstone and a marble "drum," and children's reviews of the three books below.

Content note: in the story the bus falls into a cave, stalls near an erupting volcano, and is carried out to sea on a lava flow before the class lands safely back at school.
