## About the book
*The Shaman's Apprentice: A Tale of the Amazon Rain Forest* opens in a village deep in the Amazon rainforest, where a boy named Kamana lies in his hammock burning with fever. His parents carry him to the village shaman, Natala, who gathers leaves, roots and bark from the forest and sings to the sickness as the medicine cooks. The boy recovers, and he never forgets who saved him. As he grows up, he slips away from the other boys to follow the shaman on his plant-gathering walks, hoping to learn the old man's wisdom.

Then the story turns. Strangers arrive by river, new illnesses spread through the village, and the people begin to wonder whose medicine is stronger. Kamana watches the shaman lose his place of honor at the council fire.

It suits children who like stories about learning from an elder and about knowledge handed from one generation to the next. LeVar introduces it as a true story.

## Author and illustrator
Lynne Cherry illustrated the book and wrote it with Mark J. Plotkin. Cherry was born in Philadelphia in 1952, attended the Tyler School of Art and earned an MA in history from Yale. She has written or illustrated more than thirty nature-themed children's books and founded the Center for Children's Environmental Literature. Her *The Great Kapok Tree*, about the Amazon rainforest, has sold over a million copies, and *The Sea, the Storm and the Mangrove Tangle* won the first Green Earth Book Award for picture books, in 2005.

Plotkin is an ethnobotanist, a scientist who studies how people use plants. He did research at Harvard under Richard Evans Schultes, earned a PhD at Tufts, and in 1995 co-founded the Amazon Conservation Team with Liliana Madrigal to work with Indigenous partners protecting Amazonian rainforest. His adult book, *Tales of a Shaman's Apprentice*, describes his fieldwork learning from shamans about healing plants.

Young listeners might next try Cherry's *A River Ran Wild*, *Flute's Journey* or *How Groundhog's Garden Grew*.

## In this episode
The podcast begins with LeVar's lead-in: "Are you ready for an adventure that you will never forget?" He says the group is heading to the Amazon rainforest, where the book tells of a shaman who treats his people with forest plants and the apprentice he teaches. The title card is read aloud, then Susan Sarandon reads the book, ending on its last line, "And so it was that Kamana became the shaman's apprentice."

Content note: the audio includes a boy with a high fever, a man from another tribe who dies of illness, and a village sickened by a disease the shaman cannot cure.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar visits the village of Quamala with Mark Plotkin, meets the chief, and sees arrow making and cassava bread. He meets the grown-up Kamana, now a healer teaching his twin sons, and joins him in the forest to find a plant for a sick boy.
