## About the book
Gregory is a young goat in a family where dinner means tin cans, bottle caps, old rugs and the evening newspaper. He would like orange juice, eggs, bananas and a nice piece of fish instead, and his parents are appalled. His parents take him to the doctor, and the book follows what they try.

The joke runs on a simple reversal: the goat who behaves badly at the table is the one eating healthy food. Kirkus says the author "takes the common notion that goats eat boots, tin cans, and shirts one absurd step farther." Kirkus was less taken with the book overall: it found the story similar in structure to the Kraus and Aruego picture books, said "not much distinction can be made," and called it "Ephemeral." Picky eaters may enjoy seeing the shoe on the other hoof. It suits young listeners who like silly food stories, and the reading is short and full of dialogue.

## Author and illustrator
Mitchell Sharmat was a Harvard graduate who wrote picture books, easy readers and novels, and contributed to many textbook reading programs. His author note says the idea for the story was planted on a vacation years earlier, when a young goat started nibbling on his son's coat. He also wrote *Berkley, the Terrible Sleeper*, a Ready-to-Read book illustrated by Renée Kurilla.

Jose Aruego was born in Manila in 1932 and earned a law degree at the University of the Philippines. He studied at Parsons School of Design and worked as a cartoonist before his first children's book, *The King and His Friends*, appeared in 1969. He also illustrated *Leo the Late Bloomer*, written by Robert Kraus.

Ariane Dewey was born in Chicago in 1937 and studied at Sarah Lawrence College. She and Aruego often shared the work: he drew the outlines and designed the pages, and she painted the color. She also wrote and illustrated *Pecos Bill* and *The Fish Peri* on her own.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar at the San Diego Zoo, reading a sign: "Goats love to eat clothing." He wonders whether goats really will eat almost anything and introduces the book as a story about a baby goat who is a picky eater. He talks to goats and signs the listener cannot see, so the opening may feel out of context. The book reading follows, and the audio ends on the last line, "he knew what he liked."

The book is read by Marilyn Michaels.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks after the story, a skit at Billy and Nanny's Barnyard Cafe, a zookeeper explaining what the zoo's goats really eat, the zoo kitchens feeding otters, a monkey, a red panda and a tortoise, children naming the weirdest foods they can think of, children learning to make paella in a New York hotel kitchen, and young reviewers talking about the review books.
