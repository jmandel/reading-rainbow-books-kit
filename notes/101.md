## About the book
Shirley Rat is the nosiest person in town, and snooping is her hobby. It keeps her busy all day. She reads other people's mail, listens in on private conversations, pokes through shopping carts and sometimes wears a disguise so nobody recognizes her. Neighbors have mostly grown used to her little ways, but not everyone is so understanding. When she lets slip to a boy's father that the boy has flunked history, the boy is grounded for a week, and he uses the time to think. Then a large, interesting package arrives at his house, and Shirley is determined to learn what is inside, whatever it takes.

The story is a comic tale of curiosity gone too far, and it suits children who enjoy a harmless busybody and a bit of mischief. Bookshare's catalog description calls the book a "wacky tale of snooping and sweet revenge" by "two masters of the ridiculous."

## Author and illustrator
Jeffrey Allen wrote the text of *Nosey Mrs. Rat*, published in 1985 with illustrations by James Marshall.

James Marshall was born on October 10, 1942, in San Antonio, Texas. He won a viola scholarship to the New England Conservatory of Music, but a hand injury ended his music career at nineteen. He later earned degrees in French and history, taught French and Spanish in Boston, and in 1970 moved to New York to illustrate books. His first was *Plink, Plink, Plink* (1971) by Byrd Baylor. In 1972 he published *George and Martha*, his first book as both author and illustrator, which began a series that ran until 1988.

He also illustrated *Miss Nelson Is Missing* (1977) by Harry Allard. His *Goldilocks and the Three Bears* was a Caldecott Honor book for 1989, and in 2007 he received the Children's Literature Legacy Award. He died in 1992. Young listeners who enjoy his humor might try the *George and Martha* books next.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar on the trail of a playful, elusive creature that loves to run, play in the snow and splash in the water. Every guess turns out wrong, and then he finds a "spying guide" with all the dos and don'ts of nosing around: *Nosey Mrs. Rat*. Without the cut opening, the creature's identity is never explained in the audio. The rest is the full reading by Faith Prince, ending on Shirley's last line, "Well, perhaps for special occasions."

Content note: near the end an audience laughs at Shirley and she goes to bed humiliated.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar reappearing in a disguise, a visit with primatologist Jane Goodall about how patient watching and curiosity helped her learn about chimpanzees, three children's reviews of other books, and a closing song about people.
