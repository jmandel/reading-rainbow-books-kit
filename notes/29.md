## About the book
*Watch the Stars Come Out* follows a little girl who crosses the ocean to America with her ten-year-old brother. Their aunt sees them onto a big boat, with a barrel of dried fruit and an old lady asked to watch over them. Below deck the girl counts the steps down to their room, and at night she is sad that she cannot see the stars. The first turn comes when the old lady falls ill, and her brother tells the girl he will take care of her.

Publishers Weekly, which recommends the book for ages 4-8, gave it a starred review, praising the "superb paintings" and the "quiet understatement that magnifies [the text's] emotional effects." Its summary describes a grandmother telling her granddaughter about her own mama's voyage "on a big boat to America."

It is a quiet picture book about being far from home, and about small habits, such as counting steps and watching for stars, that make a frightening trip bearable.

## Author and illustrator
Riki Levinson wrote the text, and E. P. Dutton published the book.

Diane Goode, who painted the pictures, was born January 14, 1949, and lives in Brooklyn, New York. According to Wikipedia, her French mother recited traditional children's stories from memory when Goode was a child, and she studied fine arts at Queens College, CUNY. After a year as a substitute teacher in New York City, she became an illustrator. Her first illustrated book was *The Little Pieces of the West Wind* (1978), and the first book she wrote herself was *I Hear a Noise*.

Wikipedia credits her with illustrating more than 60 books, among them *When I Was Young in the Mountains*, a Caldecott Honor Book, and *Thanksgiving Is Here!* (2003), whose pen-and-ink drawings the *New York Times* said "spin out like ragtime." Young listeners could look for either of those next. She works by hand rather than on a computer.

## In this episode
LeVar Burton opens on location with Ellis Island scenes: "Hi there, and welcome to New York Harbor, port of entry into America for millions of aliens." He explains that he means immigrants, not creatures from outer space, and that many were children who came without their parents. The reading then runs to the last line, "I watched the stars come out one two three." Laura Haymann reads the book.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to the Statue of Liberty while it was being repaired for its 100th birthday; children describing their first impressions of America; sour pickles on Essex Street in lower Manhattan; ethnic festivals at Liberty State Park in New Jersey; and children's reviews of the books below.

Content note: the lead-in includes a child crying and missing a parent, and the book includes an old lady who dies, a child crying on arrival and a separation from family.
