## About the book

*Show Way* follows seven generations of women in one family, each handing something on to the next girl in line. It opens with Sunni's great-grandma, sold away at seven from Virginia to a plantation in South Carolina with only some muslin, two needles and thread dyed red with berries. There Big Mama raises her, tells the children whispered stories about getting free, and teaches her to sew stars, moons and roads into cloth. Those quilts are the show ways of the title. From there the book moves forward, one girl at a time, with a gentle, storyteller's voice. It suits listeners ready for an honest but warm look at slavery and freedom seen through a single family.

Putnam published it in 2005, and it was a 2006 Newbery Honor Book. As quoted on Wikipedia, School Library Journal's Mary N. Oluonye called it "an outstanding tribute, perfectly executed in terms of text, design, and illustration," and Kirkus Reviews said it "takes a difficult subject and makes it accessible to young readers."

## Author and illustrator

Jacqueline Woodson was born in Columbus, Ohio, on February 12, 1963. She lived in Nelsonville, Ohio, and then in Greenville, South Carolina, before moving to Brooklyn around age seven. As a child she loved telling stories and knew she wanted to be a writer. She later served as Young People's Poet Laureate and National Ambassador for Young People's Literature. Younger listeners may enjoy her picture books *Each Kindness*, illustrated by E. B. Lewis, and *The Day You Begin*, illustrated by Rafael López. Woodson is the real-life great-granddaughter of Sunni, a character in this story.

Hudson Talbott was born in Louisville, Kentucky, on July 11, 1949. He studied sculpture and painting at Temple University's Tyler School of Art. He wrote and illustrated more than 27 books, among them *From Wolf to Woof!* and *A Walk in the Words*. His best-known work is *We're Back! A Dinosaur's Story*, which became an animated film in 1993. He also illustrated *Leonardo's Horse*, written by Jean Fritz. Talbott died in January 2026.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton explaining that his family is working on a family tree, "a chart of your family's history," and that this book goes back seven generations in one family that stitches its stories into quilts. That tree project is in the cut episode, so a child hears it without context. Diahann Carroll then announces the title and reads the whole book, ending on the line "There's a road, girl. There's a road."

Content note: in the reading, a girl is sold away from her mother, and later girls walk in lines to change segregation laws and are a little scared.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remark that Woodson inherited her grandmother's quilt; a family and children showing keepsakes such as a saddle blanket and a ring; a grandmother telling how she hid as a child during the Holocaust, and her grandson's book about her; a family building a photo tree; and kid book reviews.
