## About the book

*Uncle Jed's Barbershop* is told by Sarah Jean, looking back on her great-uncle Jedediah Johnson. He is the only Black barber in the county, and every Wednesday night he arrives with his clippers, talking about the shop he will open one day, with shiny sinks, four barber chairs and a red and white pole outside. Nobody believes him in the segregated South. Then Sarah Jean falls ill at the age of five, and the family needs money they do not have. The story follows his patience through hard years.

Kirkus Reviews called it "A fine, unusually engaging debut for Mitchell," and described Ransome's "realistic, powerfully constructed paintings" as showing characters "self-reliant and unbowed." Publishers Weekly, which called Mitchell a "First-time author," was more mixed. It found that she "crowds several themes" into the story and that the history lessons interrupt the narrative, though it praised the oil paintings. The book suits children who like warm family stories.

## Author and illustrator

Margaree King Mitchell wrote *Uncle Jed's Barbershop* as her first book, in 1993. Her later picture books include *Granddaddy's Gift* (1997, illustrated by Larry Johnson), about a man's fight to vote, and *When Grandmama Sings* (2012, illustrated by James Ransome), about a girl on tour with her jazz-singing grandmother.

James Ransome was born in 1961 in Rich Square, North Carolina. He studied illustration at the Pratt Institute in Brooklyn, where the illustrator Jerry Pinkney was his mentor. His first illustration job was *Aunt Flossie's Hats (and Crab Cakes Later)* by Elizabeth Fitzgerald Howard, in 1989. He has illustrated more than 60 children's books and lives in Rhinebeck, New York, with his wife, the author Lesa Cline-Ransome.

His paintings for *Uncle Jed's Barbershop* earned a Coretta Scott King Illustrator Honor. A Horn Book reviewer wrote that they "present a warm portrait of a loving family in both hard times and good." Ransome later won the Coretta Scott King Award for illustrating *The Creation* and, in 2023, the Children's Literature Legacy Award.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar at Mr. Johnson's barbershop, for his haircut every other Friday. Several children describe their barbers, and LeVar picks out a favorite book about a man who lived for the day he could open his own barber shop. The opening points at the shop ("this is where you'll find me"), which a listener cannot see.

Regina Taylor reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, "He taught me to dream, too."

Content note: the reading includes a child's serious illness, segregation, a bank failure that wipes out savings, and Uncle Jed's death.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks after the book. The singing group The Persuasions talk about a cappella singing and rehearsing in a Brooklyn subway station. Mr. Johnson says his father, his first customer, backed his dream. A girl who dreams of becoming a veterinarian learns about caring for horses from her mentor. Young reviewers recommend books.
