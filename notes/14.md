## About the book
Jimmy brings his pet boa constrictor along on his class's trip to a farm, and the chickens in the hen house do not take to the visitor. The story comes to the listener as a chatty back-and-forth: one child answers her mother's question about how the class trip went, and every answer raises another "but why?" Each reply explains the last, so the outing unravels link by link, with eggs, corn, pigs and a bus all pulled in. Kirkus described a chaotic class trip related backwards by a child reporting to her mother. Booklist, in a starred review, called it a "top-notch choice," School Library Journal wrote "Fine, funny and full of pep," and The Horn Book called Noble's story "hilarious" and "casually told." It suits listeners who enjoy silly cause-and-effect stories and farm animals, and the question-and-answer format keeps a young child guessing what comes next. The ending is best left to the reading itself.

## Author and illustrator
Trinka Hakes Noble was born in Albion, Michigan, in 1944 and grew up on a small farm in southern Michigan, one of seven children. Her family raised orphaned wild animals, and she has said animals were central to her childhood, which made a boy named Jimmy and his friendly boa feel natural to write. She earned a fine arts degree from Michigan State University in 1967, taught art, moved to New Jersey in 1972, and studied children's book illustration at Parsons School of Design and Uri Shulevitz's workshop. This book was named an ALA Notable Children's Book and a Reading Rainbow featured selection, according to her website. A sequel is *Jimmy's Boa Bounces Back*.

Steven Kellogg was born in Norwalk, Connecticut, in 1941 and studied at the Rhode Island School of Design, including a scholarship year in Italy. He has created more than 90 children's books and received the Regina Medal for lifetime contribution to children's literature. He wrote and illustrated *Pinkerton, Behave!* and *The Mysterious Tadpole*, both good next reads.

## In this episode
The audio opens with the host saying that this snake "may look scary, but actually she's pretty friendly," then asking what would happen if a pet boa went on a class trip to a place full of animals. "This snake" is Fang, a boa met at a pet store in cut material, so the listener never meets her. The reading follows, ending on the last line: "Yeah, I suppose if you're the kind of kid who likes class trips to the farm." Kaleena Kiff and Laura Hicks read it.

Content note: a crying cow, a farmer's wife screaming, and Jimmy's boa left behind.

Not in the podcast audio: a pet shop visit with a tarantula and Fang; children naming the pets they would want; young pig raisers; a brother and sister on their grandparents' farm with a song about eggs; and the Great Western Junior Livestock and Dairy Show in Pomona, California: sheep fitting, goat milking and a pig scramble.
