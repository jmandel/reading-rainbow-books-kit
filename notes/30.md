## About the book
Miles gets a saxophone for his birthday, and his mama and papa soon wish he hadn't. He practices until they can't stand it, and then they send him outside. He takes them at their word and plays as loud as can be. Strolling down Main Street, up Swamp Lane and over Bayou Bridge, he meets a drummer named Al, a guitarist named Bert and a trumpet player named Doc, and the four start the Swamp Band. The whole town soon tells them to play in the swamp, where a very different audience, with sharp teeth and long tails, has an invitation for them.

The title borrows the old song "Mama Don't Allow," and the story keeps that bouncing, sing-along feel. It suits children who love a loud band, a spooky-but-silly swamp and a story that builds toward a big night out. Kirkus called the book "high-energy, high-decibel fun for young rowdies." 

## Author and illustrator
Thacher Hurd was born in Burlington, Vermont, in 1949, the son of children's book creators Clement Hurd and Edith Thacher Hurd. Margaret Wise Brown's *My World*, from the year he was born, is dedicated to him. After attending the California College of Arts and Crafts, he turned to picture books. His first, *The Old Chair*, came out in 1978. Hurd is an artist as well as a writer, and he lives in Berkeley, California.

Besides *Mama Don't Allow* (Harper and Row, 1984), his books include *Art Dog* (1996), *Zoom City* (1998), *Tomato Soup* (1992) and *Bongo Fishing* (2011). *Art Dog* is a good next stop for a child who likes this playful style. *Zoom City* was named a New York Times Best Illustrated Book of the Year, and Wikipedia reports that it later inspired the name of the video company Zoom. In 2008 Hurd used his father's artwork from *Goodnight Moon* to make *Goodnight Moon 123*, a counting book.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in: "It is easy to see why alligators are the modern day cousins to the dinosaurs, especially when we add that monster movie music." It refers to a cut alligator visit, so a child hears it without context. He adds that in this book, music turns out to be a real lifesaver. Fred Newman reads *Mama Don't Allow*, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "And Mama said, Oh, how nice."

Content note: the alligators announce they are hungry for something tender and juicy, and the band is briefly in danger before the lullaby.

Not in the podcast audio: a naturalist talking alligators at Kliebert's Alligator Farm near New Orleans, Fred Newman showing how he made the band's sounds, the Creole Queen riverboat and its 20-ton paddle wheel, children saying what their parents won't allow, three kid book reviews, and a New Orleans jazz saxophonist playing with a trumpeter.
