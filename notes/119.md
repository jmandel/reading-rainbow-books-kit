## About the book

Owen is a young mouse with a fuzzy yellow blanket he has had since he was a baby. Fuzzy goes everywhere Owen goes, from upstairs to downstairs, through orange juice, grape juice and chocolate milk. Then the neighbor, Mrs. Tweezers, asks whether Owen isn't getting a little old to carry that thing around, and she is full of advice for his parents. The Blanket Fairy is the first idea, saying no is the second, and school is about to start. Owen's answer to all of it is simple: Fuzzy's perfect.

The book suits children who have a favorite soft thing of their own, and parents who are deciding what to do about it. Publishers Weekly, which recommended it for ages 3 and up, credited Henkes's "characteristically understated humor" and "brightly hued watercolor-and-ink pictures." Kirkus Reviews, listing it for ages 3 to 6, called it "a delectably amusing look at a true first love." The phrase "absolutely wonderful, positively perfect, especially terrific" returns in the story.

## Author and illustrator

Kevin Henkes wrote and illustrated Owen himself. He was born on November 27, 1960, in Racine, Wisconsin, one of five children, and has said that many of his stories are inspired by his family and the neighborhood where he grew up. He thought he would be an artist until a high school teacher encouraged his writing, and he found that children's books combined both interests. He wrote his first book during his freshman year at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. That book, All Alone, was published by Greenwillow Books in 1981, and Greenwillow has published all of his books since. He lives in Madison with his wife, the artist Laura Dronzek.

In 1994 Henkes was a runner-up for the Caldecott Medal for Owen, and in 2013 the book won the inaugural Phoenix Picture Book Award. Henkes later won the Caldecott Medal for Kitten's First Full Moon.

Listeners who like Owen may enjoy Henkes's other mouse stories, including *Chrysanthemum*, *Lilly's Purple Plastic Purse* and *Owen's Marshmallow Chick*, or *Birds*, which he wrote and Dronzek illustrated.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton on a playground: "Now, that's just what I needed. If there's anything I know how to do, it's have fun." The lead-in follows a cut phone call about a rescheduled meeting, so the child hears it without that context. Children then describe their comfort things, including a funny-looking toy, a stuffed duck and a blankie. LeVar introduces the book, and Matthew Broderick reads *Owen* through to its last line, "And Mrs. Tweezers doesn't say a thing."

Content note: in the reading Owen buries his face in his blanket and cries when he is told he cannot bring it to school.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's own stories about his blanket, becoming a big brother and his first day of kindergarten; a visit to a comic book company; and children reviewing other books.
