## About the book
A young narrator tells the story of her grandmother, who as a girl watched the last emperor of Vietnam weep on the day he lost his Golden Dragon throne. To remember him, she slips into the Imperial Garden and takes a single seed from a lotus pod. She hides it under the family altar, carries it in her pocket when she marries, and holds onto it through war, a crowded boat on a stormy sea, and a strange new life in America. Years later a curious little brother finds the seed and plants it, which leaves the grandmother distraught.

The telling is gentle and plainspoken, with short scenes and one object to follow, so it suits listeners who like a quiet pace. Kirkus Reviews praised the "spare, formally composed paintings" and called it "a thoughtful, beautifully designed book that will find a place in many discussions."

## Author and illustrator
Sherry Garland is a fifth-generation Texan and the youngest of nine children. She earned a French degree at the University of Texas at Arlington, worked in libraries, and switched from adult fiction to writing for children and teens in 1989. She has written more than thirty books; her website says several focus on Vietnam because of her association to Vietnamese families in Houston, and that she visited Vietnam for research. It also says *The Lotus Seed* won many awards. A next book might be her *My Father's Boat*, illustrated by Ted Rand, about Vietnamese shrimpers on the Gulf Coast.

Tatsuro Kiuchi was born in Tokyo, studied biology at International Christian University, then earned a BFA in illustration at ArtCenter College of Design in Pasadena. His site says *The Lotus Seed* was his first picture book and has sold over 200,000 copies. He later illustrated *The Eagle's Gift* by Rafe Martin and designed Royal Mail's 2006 Christmas stamps.

## In this episode
The podcast opens in a garden, with LeVar Burton saying there is always something magical about a garden, big or small. He points out a lotus, explains that a lotus pod is a carrying case for seeds, lets listeners hear the seeds inside, and says a seed can last over a hundred years and still grow. He then introduces one seed's journey from Vietnam to America. Rona Figueroa reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, about the day her grandmother saw the emperor cry.

Content note: the reading includes war, bombs, a family fleeing by boat, and a grandmother who cries.

Not in the podcast audio: a field segment in which Vietnamese American children in New Jersey talk about their old homes, starting at a new school, learning English and keeping their Vietnamese language and food.
