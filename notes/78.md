## About the book
Madame Pompenstance has wanted for fifteen years to win the Medal of Honor for the best headdress at the fancy dress ball, and every year the medal goes to someone else. This year she plans many designs, but none seems quite right. On the day of the ball she strolls down to the beach, thinking that a shell or two might do the trick, and bends over to put some in her hair. At that very moment a very small triple-masted sailing ship puts into port, and the story takes its first odd turn.

From there the tale is a string of comic mishaps, from a croquet game to a ballroom full of rival headdresses, including one with a real canary in a gold bird cage. Madame's friends whisper and sniff while she gets a terrible headache. Listeners who like silly situations and a heroine whose headdress is more than she bargained for will enjoy it. The ending is best left for the listener to discover.

## Author and illustrator
The book is by Deborah Nourse Lattimore, and the episode credits give the publisher as Harcourt Brace Jovanovich. An Internet Archive catalog record describes the story as Madame Pompenstance competing for the Best Headdress Award at the annual Fancy Dress Ball, unaware that a small ship has sailed onto her head.

Kirkus Reviews called Lattimore "an art historian who's created picture books on several cultures," and lists a good many of her books for children. *Punga: The Goddess of Ugly* (Harcourt, 1993), which she illustrated herself, is a story for ages 4 to 8 built around the Maori custom of sticking out the tongue during the haka; Kirkus calls her art, with its humorous characters, detailed carvings and lush green setting, the book's strength. *The Winged Cat* (HarperCollins, 1992) is set in ancient Egypt, and Kirkus calls its illustrations "sumptuous" while finding the story "rather muddled and sometimes arbitrary," a mixed effort for ages 7 to 10. Her other titles listed by Kirkus include *Medusa*, *Cinderhazel*, *Arabian Nights* and *The Fool and the Phoenix*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton as a news anchor in a mock "News from the neck up" report packed with head puns: a seal spokesman insists seals have necks but no elbows. The report teases other stories from the television episode, so some jokes arrive without context. He then turns to the feature story, a woman on a ship called a figurehead, with the twist of a ship on a woman. Marilyn Pasekoff reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to people making hats, a trip to a New York barbershop, a spoof science report on the human head and a spoof interview with a band called the Grateful Head, followed by three children reviewing books.

Content note: a character has a throbbing headache and feels dizzy.
