## About the book

Abbie's father sails from Matinicus Rock, a lighthouse island off the coast of Maine, to fetch medicine, food and lamp oil. The weather looks calm, so he leaves Abbie a job: keep the lights burning until he gets back. Then another storm rolls in. Based on a true story from 1856, the book follows Abbie through long nights of climbing the tower steps and scraping ice off the windows so ships can see the light.

It suits young listeners who like real-life adventure told in short, clear sentences. School Library Journal said the Roops "allow the natural drama of Abbie's story to emerge in simple sentences that are sometimes cut up awkwardly, but for the most part they are clear and compelling." The same review called the watercolor paintings "highly effective in detailing Abbie's job as well as creating atmosphere" and described the book as "one of the best historical beginning-to-reads." It judged that "the tale stands alone as an exciting account of a young girl's courage."

## Author and illustrator

Peter Roop was born in Winchester, Massachusetts, and studied at Lawrence University. He taught elementary school in Appleton, Wisconsin, for about 25 years and received the Wisconsin State Teacher of the Year award in 1986. He earned a master's degree in children's literature from Simmons College and began writing historical articles for Cobblestone and Cricket magazines. After four books, he brought in his wife as a writing partner.

Connie Roop was born in Wisconsin and also attended Lawrence University, where she first planned to become a doctor before turning to geology. She taught science, earned a master's degree in science teaching from Boston College, and later taught high school environmental science in Appleton. She began using children's books in her science classes, and a writing project with her husband became a series of six riddle and joke books.

Together the Roops have seen more than 100 fiction and nonfiction books published. For listeners who like jokes, those riddle books are a natural next step. Pictures are by Peter E. Hanson.

## In this episode

The podcast opens on a real tall ship, the schooner Heritage, with the line "Big hands or little. Everyone's job is really important on a big schooner." The narration then describes a wild downeast gale and the one sign that could save a sailor, a lighthouse, then introduces the true story of a girl who kept her lighthouse burning. The opening starts mid-scene, so the visit's earlier moments are cut.

Sada Thompson reads the book, and the audio ends on Abbie's last line, "I kept them burning."

Content note: a fierce storm, Abbie afraid with shaking hands, a huge wave hitting the door, and a family cut off from supplies.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's visit to Owls Head Light in Maine, where a Coast Guard keeper shows the lamp, lens and fog horns; a craftsman who builds ships in bottles; and children reviewing other books.
