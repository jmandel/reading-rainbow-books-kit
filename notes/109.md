## About the book

*My Shadow* is Robert Louis Stevenson's poem from *A Child's Garden of Verses* (1885), presented as a picture book with watercolors by Ted Rand. A child describes a small companion that goes in and out with him, copies him from heels to head, and has no idea how children ought to play. The shadow shoots up tall like an India rubber ball, then shrinks until there is none of him at all.

Wikipedia counts it among Stevenson's most famous poems for children. It suits toddlers who have just noticed the dark shape that follows them across the floor or the sidewalk.

Kirkus Reviews (November 1990) liked the art: "lively watercolors in Rand's familiar, popular style," and "Rand deftly gives the kids' shadows a drama that reinforces their universal fascination." The same review calls the book "cheerful and attractive," but judges that the added multicultural theme, showing children of many races and countries, "detracts from the poem's unity."

## Author and illustrator

Robert Louis Stevenson was born in Edinburgh in 1850 and died in Samoa in 1894 at age 44. He is best known for the novels *Treasure Island*, *Kidnapped* and *Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde*, and for *A Child's Garden of Verses*. That book gathers 64 poems for children, written from a child's point of view, and Stevenson dedicated it to his childhood nurse, Alison Cunningham. He lived with serious bronchial trouble for much of his life, yet wrote prolifically and traveled widely.

Ted Rand (1916-2005) was born on Mercer Island, Washington, drew from an early age, and worked as a portrait and advertising illustrator. He served in the Naval Air Corps during World War II, ran his own firm called Graphic Studios, and taught at the University of Washington for 20 years. He began illustrating children's books later in life and illustrated 78 of them. He received the Kerlan Award in May 2005, after his death. His books include the *Salty Dog* series, *If Not for the Cat* and *A Pen Pal for Max*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "There are shadows everywhere in your life if you know where to look." He then says that of all the shadows in the world, he has a favorite, one that is always with him. A title announcement follows, naming Stevenson, Ted Rand and the reader, and the reading begins.

Robert Guillaume reads the poem, and the audio ends on its last line, about the lazy little shadow fast asleep in bed.

Not in the podcast audio: a cold open about light, photons and how shadows form, with a butterfly shadow and an explanation of sundials; a visit with Sonny Fontana, who makes animal shadows with his hands; a photographer who explains cameras and darkroom printing; and kids reviewing books.

The lead-in refers to the cut cold open, so the child hears it without the sundial explanation that came before.
