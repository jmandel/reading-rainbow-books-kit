## About the book
Old Sam, whose real name is Simon Rodia, came from Italy as a young man and settled in a bungalow in Watts, a poor neighborhood of Los Angeles. He works at a tile company, and every evening he comes home with a sack of broken tiles. On weekends he gathers bottles, pottery and seashells from a vacant lot, and the neighbors keep asking what he wants with all that junk. Then something strange and beautiful begins to rise over the fence in his backyard.

The story is true, and it suits children who collect rocks, shells or odd treasures.

Booklist wrote that the book "will intrigue readers, and the artwork has a charm all its own." The *Bulletin of the Center for Children's Books* said that "the fascination of collecting and creating, of turning garbage into something splendid, shines through the book." *School Library Journal* said it "could also spark an interesting discussion on art and its role in our society." Publishers Weekly judged that the fanciful interpretation "carries the book . . . away from history."

## Author and illustrator
Patricia Zelver wrote this picture book, published by HarperCollins and later reissued in paperback. Publishers Weekly identified her as the author of *The Wedding of Don Octavio*.

Frané Lessac grew up in Cliffside Park, New Jersey, and spent weekends in New York museums and galleries. At 18 she moved to Malibu, California, to study ethnographic film. In 1979 she moved to the Caribbean island of Montserrat and began painting its old buildings and people. Her first book, *My Little Island* (1984), began as what she called a "love letter" to Montserrat. She later settled in Western Australia with her husband, the author Mark Greenwood.

Publishers Weekly said her gouache paintings capture "the gaudy beauty of his achievement." Her other picture books include *Nine O'Clock Lullaby* and *On the Same Day in March*, both by Marilyn Singer, which young listeners may enjoy next. She also illustrated *We Are Grateful: Otsaliheliga* by Traci Sorell, which won a 2019 Boston Globe-Horn Book Picture Book Honor Award.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in, and it begins mid-thought: "A community is a mosaic of different people and places." He points out the Watts Towers as one of the community's treasures, with pieces of cups and saucers, broken plates, and kitchen and bathroom tiles. The lead-in refers to "this community" and "here", places the cut footage showed, so a child hears it without that context. The audio then goes to the title card and the full reading of *The Wonderful Towers of Watts*, read by Angela Bassett, and ends on the book's last line about people coming from all over the world to marvel at Old Sam's crazy dream.

Not in the podcast audio: after the reading, LeVar talks with Watts school kids about growing up there. They name the library, the parks, the train station and church as good things, and also describe gangs, drugs and shootings, and what they would change.
