## About the book
Theodora, called Theo, arrives in the small town of Jasper, Texas, for her first summer at Aunt Mazel's. Everyone she meets has the same advice: stay away from the junkyard. The housekeeper says the old man who lives there with his pig is clearly mad, the general store owner says he swallows kids whole, and the baker warns of booby-trapped holes. Theo sets out to explore anyway, and the first turn comes when she climbs over a hill and finds a glittering mountain of discarded objects, with something moving inside it.

Publishers Weekly, reviewing the paperback edition in 1992, described "the fetchingly gawky Theodora" and listed the book for ages 5 to 8. The rumors suit children who are curious about scary-sounding places. The book also invites a second look at ordinary things: an old trumpet, a lampshade and a chandelier turn up in the junk pile and catch Theo's eye. It suits any child with a favorite thing that someone else might call junk.

## Author and illustrator
Tricia Tusa wrote and illustrated this book herself. According to her website, she was born in 1960 in "the swampy parts of Texas,". She has illustrated more than 40 children's books and both written and illustrated ten of them.

Kirkus Reviews lists several other picture books that she both wrote and illustrated, including *The Family Reunion* (reviewed 1993), *Sisters* (1995), *Follow Me* (2011) and *Is That You, Eleanor Sue?* (2018). For listeners who enjoy her artwork, she also illustrated books by other authors, among them *The Magic Hat* by Mem Fox, *The Sandwich Swap* (by Rania Al Abdullah, with Kelly DiPucchio), and *Treasure Map* by Stuart J. Murphy. Her website groups her work into drawings, printmaking, paintings, ceramics, cartoons and children's book art.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton in a garage, announcing he has a job to do: "See all this junk? But today, I'm getting rid of it." He keeps finding things he cannot part with, including his first watch and his old letterman's jacket. He concludes that "just because something's old doesn't mean it's junk," then introduces the book. The opening mentions "Gonzo" in passing, which the child hears without context.

The book is read by Michele Mariana, and the audio ends on the book's last line, with Theo beaming at the crowd.

Content note: characters in the story spread rumors that the old man swallows kids whole and that the junkyard has deep holes.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit with an artist who turns junk into a "story box" with a rodeo scene built in an old dresser drawer, LeVar looking through his old yearbook and photos, and kid reviews of the books below.
