## About the book
On a family safari in Africa, young Scotty Lazardo finds a dinosaur who reminds his mother of her Uncle Bob, so Bob he becomes. The Lazardos bring him home to Pimlico Hills, and the trouble starts when the town sees what a dinosaur gets up to in a neighborhood. Publishers Weekly called the book a "campy extravaganza" and described Bob as "gargantuan yet unthreatening." The same review praised the art, rendered in a style "reminiscent of 1920s magazine advertising," with "surprising juxtapositions of scale" and many "unorthodox aerial perspectives." Baseball is at the center of the story, including a local team that badly needs a lift.

The story is full of things to picture: a dinosaur used as a river boat, passengers dancing the conga on his back, and bedtime snacks of double Dutch chocolate cake. It suits listeners who like big, friendly creatures. Harper and Row published it, and the review lists it for ages 4 to 8.

## Author and illustrator
William Joyce wrote and illustrated the book himself. He was born on December 11, 1957, in Shreveport, Louisiana, attended Southern Methodist University, and has written and illustrated more than 50 children's books and novels. His first book, *Tammy and the Gigantic Fish*, written by Catherine and James Gray, was published by Harper and Row in 1983.

Joyce later moved into animation, working as a concept artist on *Toy Story* and *A Bug's Life*. His picture book *A Day with Wilbur Robinson* became the Disney film *Meet the Robinsons* in 2007. His short film *The Fantastic Flying Books of Mr. Morris Lessmore* won an Academy Award, and the picture book of that name reached number one on the New York Times bestseller list in July 2012.

He received a gold medal from the Society of Illustrators for *Santa Calls* and the 2008 Louisiana Writer Award. Listeners who enjoy Bob may like his picture books *George Shrinks* and *A Day with Wilbur Robinson*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar welcoming listeners to spring training with the American League champion Oakland Athletics in Scottsdale, Arizona. He says everyone loves baseball but not everybody is a major league player, and that every team seems to have a star, "like Bob." The spring training visit itself is cut.

Edward Asner reads *Dinosaur Bob and His Adventures with the Family Lazardo* in full, ending on the line about everyone singing and dancing late into the summer night.

Content note: Bob is arrested and threatened with being sent back to Africa, and he escapes with help from Dr. Lazardo and the baseball team.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar gets batting tips from A's hitting instructor Merv Rettenmund, and pitcher Dave Stewart explains his forkball and curveball. A spoof fan guide and a Brooklyn softball game between the 78th Precinct Bombers and the Bergen Beach Bengals, called by Steve Albert, follow. Three children review the books below.
