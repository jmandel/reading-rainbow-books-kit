## About the book
On a drizzly evening, a girl and her older sister walk into the park after the sister's band practice. The sister carries a flute case, and she whispers that the carousel horses are asleep, something their mother used to say. The carousel has been broken for a long time. Then the girls hear a whinny, pull back the canvas, and find the horses moving, their harnesses chiming like Christmas bells. The younger girl climbs onto her favorite silver-gray mare, and the sisters are swept into a wild night ride through the park.

The Harcourt Brace record describes it simply: two sisters find that the horses of a broken carousel have come alive in the rain. It suits listeners who like a little magic at bedtime and stories about sisters looking after each other. A mother's memory runs through it, and the storm, the galloping and the flute music give it plenty to listen to.

## Author and illustrator
Liz Rosenberg was born February 3, 1955, in Glen Cove, New York, and grew up in Syosset on Long Island. Her father owned a tool manufacturing company, which he ran with several cousins. At Bennington College her first short story, "Memory," won an Atlantic First Award and was published in *The Atlantic Monthly*. She earned a PhD in comparative literature at Binghamton University, where she has taught since 1979. She is a poet, novelist, children's book author and book reviewer.

Her other picture books include *Adelaide and the Night Train* (1989), *The Scrap Doll* (1991), *Monster Mama* (1993), *Moonbathing* (1996), *Eli and Uncle Dawn* (1997) and *Eli's Night Light* (2001).

Jim LaMarche illustrated this book. He is the author and illustrator of a retelling of *The Elves and the Shoemaker*, published by Chronicle Books in 2003. Kirkus called its art "honey-toned illustrations." In Kirkus's account, the retelling sets the Grimm tale in an old-style town with cobbled streets.

## In this episode
LeVar opens by asking, "Have you ever seen a carousel when the horses are sleeping?" He describes a carousel closed for the winter, opening its doors for one night, and introduces the story of two sisters who ride on a misty, rainy night and find a part of their lives they thought they had lost. The book is read by Sarah Hubbard, and the audio ends on the book's last line, with the family safe inside the umbrella.

Content note: the sisters speak of their mother only in the past tense, and the story includes thunder, lightning and frightened horses.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's reflection on legacies from loved ones, quilt maker Peggy Hardwell teaching her grandniece Jocelyn to quilt, a visit to a Brooklyn carousel, and artist Milo Mottola building a Harlem carousel with children's drawings.
