## About the book

After a picnic on the beach, Lolly, Sam and Spider are full of hot dogs and lemonade, and none of them wants a nap. So they decide to tell stories instead. Lolly goes first, with a very plain tale from her school reader. Sam and Spider are not impressed, and each is sure he or she can do better. The three friends take turns, and each new story has its own flavor.

Penguin's current edition is a 48-page Penguin Young Readers Level 3 title, listed for ages 6 to 8. Penguin's description sums it up this way: "Lolly's is an eerie story from her school book. Sam's is suspenseful, with an unexpected ending. But Spider's might be the scariest of them all!" The fun lies in how the friends react to one another's stories, and in the short, plain dialogue.

## Author and illustrator

The text is credited to Edward Marshall and the pictures to James Marshall, but they are the same person. James Edward Marshall (1942-1992) was born in San Antonio, Texas, and grew up on a farm near Beaumont. He studied viola and violin and won a scholarship to the New England Conservatory in Boston, but a hand injury ended his music career. He graduated from Southern Connecticut State College in 1967 and taught French and Spanish in Boston before turning to books. A bookseller's note says he liked to use his middle name, Edward, as a pseudonym for his first readers.

Marshall was self-taught as an illustrator. His first assignment was *Plink, Plink, Plink* by Byrd Baylor (1971). The next year he wrote and illustrated *George and Martha*, the first of eight books about two hippopotamus friends. In 2007 he was posthumously awarded the Wilder Medal, now called the Children's Literature Legacy Award. Listeners who like this book might enjoy his Fox books, including *Fox on Wheels* and *Fox in Love*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the host at the beach: "Boy, what a beautiful day, huh?" He has brought lunch and sunglasses but left his favorite book at home. Then he sees everyone around him reading *Three by the Sea*, buys a copy from a vendor, and starts at the beginning. The audio ends on the book's last line, "And that was that."

The reading is credited to Chicago City Limits, the improvisational comedy troupe. Content note: in Spider's monster story, the children are about to be eaten and jump ten feet, but there turns out to be no monster at all.

Not in the podcast audio: the host's closing thoughts on imagination; a message in a bottle; sand sculptures on the beach, including a triceratops and a dolphin; Chicago City Limits improvising a story with a child as a football player and a tap dancer; a song about making your own book; and three children's reviews of the books below.
