## About the book

Tanya is stuck inside with a cold on a spring day when she notices Grandma gathering scraps of cloth: plaid from Papa's work shirt, red from her brother Ted's shirt. Grandma is starting a patchwork quilt, a "masterpiece" that will take at least a year, and she explains that a quilt "can tell your life story." Tanya, who thinks a year is far too long, soon sees pieces of her own family's clothes, from Jim's blue corduroy pants to her Halloween costume, joining the pile. Then Grandma becomes unwell, and the quilt waits.

The story moves through a single year and suits listeners who like cozy family stories, grandparents, and watching something get made slowly. Kirkus Reviews wrote that Pinkney's pictures "glow softly and abound in loving smiles." The same review called the book "thoroughly platitudinous," though it still expected it to be admired. The book was named an American Library Association Notable Book and received the Christopher Award in 1985, according to a Perma-Bound teacher's guide.

## Author and illustrator

Valerie Flournoy was born on April 17, 1952, in Camden, New Jersey, and earned her teacher's certificate and B.A. from William Smith College in 1974. She has worked in publishing as an editor and editing consultant. Besides *The Patchwork Quilt*, her books include *The Best Time of Day*, *The Twins Strike Back* and *Until Summer's End*.

Jerry Pinkney (1939-2021) was born in Philadelphia, the middle of five children. He struggled in school because of dyslexia but loved drawing, and he illustrated his first picture book, *The Adventures of Spider*, in 1964. Over his career he illustrated more than 100 books, usually in watercolor. His pictures for *The Patchwork Quilt* won a Coretta Scott King Award for illustration in 1986, the first of five he received. In 2010 he won the Caldecott Medal for *The Lion & the Mouse*, an Aesop retelling. Young listeners who enjoy his art may also like *Mirandy and Brother Wind*, written by Patricia McKissack, a 1989 Caldecott Honor book that he illustrated.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with a lead-in set at the Recycle Store at the Boston Children's Museum, "the best junk shop in Boston," where barrels brim with buttons, rickrack and calico. LeVar explains that a patchwork quilt is made of small pieces of cloth stitched together, "like a jigsaw puzzle," and that it can tell a story without using a single word. Isabel Sanford then reads the book, which runs through the last line, "For Tanya from your mama and Grandma."

Content note: in the reading, Grandma falls ill and the family waits a week before the children may see her. Tanya misses her and the work stalls.

Not in the podcast audio: a classroom at the Joseph Lee School in Dorchester, Massachusetts, sews a fabric quilt with each child's own square; a visit to the Balducci family's food market in New York City; and three child reviews of the review books.
