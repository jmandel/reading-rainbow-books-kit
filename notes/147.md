## About the book
At Monique's school, every child wears a brown and blue uniform, and Monique thinks they are the ugliest colors and clothes she has ever seen. She tells her mother every day that she does not want to wear it. Then she finds an old trunk in the attic, and the first thing inside is a huge red hat. She tries it on in the mirror and decides to wear it to school the next day. Her classmates notice at once, and one says it looks like a giant tomato.

*Unique Monique* is a funny story about wanting to stand out and how fast a good idea spreads through a classroom. The trunk holds more surprises, and Monique keeps finding new ways to be herself while the grown-ups try to restore order. A library catalog describes it as a book in which Monique "hates her school uniform, so she tries to find a way to make her individuality shine through." It suits listeners who are starting to notice rules, sameness and the wish to be different.

## Author and illustrator
The author is credited in the episode as Maria Rousakis, while library catalog records spell the name Maria Rousaki. The English edition is catalogued as a 32-page book from 2003, and a separate 2008 library-bound record also exists.

Catalog records show that Rousaki writes in Greek as well. A Greek-language record from 2001 titled *He Melpō he Monadike* lists her with a second contributor, Polina Papanikolaou, and describes a story in which little Monique's schoolmaster wants her pupils to look all the same in the classroom. Other Greek-language titles under her name include a 2013 book with a title transliterated as *Dhiastimikes idhisis*, a 2018 title transliterated as *Hē nychta tēs Kassianēs*, and a 2022 title transliterated as *Paize, Max!*

## In this episode
The lead-in opens on a girls' soccer practice for the Queen Eagles, with LeVar Burton noting that the team's uniforms help players feel part of a team while each girl still has her own unique approach to the game. He then introduces a girl who wore a uniform but liked to stand out, and names the book, its author and illustrator, and the reader. The soccer scene is the on-location opening that is in the podcast audio. The audio ends on the book's last line.

Eliza Dushku reads the book.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to the Twins Days Festival in Twinsburg, Ohio, where identical twins enter contests such as most alike and least alike; a 20-year-old from Brooklyn who runs an arts program called Expressions and writes poetry, and who talks about growing up with a stutter; three kid book reviews; and a "Double Vision" song about twins.
