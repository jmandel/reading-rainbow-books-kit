## About the book
*Our Big Home: An Earth Poem* is a celebration in rhyme and refrain of everything living things hold in common. It opens with a list of neighbors, "People, ants, elephants, trees, lizards, lichen, turtles, bees", all sharing the same big home. Each spread then turns to one thing they share: water, rain, sun, soil, air, wind, the dark of night, and the plain feeling of being alive. Most sections close with a variation on "Here on Earth, our big blue home".

The refrain makes it easy for a small listener to join in, and the lines are full of things toddlers know firsthand: rain on the nose, dirt under the feet, a stretch and a hop. It suits any child who likes naming animals and plants.

School Library Journal wrote that "Glaser's joyous appreciation of the interconnectedness of all living things forms the heart of this celebratory picture book" and that "Kleven's brilliant, busy art gives it its soul." The book was a Reading Rainbow Feature Book and an NSTA/CBC Outstanding Science Trade Book, according to the illustrator's website.

## Author and illustrator
Linda Glaser grew up in New York, earned a master's degree at San Francisco State University, and taught at Vista College. Her first picture book, *Keep Your Socks On, Albert!*, came out in 1992, and she has since published more than 30 children's books, often on the environment and tolerance. *Emma's Poem* won the 2011 Jane Addams Children's Book Award for Younger Children, and *Hannah's Way* won the 2013 Sydney Taylor Book Award for Younger Readers. Nature-minded listeners might look for her Millbrook titles *Wonderful Worms*, *Beautiful Bats* and *Magnificent Monarchs*.

Elisa Kleven grew up in Los Angeles and now lives near San Francisco. She studied literature at UC Berkeley, earned a teaching credential, and after several years of teaching and reading to children began making picture books. Her mother was a printmaker and her grandmother a sculptor. Her pictures combine watercolor, colored pencil, ink, crayon and collage. She has written and illustrated about 30 books, including *The Paper Princess*, *Sun Bread* and *Welcome Home, Mouse*.

## In this episode
LeVar Burton opens in front of a mosaic at the United Nations: "Isn't this a beautiful mosaic? This is an art exhibit here at the U.N. and it's called The Amazed World." He explains that each tile was made by a different child from around the world, and that everyone shares one planet, which leads into the book.

The book is read by Naomi Judd, and the audio ends on its last line, "one precious living home."

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's tour of the United Nations grounds and its flags; a visit with the youth group Kids Can Free the Children, including a video diary of a trip to build a school addition in a remote village in Nicaragua; other ways children volunteer, from an animal shelter to collecting food for neighbors; a library visit where a young reviewer recommends the review books; and LeVar's closing remarks at the U.N.
