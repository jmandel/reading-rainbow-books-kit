## About the book

A boy begins drawing a story about Simon and a big furry beast, then gets drowsy and goes to bed, leaving Simon stranded on the page. After the boy falls asleep, the pens creep out of their jar, the ink bottle follows, and they introduce themselves to Simon. They promise to get him out of the monstrous predicament he was drawn into so thoughtlessly. The beast leaps, the pens draw escape routes across the pages, and the chase is on.

The book is a good fit for children who like silly monsters, drawing, or the idea that a picture can come alive. The chase is lively but the tone stays light: Horn Book reviewer Mary M. Burns called it "Original, fresh, and engaging" and "deliciously thrilling but never terrifying." It also suits bedtime, since the whole adventure happens while the boy sleeps.

## Author and illustrator

Henrik Drescher wrote and illustrated *Simon's Book*, so one person made both the words and the pictures. He was born in Denmark in 1955, and his family moved to the United States in 1967. He studied at the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, but left after one semester to become an illustrator. His editorial drawings have appeared in the *New York Times*, the *Washington Post*, *Newsweek*, *Time* and *Rolling Stone*. He has also led bookmaking workshops for children.

*Simon's Book* was published by Lothrop, Lee and Shepard in 1983. It was named a *New York Times* Best Illustrated Book of 1983, won a Parents' Choice award, and was a Horn Book Graphic Gallery selection. MacAdam/Cage reprinted it in 2005. Drescher's earlier picture book *The Strange Appearance of Howard Cranebill, Jr.* (1982) was also a *New York Times* Best Illustrated Book.

Other picture books by Drescher that a young listener might enjoy include *Looking for Santa Claus* (1984), *Look-alikes* (1985), *Pat the Beastie* (1993) and *Hubert the Pudge* (2006).

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in. Just before it, the TV episode showed children drawing silly monsters (cut), so the doodle LeVar calls "just like this one" is not shown. LeVar says, "That's marvelous. When you start out with a doodle and an inspiration, there's no telling where you might end up." He explains that this book started with a doodle, then introduces *Simon's Book* by Henrik Drescher, read by Ruby Dee. Her reading follows, and the audio ends on the book's last line: "And that is the same book that you've just finished reading."

Content note: the story includes a monster that chases Simon and his friends before turning out to be friendly.

Not in the podcast audio: Henrik Drescher shows LeVar the original doodle, a black-and-white dummy book and his end papers. A printing plant tour follows. Three young reviewers then talk about other books.
