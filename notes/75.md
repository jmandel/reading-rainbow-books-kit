## About the book
Fox has been showing off for the girls, and one stunt too many leaves his bike a wreck. He expects Mom to buy a new one. Instead she points out, "I'm not made of money," and tells him to find a job. His little sister Louise will not help, so off he goes to the pizza parlor, the amusement park and beyond, with friends Carmen and Dexter turning up along the way.

Kirkus Reviews called Fox "amusingly ridiculous and recognizably human" in what it counted as his sixth appearance, and said Marshall's "comic illustrations, situations, and well-phrased text are sure to keep them reading." Kirkus also found the conclusion "somewhat tame."

It suits listeners who enjoy a character who gets into trouble through his own overconfidence. Kirkus calls his jobs "predictably, disasters," and the mishaps are short and easy to follow by ear. Dial Books published it in 1988.

## Author and illustrator
James Marshall wrote and illustrated the book. He was born on October 10, 1942, in San Antonio, Texas, and grew up on his family's farm. He won a viola scholarship to the New England Conservatory in Boston, but a hand injury ended his music career at nineteen. He later earned degrees in French and history, taught high school, and moved to New York City in 1970 to work as an illustrator.

His first picture book as an illustrator was *Plink, Plink, Plink* by Byrd Baylor (1971). In 1972 he published *George and Martha*, his first book as both author and illustrator, which began a series about two hippo friends that ran to 1988. He also illustrated Harry Allard's *Miss Nelson Is Missing* and *The Stupids*. His illustrations for *Goldilocks and the Three Bears* made him a Caldecott Honor runner-up in 1989.

Other Fox books include *Fox and His Friends*, *Fox at School* and *Fox on Wheels*. Marshall died on October 13, 1992. In 2007 he was posthumously awarded the Children's Literature Legacy Award.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar saying it is his day off and a perfect day for chores, and that out in the work world, if you want a job you have to find one, which is what his friend Fox does in this book. Fred Newman reads the book.

The audio holds two stretches. The first runs from the start of the story to Fox being fired from the pizza parlor. The second begins at the haunted house and ends with Fox dreaming of his new bike. Material between them is cut, so the story jumps from the pizza parlor to the amusement park.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar at his own chores, adults who love their jobs (insurance, dog walking, firefighting, LEGO models), a 15-year-old who runs a nail polish business in the South Bronx, and kid reviews of the books below.

Content note: Fox is fired, collides with his sister, and is frightened in a haunted house.
