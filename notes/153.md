## About the book
In the spring of 1945, a Dutch girl named Katje lives in a town where people patch their clothes again and again and go without soap, milk, sugar and new shoes. One morning the postman arrives with a box from America. Inside are soap, wool socks, chocolate and a friendly letter from a girl named Rosie. Katje writes back to say thank you, and that first exchange grows into something much bigger for both towns.

The story is gentle and easy to follow, with short letters between two girls that suit a listener who likes to hear what happens next. It is a good fit for a child ready for a story about sharing, thanks and friendship across a distance.

Kirkus Reviews called the book "As heartwarming and uplifting as a bouquet of tulips." Publishers Weekly, in a starred review, described it as a "potent and merry lesson in generosity." Booklist, in a mixed review, called the story still moving, and noted that in the real-life events adults, not children, organized the effort.

## Author and illustrator
Candace Fleming was born in 1962 in Michigan City, Indiana, and earned a bachelor's degree from Eastern Illinois University in 1985. She taught at Harper College near Chicago from 1997 to 2005 and has written full time since. Her first picture book appeared in 1994, and she has since written more than 50 books for young people. Booklist reports that she based *Boxes for Katje* on her mother's experience, which she describes in an author's note. Another of her picture books is *Muncha! Muncha! Muncha!* (2002), illustrated by G. Brian Karas.

Stacey Dressen-McQueen made the pictures for this book. Kirkus praised her "detailed mixed-media paintings," and Booklist described the colored pencil, oil pastel and acrylic art as rich in color, pattern and historical detail. Her other picture books include *The Biggest Soap* by Carole Lexa Schaefer (2004) and *The Elephant Quilt* by Susan Lowell (2008).

The book was named a *Publishers Weekly* Best Book of 2003 according to Fleming's site.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar pointing to a stack of boxes and explaining that they will be filled with books for a town that lost its library and needs help restocking its shelves. He says boxes have helped people do good before, and that this story is based on true events from World War II, when many European towns were devastated by the war. He then names the book and its creators, and the reading follows. The reading is by Elizabeth Jute and runs to the closing letter that goes with a box of tulip bulbs.

Content note: the story mentions hunger, cold and shortages after a war, resolved by gifts from strangers.

Not in the podcast audio: children in New York City decorate and fill boxes for families affected by Hurricane Katrina, a middle school class holds an overnight video conference with students in Mexico, and young readers talk about the three review books.
