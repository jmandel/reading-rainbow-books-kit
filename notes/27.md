## About the book

Alistair Grittle is a sensible boy. Every day he makes a list of the things he has to do, and another of things he does not have to do. He is never late for school, and he takes such good care of library books that he washes his hands before he reads them and always returns them on time. One day, returning his books, a spaceship picks him up and whisks him off into space.

The two creatures flying it call themselves Goots, and they are friendly. They like Alistair so much that they want to take him home. Alistair politely explains that his library books are due back today and the librarian will be expecting him. The Goots pretend not to hear.

The humor comes from the gap between a huge adventure and one very small, very serious worry. It suits listeners who like a tidy character in an absurd situation, or who are fond of library visits and rocket ships. Published in 1984, it is the second book about Alistair.

## Author and illustrator

Marilyn Sadler was born on November 17, 1950, in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. She writes children's books, including the Alistair books about a boy genius whose activities include building a time machine, exploring outer space and ironing his shoelaces. Her P. J. Funnybunny series began with *It's Not Easy Being a Bunny*, and her book about a girl from space became the Disney movie *Zenon: Girl of the 21st Century*. She is also one of the creators of the Disney Junior show *Handy Manny*, and her television credits include two Reading Rainbow programs featuring Alistair.

Roger Bollen (1941 to 2015) was born in East Cleveland, Ohio, and attended Kent State University. He wrote and drew the newspaper comic strip *Animal Crackers* from 1967 to 1994, and *Catfish* from 1973 to 1986. With his second wife, Sadler, he produced more than 50 children's books. Their first, *Alistair's Elephant*, appeared in 1983.

Next, try *Alistair's Elephant* (1983), *Alistair's Time Machine* (1986) and *Alistair Underwater* (1990). Bollen illustrated *It's Not Easy Being a Bunny* (1983).

## In this episode

The audio opens on location at the Library of Congress with a welcome to Washington, D.C., "home of the White House, the Capitol, and this building." A short tour explains that books there are looked at in the building, not checked out. The introduction then turns to the book, and Arnold Stang reads *Alistair in Outer Space*. The recording ends as Alistair lands back on Earth, with the final pages cut. The opening promises a "treasure hunt" whose later stops are not included.

Content note: Alistair is carried off in a spaceship and briefly loses his way in space.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to the geography room with a moon globe and a cloth map made for World War II rubber rafts, the film library's early science fiction film about a trip to the moon, a skit about getting a first library card, how old books are repaired and rebound, and three children's reviews.
