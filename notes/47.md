## About the book
*Bugs* is an information book built on a question-and-answer rhythm. Pages open with a small riddle about an insect, then answer it with a few plain facts. What did Sam find swimming in his apple cider? A spider. Why did the porch collapse one night? Termites. Whose shrill sound woke Ada? A cicada. The answers lead into short explanations: orb weaver spiders spin round webs and wait, crab spiders pounce from a flower, dragonflies live near ponds and rivers, termites tunnel into wood and carve out rooms for a whole colony, and a male cicada sings to attract a female.

Hard words such as "camouflaged" and "pedipalps" are explained on the spot, and the narration points out what the animals on screen are doing as it goes. It suits curious listeners who like to guess the answer before it arrives.

## Author and illustrator
*Bugs* is credited to Nancy Winslow Parker and Joan Richards Wright, with illustrations by Nancy Winslow Parker. The book was published by Greenwillow, which is also the publisher of Parker's *Working Frog* (1992).

Parker both writes and illustrates her own picture books and illustrates books by other writers. Kirkus Reviews lists her own titles, including *Working Frog* (1992), *Locks, Crocs, and Skeeters* (1996) and *Land Ho!* (2001). Kirkus describes *Working Frog* as a picture book narrated by a bullfrog named Winston who is taken from a pond in Westchester County, New York, to the Bronx Zoo, and says its full-color illustrations have a droll humor and show the zoo from the animal's point of view.

Parker also illustrated several picture books written by others, among them *No Bath Tonight* by Jane Yolen (1979), *Black Crow, Black Crow* by Ginger Foglesong Guy (1991), and *When the Rooster Crowed* by Patricia Lillie (1991). Listeners who enjoy her animal pictures can look for any of these next.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton admiring a butterfly's wings: "Isn't this beautiful? Butterflies are such amazing insects." He goes looking for other insects, explains that "bugs" is a nickname and that bugs are only one kind of insect among thousands, and then introduces the book. Lori Delgado and Edwin Maldonado Jr. read it, and the audio ends with the cicada page and the closing exchange, "I really like insects." "Me too."

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar looks for insects in a fallen log and reads from a reference book about insects. He then visits Insect World at the Cincinnati Zoo, where Thane Maynard shows him a hissing cockroach, a royal goliath beetle, a Javanese walking leaf and a giant walking stick. A second segment follows the monarch butterfly migration to the mountains of Mexico with scientist Lincoln Brower. Children review the Reading Rainbow books near the end.
