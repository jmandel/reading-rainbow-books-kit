## About the book
Sophie is so shy that she arranges her shopping so she will not have to talk to anyone. Every Wednesday the bookmobile parks in front of the supermarket, and every Wednesday she almost climbs aboard, but the librarian is so tall. Her days are quiet until the building across the way is made over into a dance studio, and music begins drifting in waves to her window.

What follows is a gentle story about working up courage one small step at a time. Sophie borrows books on dancing, paints colored steps on her floor (blue for walls, red for tango, green for fox trot), and practices at home where no one can see. The text is short, with counting for a child to join in, so it suits listeners who like to move along and anyone who has ever hung back at the edge of something new.

## Author and illustrator
Petra Mathers was born on March 25, 1945, in Todtmoos, in Germany's Black Forest. She skipped university for a three-year apprenticeship in the book business, then moved to the United States with her first husband and their young son and settled in Portland, Oregon.

Her first children's book job was illustrating *How Yossi Beat the Evil Urge* by Miriam Chaikin (Harper and Row, 1983). She then wrote and illustrated *Maria Theresa* (1985), about an adventurous hen who escapes her coop on a New York City rooftop and joins a circus. That book won the Ezra Jack Keats Award. Publishers Weekly says *Sophie and Lou* (HarperCollins, 1991), together with *Theodore and Mr. Balbini* (1988) and *Victor and Christabel* (1993), featured "richly hued folk-art style images" and "sweet, offbeat characters."

Listeners who enjoy her animals may like the six books of her Lottie's World series, starring a resourceful hen named Lottie and her friend Herbie the duck, which begins with *Lottie's New Beach Towel* (1998). She published more than 40 titles and died in 2024 at age 78.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar describing a worn wooden rehearsal floor, street clothes draped over long smooth bars, and mirrors and music in a dance studio, a room that can change from a ballroom to a scene in a rock video in an instant. He says dancing can change people, too, and introduces a story about someone whose life took a wonderful turn when she learned to dance. The book is read by Jessica Walter and ends on its last line.

Not in the podcast audio: after the reading, LeVar talks with a Latin dancer about the mambo, which she says comes from Cuba, and learns the basic step; a short tour of dance around the world covers Bali, Irish step dancing and Aboriginal Australian corroboree; and a dancer who also played college football shows hip-hop moves and how ballet helps athletes. Three young reviewers then recommend three books.
