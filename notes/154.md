## About the book

Ronde Barber is a blocking back for the Vikings, his youth football team. His twin brother Tiki carries the ball, and the long touchdown runs earn Tiki the cheers. Ronde does not resent it, but he would like someone to notice the blocks that clear the way. After talking with his teammates, his coach and his mother, he starts to see how much the whole team depends on every job. Then a sore ankle keeps him on the bench with a big game coming.

Kirkus Reviews said that "kids who have played any sport in a vital but unsung capacity will identify with" the book, and that its message is that "everyone has a critical part to play on a team and everyone can revel in the accomplishments." It also praised "lots of great football talk" and Barry Root's "rich colors of amber, green and deep blue."

A family dinner scene, about who cooks and who takes out the garbage, gives young listeners an easy way to see why every job counts.

## Author and illustrator

Tiki and Ronde Barber are identical twins from Roanoke, Virginia, born five weeks early; Ronde arrived seven minutes before Tiki. Both attended the University of Virginia. Tiki became a running back for the New York Giants and retired after the 2006 season as the team's all-time rushing and reception leader. Ronde played cornerback for the Tampa Bay Buccaneers, where he was part of the team that won Super Bowl XXXVII, and he was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2023.

*Game Day* is the second of their picture books together, after *By My Brother's Side* (2004), and *Teammates* (2006) followed it. In a Buccaneers.com feature, Ronde said the events are based on true experiences, with some embellishment, and that the book is about the fact that "not everybody is the superstar on your team." Robert Burleigh helped put their story on paper.

Barry Root illustrated all three of those books. His other picture books include *Christmas Farm* by Mary Lyn Ray (2008) and *The Birthday Tree* by Paul Fleischman (2008). He also wrote and illustrated *Gumbrella* (2002).

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "Whether you're running a race, kicking a ball, or scrambling a play, sports are a great way to have fun." He explains that Tiki and Ronde Barber, twins who were then NFL players, wrote the book about how they learned what playing as a team means. James Avery reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, the team's cheer of "Hooray!"

The rest of the TV episode is not in the podcast audio. It includes two 14-year-old twin sisters, Ashley and Kristen, who swim competitively and hope for a shot at the Olympic trials, and Daniel, who walks and runs races after recovering from a brain aneurysm. Three young reviewers also discuss the review books.

Content note: the story includes a player feeling overlooked and sitting out a game with an injured ankle.
