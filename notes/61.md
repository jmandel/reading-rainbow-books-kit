## About the book
*Dive to the Coral Reefs* is a New England Aquarium book, first published by Crown in 1986. The Internet Archive's catalog record says it "describes the formation of a coral reef and the many plants and animals that live in and around these underwater communities." It opens by asking whether the reader knows that a city of fantastic shapes lies beneath the surface of the sea, built and lived in by millions of creatures. The first turn comes quickly: the aquarium sends a team of scientists and divers to explore a reef, and the reader learns that reefs are built slowly by tiny animals, each about the size of a pencil eraser. Corals look like plants but are animals. Some grow hard stony skeletons, while soft corals sway like tree branches in a breeze. Along the way come an octopus, a moray eel and a sponge big enough for a diver to stand inside. It suits young listeners curious about the sea and how its pieces fit together.

## Author and illustrator
The Crown edition credits three authors, Elizabeth Tayntor Gowell, Paul Erickson and Les Kaufman, along with the New England Aquarium. The episode captions spell the first name "Tainter"; the Internet Archive record gives Gowell.

Gowell went on to write more ocean books for young readers, including *Sea Jellies: Rainbows in the Sea* (1993), *Whales and Dolphins: What They Have in Common* (2000) and *Amazing Jellies: Jewels of the Sea* (2004), according to Internet Archive records.

Les Kaufman is an evolutionary ecologist who studies aquatic ecosystems, including coral reef biology. Wikipedia says he earned his Ph.D. at Johns Hopkins University in 1980 and worked full-time at the New England Aquarium from 1983 through 1994, ending as Chief Scientist. He is now a professor of biology at Boston University and has received a Pew Marine Conservation Fellowship and a MacArthur Fellowship. His research has taken him to reefs in the Caribbean, Indo-Pacific and Red Sea.

Paul Erickson is credited on the Crown edition alongside the other two authors.

## In this episode
The podcast audio begins in the middle of an on-location scene, with divers talking about the blue water and what it promises: good visibility on the reef. A ranger then explains that they are at an underwater park, a national marine sanctuary off the coast of Florida. The host introduces the book and says underwater photographers have brought the reefs to viewers. Listeners hear "this is the spot" without the earlier part of the scene.

Fran Brill reads the book. The audio ends on the reading's invitation to "put on scuba tanks and explore this fantastic underwater," where the kept text stops mid-phrase.

Not in the podcast audio: the host dives the reef, naming fish and corals; a reef doctor transplants coral onto a reef damaged by a freighter; and three young reviewers recommend books.

Content note: the reading briefly mentions that disasters and anchor-dragging boats can damage coral.
