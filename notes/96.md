## About the book

Long ago, before the Civil War, an old sailor called Peg Leg Joe works for plantation owners and teaches the people enslaved there a song that secretly tells the way to freedom: "Follow the Drinking Gourd." The Drinking Gourd is the group of stars that points north. When Molly learns that her husband James is about to be sold away from the family, the two remember the song, gather their small son Isaiah and two older companions, and set out that very night.

From there the book is a journey told in a few plain sentences and lines of the song: hiding by day, walking by night, hounds, hunger, and kindness along the Underground Railroad. Publishers Weekly described the text as "told in a few, poetic words" and said the story has "a clarity of language suitable for preschoolers." The same review called the illustrations "highly dramatic," with "the flat perspectives that are characteristic of primitive art." It suits children ready for a serious subject, with a song to learn along the way.

## Author and illustrator

Jeanette Winter wrote and painted this book. She was born in Chicago on October 6, 1939, the only child of Swedish immigrants, and studied painting at the University of Iowa. After moving to New York she worked at the New York Public Library. Her first book, *The Christmas Visitors* (Knopf, 1968), retold a Norwegian folktale.

*Follow the Drinking Gourd* (1988) was her first self-illustrated original story. Publishers Weekly's obituary quotes her saying it was a turning point: "I gave up line... That's when color took over." She went on to write and illustrate more than 65 books for young readers, many of them true stories about real people. Her book *Mama* won a Boston Globe-Horn Book Picture Book Honor, and *Nasreen's Secret School* won the Jane Addams Children's Book Award.

Young listeners who like this one might try her picture books *The Librarian of Basra*, *Biblioburro* or *Nanuk the Ice Bear*. Winter died in New York City in November 2025.

## In this episode

The podcast begins with a spoken introduction to slavery: "One of the worst things about slavery is that families were separated." A dramatized auction scene follows, with a mother remembering her son being taken. It goes on to describe forced labor, enslaved people's music, the wish to escape, and the Drinking Gourd stars (pointing to a gourd dipper only seen on screen), and it leads into the song. Keith David then reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, that they "had followed the Drinking Gourd."

Content note: the opening and the story include family separation, a child taken from a mother, tears, fear, hounds and slave catchers.

Not in the podcast audio: an earlier opening with shackles and an old drawing of a slave ship, and, after the book, Sweet Honey in the Rock on spirituals and code songs such as "Balm in Gilead" and "Run, Mona, Run," then three kid book reviews.
