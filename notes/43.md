## About the book
Kathy and Louise Jenkins are best friends who share a pretend world. They imagine that a stallion named Golden Silverwind lives in a stable between their houses. In their magic witch hats they can turn the neighborhood into anything. Then Louise is taken off to a mountain resort for the summer, and her neighborhood becomes, in her words, a lonely desert.

The Internet Archive's catalog record sums up the story as one in which Kathy "feels lonely and betrayed when her best friend goes away for the summer and has a wonderful time." A postcard, a new neighbor and a dog expecting puppies change the summer.

The book suits children who have missed a friend. Kathy's feelings are allowed to be messy: she is jealous of Louise's new friends and keeps her anger to herself. Steven Kellogg both wrote the story and drew the pictures, and the Parents' Choice Foundation gave *Best Friends* an award in 1986, the year it was published by Dial Books for Young Readers.

## Author and illustrator
Steven Kellogg was born in 1941 in Norwalk, Connecticut. As a boy he drew pictures for his younger sisters and told them stories to go with them. He earned a B.F.A. from the Rhode Island School of Design in 1963.

He began by illustrating books by other writers, starting with George Mendoza's *Gwot!* in 1967. His first book as both author and illustrator, *The Wicked Kings of Bloon*, followed in 1970. He married Helen Hill in 1967, and her six children became his stepchildren and inspired many of his drawings. Wikipedia credits him with more than 90 children's books, and he has received the Regina Medal for lifetime contribution to children's literature.

Pinkerton, the Great Dane of *Pinkerton, Behave!* (1979), was inspired by the Kellogg family's own dog of the same name. For more, try *A Rose for Pinkerton* or *Can I Keep Him?*, which Kellogg has called "a little bit autobiographical."

## In this episode
The podcast opens on LeVar Burton's lead-in, which begins in the middle of a puppy cuddle: "Yeah, good boy." He is helping care for a litter of seven-week-old golden retrievers, and says other friends can be special, too, as in this book. The audio then runs through the full reading, ending on the book's last line. Liza Siegler reads the book.

Content note: the story includes loneliness, jealousy and missing a friend who is away.

Not in the podcast audio: after the book, children describe their own best friends. A girl raising a guide-dog puppy, a black Lab named Abby, hands her over to a woman who is recently blind, and a dog-show segment shows how show dogs are groomed. Young reviewers then recommend *The Story of Mrs. Lovewright and Purrless Her Cat*, *A Gift for Tia Rosa* and *The Puppy Who Wanted a Boy*.
