## About the book
Larnel is a boy who stops by with his mother to visit Mrs. Katz, an elderly immigrant who has been left alone since her husband Myron died. Kirkus calls the book a "touching friendship" story about a "lonely old immigrant" and a boy who goes along with his mother "to pay a comforting visit." Larnel soon returns with a tailless kitten that nobody else wants, and Mrs. Katz agrees to take her "if you'll come and help me with her." Between baking, knitting and storytelling, the two trade memories of places where they were not welcome, and of what their families have in common. Along the way young listeners hear Yiddish words, a Passover table, and a warm picture of an unlikely friendship across ages and backgrounds. It suits children who like animal characters, family stories and quiet, affectionate pacing, and it opens good conversations about grandparents and neighbors. Kirkus praises the "energetic illustrations" and "decorative patterns and lively action," and ends: "Truly affectionate and heartwarming."

## Author and illustrator
Patricia Polacco wrote and illustrated *Mrs. Katz and Tush* herself. She was born Patricia Barber on July 11, 1944, in Lansing, Michigan, the daughter of a teacher and a salesman turned talk show host. She struggled to learn to read and hid it until a teacher recognized the problem and began to help her. *Thank You, Mr. Falker* retells that encounter. She began her first children's book at age 41, and her picture books often draw on family stories. *The Keeping Quilt* (1988) received the Sydney Taylor Book Award, and *Pink and Say* (1994) won the 1997 West Virginia Children's Book Award.

Wikipedia lists an Honor Award for *Mrs. Katz and Tush* "for its effective contribution to peace and social justice," dated November 9, 1993, from the Jane Addams Peace Association and the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom. Listeners who enjoy this one might like her *Thunder Cake*, *Chicken Sunday* or *The Keeping Quilt*, all by Polacco.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in over a visit to a neighborhood: "There's something so great about living in a neighborhood, you know all the shops and shopkeepers, and you really feel like you belong to a community." The opening is on-location banter with shopkeepers, so a child hears voices without context. He adds that neighbors may speak other languages or celebrate unfamiliar holidays, then introduces the book. Reizl Bozyk and Rick English read it, and the audio ends on the book's last line.

Content note: the story includes a husband's death, loneliness, a voice breaking with grief, a missing pet, and a funeral prayer and cemetery visit at the end.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar and a friend named Sherry, whom he calls a grandmother figure, bake challah and cook latkes and matzah ball soup, then share the meal. Children name their favorite older people. Kids review *Wilfrid Gordon McDonald Partridge*, *Abuela* and *Kwanzaa*.
