## About the book
Jack has been a fisherman since boyhood, working the same waters his father did. Lately the sea no longer sparkles, and the fish in his nets are small and sickly. One day he hauls in a seal pup among the mackerel, its fur coated in an oily film and flecked with debris. Jack washes and feeds the pup, then looks for other seals to return it to, and what he sees along the shore is troubling.

Google Books describes the book as a children's fable about water pollution and conservation. The story has a clear line a young listener can follow: a clean sea, a sick sea, and a man who has to decide what to do about it. It suits children who like animal stories and boats, and it gives a parent a natural opening to talk about litter and caring for oceans and rivers. A dream partway through changes the story's direction.

## Author and illustrator
Gerald Aschenbrenner wrote the original story, titled *Jan und das Meer*. Google Books lists him as both author and illustrator of the English edition, so the pictures are his as well as the words. Joanne Fink, born in 1954, made the English adaptation.

The adaptation was published by Silver Burdett Press of Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, in 1988. Its subjects are listed as water, seals and sea stories, and the book has illustrations on its lining papers. Google Books gives the length as 28 pages and tags the edition as a Reading Rainbow book, so a read-along with the pictures is short enough for one sitting. The Silver Burdett edition carries the ISBN 0382099850. A Demco Media edition is dated July 1990.

Fink's name appears on other Silver Burdett titles from the mid-1980s, including *William Tell* (1984), *Don Quixote* (1984), *Robinson Crusoe* (1985), *Ivanhoe* (1985) and *Little Red Riding Hood* (1986).

## In this episode
The podcast opens with the lead-in: "Every living thing needs it to survive," a reminder that plants, animals and people all depend on water, and that it is important to learn how to take care of it. The lead-in mentions "this floating laboratory" on San Francisco Bay, where marine biologists check on the water, and a brief exchange with a colleague; that boat and crew are not shown in the podcast. It then introduces the book. Leslie Uggams reads *Jack, the Seal and the Sea*, and the audio ends on the book's last line.

Content note: the story includes a sick, oil-covered seal, sick, half-dead fish and a frightening storm at sea.

Not in the podcast audio: a trawl on the bay that brings up a leopard shark, a skate and a topsmelt; footage of an Alaska oil spill and the cleanup of an otter and a bald eagle; and child reviews of the other books.
