## About the book

Milton is a bull with a wonderful pasture and an endless supply of homemade ice cream, and he should be the happiest bull in the world. But he cries, a lot, which is how he got the nickname Moo Hoo. He cries when he is sad, when he is nervous, when he is frustrated, when the music swells in his favorite song, and even when he is so happy he can't help but weep with joy. Then he cries again because he is embarrassed about crying. Tired of ruining everything with his tears, Milton tries toughening up, and then tries to cry it all out until nothing is left.

A picture book about big feelings, it is meant to reassure readers that it is fine to be exactly who they are. Kirkus called the story thin and somewhat silly, but it ended on a kind note: "Kids may learn to laugh at themselves a little—and accept themselves, too." It was published by Scholastic in April 2024.

## Author and illustrator

Audrey Perrott studied English and Children's Literature at the University of Florida. She had a career in communications and is now a full-time author and freelance copywriter who lives and writes in North Carolina. She says funny books matter as much as serious ones.

In an interview with Picture Book Builders, Perrott explained where the title came from. Her preschool daughter had trouble with the "b" sound and loved pretending to cry, saying "Moo hoo" instead of "boo hoo." Perrott's son, who is very empathetic, helped complete the idea, and Milton was born. Her favorite spread is the one where Milton cries happy tears, which draws on something her son said at age six: "You can't help but weep with joy."

Ross Burach is an author, illustrator and full-time picture book maker who lives in New York City. His publisher says he has written and illustrated over fifteen books, including *The Very Impatient Caterpillar*, *I Am Not a Chair*, *Truck Full of Ducks* and *There's a Giraffe in My Soup*. He also illustrated Perrott's *Baa Haa*, from 2025.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with the host, Mychal the librarian, saying, "I'm Mychal the librarian and this place is the library." He asks whether you have ever felt sad or angry and says leaning on friends can help with big feelings. He then introduces *Moo Hoo*, and the book is read by Adam DeVine. The audio ends on the book's last line, "The end."

Content note: the story has a lot of crying, and in one stretch Milton tries acting tough, shouting "charge" and "my shovel," and then says "I'm sorry."

Not in the podcast audio: a short game of "what can you remember?" about the story; two dancers visiting a paint splatter room to throw paint and let out feelings; the host making glittery sensory bottles with a group of children, with belly breaths to calm down; a psychologist answering kids' questions about crying; and children from West Orange Public Library reviewing books.
