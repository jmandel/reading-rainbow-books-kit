## About the book
Berlioz the bear has practiced for weeks, and tonight his orchestra is due to play at a gala ball in the village square. Then a strange buzz starts coming from his double bass. The musicians pile onto the bandwagon anyway, and off they go behind a mule, until the front wheel drops into a hole and the mule sits down and yawns. One animal after another strolls up to offer help, each sure the job is easy, while the tower clock ticks toward the start of the ball.

The boastful offers of help ("Let me do it," "This will be easy") and the growing pile of failed helpers give young listeners plenty to predict and join in on. Kirkus called the story "rather slight" but "well contrived to serve the charming illustrations," and said the animals are "wonderfully expressive of the comical side of human emotions." It closed by praising the book's "wealth of visual pleasures," so a library copy is worth finding for the pictures.

## Author and illustrator
Jan Brett wrote and illustrated *Berlioz the Bear*, published in 1991. She was born on December 1, 1949, in Norwell, Massachusetts, and still lives in Massachusetts. She studied at Colby Junior College, now Colby-Sawyer College, then transferred to the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. Her detailed artwork is painted in gouache, and Wikipedia notes that it takes her an hour to paint a single inch.

A musical household sits behind this book: her husband, Joseph Hearne, is a bassist with the Boston Symphony Orchestra, and her own site says that together with him she visits other countries, researching the architecture and costumes in her work and using the traditions she studies as a starting point for her books. Wikipedia lists *Berlioz the Bear* among books chosen for Newsweek's and The New Yorker's best children's book lists.

Listeners who like this one may enjoy more of her picture books, including *The Mitten*, *The Hat* and *Gingerbread Baby*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton saying, "Hi. What a jumble of noises. The city is so alive." He describes the sounds of the city, introduces a street band that makes the sidewalk its stage, and then leads into the story: a group of musicians who try to take their show on the road but get stuck in a rut. The book is read by James Avery and runs to the line where Berlioz dedicates his encore "to the buzzing bee."

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's closing remarks about where city musicians choose to play, then field segments with a singer performing in a train station, a look at the voice as an instrument, the Boys Choir of Harlem rehearsing and talking about singing, and young reviewers talking about three music books. A street musician taking requests ("New York, New York") and LeVar's closing words about music in the city streets follow the reviews.
