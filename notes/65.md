## About the book

*Sports Pages* is a book of poems about what goes on inside an athlete during a game. The voices belong to players at the moment of play: a soccer sweeper who calls himself a brown tornado on a muddy day, a basketball player at the free-throw line who bounces the ball once and takes a deep breath. The poems are about thoughts and feelings as much as action, including wanting to win, determination and playing alongside a team. Lippincott published it in April 1986.

Kirkus Reviews called the poems "accessible but uneven," said some read like prose rearranged as verse, and found most of them "staccato, hard-hitting evocations" of familiar sports. The same review praised Kuzma's "kinetic, faceless figures," which it said capture "the feeling of action at all costs," and it pictured adolescent readers. A young listener who likes to move or watch ball games may enjoy hearing what a player might be thinking. Encyclopedia.com records a 1986 Child Study Association of America Children's Books of the Year citation for the book.

## Author and illustrator

Arnold Adoff (1935-2021) grew up in the South Bronx, the son of Jewish immigrants. He studied at Columbia University's School of Pharmacy, then transferred to City College of New York, where he earned a B.A. in history and literature, and he taught social studies in Harlem. In 1960 he married the writer Virginia Hamilton, and the couple settled in Yellow Springs, Ohio. His website says he has published more than thirty books for young people and tries to turn sights and sounds into words.

Encyclopedia.com says Adoff regarded *Sports Pages* as a breakthrough, because it combined poetic prose and poetry and drew on autobiographical material, and it quotes the Bulletin of the Center for Children's Books calling it "one of his best collections." He won the National Council of Teachers of English Award for Excellence in Poetry for Children in 1988. Younger listeners might like his picture books *Black Is Brown Is Tan* (1973) and *Flamboyan* (1988).

Steve Kuzma illustrated *Sports Pages*; Encyclopedia.com spells the first name "Steven."

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in, which begins "Hi there. My last set of 10." He explains that when your body works hard you can hit a wall, and that "not just thoughts, feelings, too" go through your mind. He then introduces *Sports Pages*. The opening line refers to a workout from the cut part of the show, so a child hears it without context.

LeVar Burton reads the book. The audio includes two poems, the soccer sweeper and the free throw, and ends on "You get your point, your score, your wish."

Not in the podcast audio: clips of cycling, ice hockey goaltending, an obstacle course, horse racing and swimming; a gymnast talking about bars, floor exercise and the balance beam; a brother-and-sister pair of figure skaters on practice, the fear of dropping a partner, and a hometown fundraiser that helped them pursue the Olympic team; and kids reviewing other books.
