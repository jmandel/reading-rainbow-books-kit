## About the book
A little boy tells the story of how he came to his new parents, speaking to his mama as the two of them remember it together. Before he arrives, he knows them only from pictures: a papa and mama, a red dog, a white house with a green tree out front, and a bed with a teddy bear quilt. Then comes the long trip by plane, through a day and a night, with the pictures held tight in his hand. The first turn is the arrival, when the house is full of strange things and dark corners.

The text is quiet and keeps returning to moon, stars and night skies. It suits adopted children, families thinking about adoption, and any child nervous about a new place. Kirkus found the illustrations "full of affection" and the text "quiet, gracefully cadenced." Publishers Weekly praised "newcomer Hale's gentle watercolors and Turner's lyrical, thoughtful text."

## Author and illustrator
Ann Turner was born Ann Warren in Northampton, Massachusetts, in 1945. She studied at Bates College and the University of Massachusetts, taught high school English in Great Barrington for a year, and has written full-time since 1970. Her first book, *Vultures* (1976), was illustrated by her mother. She has written nonfiction, historical fiction, novels, poetry and picture books. According to Wikipedia, Turner won first prize from the National Council for the Social Studies in 1991 for this book.

Turner's other picture books include *Heron Street* (1989), *Stars for Sarah* (1991), *Rainflowers* (1992), *Apple Valley Year* (1993) and *A Moon for Seasons* (1994).

James Graham Hale is the illustrator. Kirkus called him a "new illustrator" when it reviewed the book in February 1990, and Publishers Weekly used the word "newcomer." The book was published by Harper and Row in 1990 as a Charlotte Zolotow Book, and Publishers Weekly lists it as suited to ages 4 to 8.

## In this episode
The podcast audio opens at family skate day in Prospect Park, where kids and parents say what annoys them and what they love about their families. The host then introduces the book as a story of "parents who wanted a child" finding "a child who needed parents." Andrew Lei reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line.

Content note: the boy is afraid on the plane and in the unfamiliar house, and the story involves separation from his old life.

Not in the podcast audio: the host explains adoption, and the Harris family retells how Andrew and Gavin joined them. The Peek family, with nine adopted children, talks about birth parents. The Abney family welcomes baby Tess. Young viewers review three books.
