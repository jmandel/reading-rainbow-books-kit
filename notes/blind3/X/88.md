## About the book
*Seashore Surprises* is an outdoor science book, a nature guide rather than a story. It explains how waves, sand and pebbles form at the seashore, and it describes the animals, plants and shells that can be found when the tide goes out. Catalog records also note that it includes experiments and activities. The book has 32 pages of color illustrations and was published in 1991 by Julian Messner.

Because it is a reference book, it is meant to be carried along and dipped into rather than read straight through. It suits curious children who like to pick things up, look closely and ask what made them. A parent can use it before a beach trip to learn the names of shells and what lives inside them, or afterward to work out what was found. TeachingBooks lists it for grades PK-6 and files it under science, English language arts and math, so it can grow with a child.

## Author and illustrator
Rose Wyler earned a B.A. from Barnard and a master's degree from Teachers College at Columbia, and she wrote more than 50 books for children on science and other topics. Her titles include *A Child's First Book of Nature Adventures* (1953), *My Little Golden Book about the Sky* (1956) and *Exploring Space* (1958). She wrote *Planet Earth* (1963) with Gerald Ames.

Steven James Petruccio was born in Brooklyn in 1961, the son of an art director who often did retouching work at home. Self-taught as a child, he studied anatomy through the drawings of George Bridgman and Burne Hogarth, and Hogarth later wrote a letter of recommendation that helped him attend the School of Visual Arts in New York. Before he graduated he was getting commercial assignments, first for magazine articles and then for picture books. He has illustrated more than sixty picture books, and his honors include a Parent's Choice Award and a Ben Franklin Award. He teaches studio art at Marist College. Another title of his for young nature fans is *Learning About Cats* (Dover, 1997).

## In this episode
This episode has no book reading. LeVar Burton carries *Seashore Surprises* on a beach walk on an island off the southwest coast of Florida and quotes from it, and that walk is the podcast audio. It opens with the lead-in "There's something so magical about seashore," and it ends with the line about the wind, the sun and the sea sculpting what the waves leave behind.

Along the way LeVar looks at coral, a live shell with a trap door, jingle shells, a lightning whelk egg case full of tiny shells, and trees left hanging where waves have carried the sand away.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit with a naturalist to the island's mangrove trees, a seine-net haul of fish and sea stars in the underwater grass beds with a marine scientist, and children's reviews of other seashore books.
