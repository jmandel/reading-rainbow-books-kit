## About the book

*Snowy Day: Stories and Poems* is a collection of short fiction, poetry, tales and activities that share one theme: snow. Caroline Feller Bauer edited it, and Margot Tomes illustrated it. The contents include work by Isaac Bashevis Singer, Aileen Fisher, Eve Merriam and Ogden Nash, and it includes a bibliography of snow books. Publishers Weekly opens its review by addressing "Anyone who has ever made an angel in the snow or reveled in winter." The review is mixed on the poems: "Although some poems are better than others, this is nonetheless a strong collection." It is more enthusiastic about the art, saying that Tomes's paintings, "varied and beautifully designed, etch black shadows on white pages with graceful charm." Publishers Weekly gives the age range as 7 to 10.

## Author and illustrator

Caroline Feller Bauer was a librarian and educator. She worked as a children's librarian at the New York Public Library and taught library science at the University of Oregon. Her books include *This Way to Books* (HW Wilson, 1983), *The Poetry Break* (HW Wilson, 1994) and *Handbook for Storytellers* (ALA, 1995). American Libraries reports that she died in Miami at 77, and that she had set up a play park, library and arts center for children in Bhattiary, Bangladesh.

Margot Tomes was born in Yonkers, New York, in 1917. After studying at Pratt Institute she designed wallpaper and fabric before turning to picture books. She worked on children's books full time from 1963. The New York Times named two of her books among its Best Illustrated Children's Books of the Year: *Jack and the Wonder Beans* (1977) and *If There Were Dreams to Sell* (1984). She died of pancreatic cancer in June 1991, and the Kerlan Award was given to her posthumously in 1995.
## In this episode

The podcast begins with the line "Hi, here we are in Jackson Hole, Wyoming, where the temperature is below freezing almost all winter." It then moves through a snowshoe walk, a poem by Ogden Nash read by Lena Horne, a poem by Carla Kuskin read by Lacey Chabert, and Robert Frost's "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening" read by Richard Courtney. The audio ends on the poem's last line, "And miles to go before I sleep."

Cut from the podcast, and not in the audio: a sledding trip to Gut Flop Hill, a segment on Susan Butcher and the Iditarod sled dog race, a snowman-building segment, two book reviews (*Over the River and Through the Wood* and *Winter*), a reviewer's introduction to Frost's poem, and the closing remarks about cocoa.
