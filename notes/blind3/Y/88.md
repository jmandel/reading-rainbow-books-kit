## About the book

*Seashore Surprises* is a nonfiction outdoor science book, published by Julian Messner in 1991 and listed for readers from preschool through grade 6. It explains how waves, sand and pebbles shape the seashore, then describes the animals, plants and shells that can be seen when the tide goes out. Experiments and activities are included. Because it is a guide rather than a story, there is no plot to follow. A child who likes to examine things, turn them over and ask what they are will get the most from it. Many of its subjects can be found on the sand and in the tide line, so it works well as a companion on a beach visit. The episode treats it that way: LeVar carries it along on a walk and checks its descriptions against what he finds.

## Author and illustrator

Rose Wyler earned a B.A. from Barnard College and a master's degree from Teachers College, Columbia University. She wrote more than 50 children's books on science and other topics. Her titles include *Exploring Space* (1958), *Planet Earth* (1963) and *Earth, Sea and Sky* (1968). A young listener who enjoys this book could try *My Little Golden Book about the Sky* (1956), another of her books for children.

Steven James Petruccio was born in 1961 in Brooklyn, New York. He taught himself to draw, studied anatomy through the work of George Bridgman and Burne Hogarth, and then attended the School of Visual Arts in New York to study illustration. His picture book work has earned the Parent's Choice Award, the Ben Franklin Award and a School Library Journal selection. One of his books was featured on the television show *Reading Rainbow*.

## In this episode

The audio opens on LeVar's sing-song title card, which is cut. The podcast begins with his lead-in: "There's something so magical about seashore." LeVar Burton, who says he likes to "bring along a reference book with any information I might need," carries the book on a walk along the shore, reading from it and pointing out the wave line, shells, a jingle shell, a sea snail's egg case and lightning whelks, and the way waves reshape the beach. It ends on the book's last line, "The wind, the sun, and the sea."

Not in the podcast audio: a mangrove walk with naturalist Lisa Satchel, a seagrass net-sampling trip with marine scientist Christy Seaman, and three children reviewing other books.

Content note: the walk mentions predators such as birds and crabs, and empty shells whose animals are gone.
