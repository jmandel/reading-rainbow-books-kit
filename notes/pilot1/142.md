## About the book

*The Tin Forest* (2001) is written by Helen Ward and illustrated by Wayne Anderson. An old man lives in a small house in the middle of a windswept place "near nowhere and close to forgotten," surrounded by things nobody wanted. Every day he sorts and buries the junk, and every night he dreams of a forest full of toucans, tree frogs and tigers. One day he has an idea, and he starts building a forest of his own out of tin. What happens after that is the heart of the book.

The words are short and rhythmic, and the story is about hope and patience without spelling out a lesson. Kirkus suggested it for ages 6 to 8 and noted how the pictures move from "dingy blues" in the junkyard to "brighter hues" for living things, with each leaf and vine drawn with care. Younger listeners can follow it too, and the color change is worth pointing out if you have the book in hand.

## Author and illustrator

**Helen Ward** trained at Brighton School of Art, where her teachers included the children's illustrator Raymond Briggs, and she lives in Stroud, Gloucestershire. *The Tin Forest* won the National Art Library Award, and in 2003 her *The Cockerel and the Fox* was shortlisted for the Kate Greenaway Medal.

**Wayne Anderson** is an illustrator from Leicestershire, England, who works in watercolor, ink and colored pencil. After college he tried to find work as a graphic designer, until an interviewer noticed how much illustration was in his portfolio and pointed him that way. He has also painted greeting cards, posters and record covers. His own book *Ratsmagic* won a Gold Medal from the Society of Illustrators. If your listener likes this one, try *The Dragon Machine*, another book by Ward and Anderson.

## In this episode

LeVar Burton's short lead-in calls this "a book that's all about the message of hope." He describes the old man's dark and gloomy home and compares it to how a New York neighborhood felt "right after the tragedy," without saying more. Jeff Bridges then introduces himself and reads the book.

The full TV episode, which is not in the podcast audio, was filmed near Ground Zero in the months after September 11, 2001. LeVar visited PS 234, a school whose students had to leave that morning and spent months in another building. The children talk about that day, show some of the thousands of gifts sent from around the world, and make a music video called "Back Where We Belong" to say thank you.
