## About the book

*Through Moon and Stars and Night Skies* (Harper & Row, 1990) is written by Ann Turner with pictures by James Graham Hale. A small boy asks his mama to let him tell the story of how he came to her. Before he met his new parents, they were only photographs in his hand: a mama and papa, a red dog, a white house with a green tree. He flew for a day and a night to reach them, too frightened to sleep, and his new home was full of strange things and dark corners. Little by little he learns his mama's smile and his papa's eyes.

It is a quiet, comforting bedtime-style story, and it fits any child who has wondered how families begin, not only children who were adopted. *Publishers Weekly* recommended it for ages 4 to 8, praising Turner's "lyrical, thoughtful text" and Hale's "gentle watercolors."

## Author and illustrator

**Ann Turner** (Ann Warren Turner) was born on December 10, 1945, in Northampton, Massachusetts. Her father was a printer and her mother was an artist, who illustrated Ann's first book, *Vultures*. She started out teaching high school English in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, and went on to write poetry, picture books and novels for young readers. Another picture book of hers is *Heron Street* (1989), illustrated by Lisa Desimini.

**James Graham Hale** painted the watercolors for this book. *Publishers Weekly* called him a newcomer when it reviewed the book in 1990. We could not find a reliable biography of him.

## In this episode

The lead-in is LeVar Burton at a family skate day in Prospect Park. Parents and kids say what drives them crazy about each other (one mom calls her kids every name but the right one) and what they love, and LeVar points out that families are made in many ways. He introduces this story as one where "parents who wanted a child found a child who needed parents." Andrew Lei reads the book.

The full TV episode, not in the podcast audio, went on to visit three families built through adoption: the Harris family, whose two boys were adopted as babies; the Peek family, a mother and her nine adopted children; and the Abney family, whose newest member is a baby girl named Tess. It ended with a song about how "a family is where you find love."
