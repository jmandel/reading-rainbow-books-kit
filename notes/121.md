## About the book

A girl who lives with her mother and brothers longs to see her father, who moves around a lot and whose jobs keep changing. When the children spend a summer on their grandparents' farm, he turns up one evening on the path, and ordinary things become special: brushing teeth at the outdoor pump, going barefoot, catching lightning bugs in jars. Then the visit ends, and she holds on to what he tells her.

It suits young listeners who have a parent living elsewhere, and any child who has waited for someone to arrive. The language is plain and sensory, with crickets, blackberry cobbler and a big orange moon. Kirkus called it a "melancholy look at a child's love for a parent and pain at separation," and said "Newcomer Colón's muted, stylized prints beautifully enhance the bittersweet tale." Knopf published it in 1995.

## Author and illustrator

Sharon Dennis Wyeth grew up in Washington, D.C. She studied at Harvard, earned an M.F.A. at Hunter College, worked as a family counselor in New York City, and is a visiting associate professor at Hollins University. She belongs to the Cave Canem fellowship of African American poets.

Her other books for children include *The World of Daughter McGuire* (1994), *Once on This River* (1998), *A Piece of Heaven* (2001) and *The Granddaughter Necklace* (2013, illustrated by Bagram Ibatoulline). Her picture book *Evette: The River and Me*, inspired by her childhood swimming in the Anacostia River, was the basis for the first biracial American Girl doll in the World By Us collection.

Kirkus called Raúl Colón a "newcomer" in 1995. He has since illustrated dozens of children's books, including *My Mama Had a Dancing Heart* by Libba Moore Gray (1995), *Tomás and the Library Lady* by Pat Mora (1997) and *Doña Flor* by Pat Mora (2005). He also writes his own, such as *Draw!* (2014) and *Imagine!* (2018).

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar in a park on Family Day, when families in the neighborhood get together. He remembers picnicking there with his mom, his sisters and cousins, and says the one person missing was his dad, because his parents separated when he was about eight. He then introduces the book.

Always My Dad is read by BJ Crosby and runs to the book's last line, "He's always my dad." The opening refers to the neighborhood, which the audio does not show.

Content note: the lead-in and the story deal with parents separating, a father leaving again, and a child trying not to cry.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's memory of his mother being both mom and dad, and of a scout father-and-son camp out; a visit with a single father and his children at home; a Big Brothers matchup between a boy and an adult volunteer; and three children reviewing books.
