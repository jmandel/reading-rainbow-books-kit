## About the book

A girl and her mother and grandmother keep a big glass jar, and every coin they can spare goes into it. The girl helps out at the Blue Tile Diner, where her mother works as a waitress, and each evening the tips are emptied out and counted together. The jar is for one thing, a wonderful, soft, comfortable armchair, because the family lost all its furniture in a house fire.

It suits young listeners who like counting, small everyday jobs and working toward something together. Kirkus describes the pictures as "vividly colored" and "proto-primitive," and the Boston Globe's obituary of Williams says her illustrations, "known for bold colors and a style reminiscent of folk art," were praised by reviewers.

Kirkus Reviews called it a "tender knockout" in 1982. The book also won the 1983 Boston Globe-Horn Book Award in the picture book category and was named a 1983 Caldecott Honor Book.

## Author and illustrator

Vera B. Williams wrote and illustrated the book. She was born Vera Baker in 1927 and grew up partly in the Bronx, studying at the High School of Music and Art and then at Black Mountain College in North Carolina, earning a degree in graphic art. She taught at alternative schools before turning to children's books in the 1970s, and illustrated a first book while living on a houseboat in Vancouver.

Williams said the story grew from her own childhood in a family that had trouble making a living. Her mother worked very hard and bought herself a chair to rest in after tiring days, which the young Williams did not understand at the time. The book was written in memory of her mother.

Listeners who enjoy this one can follow the same family in *Something Special for Me* (1983), where the girl's saving is for an accordion, or look for *A Chair for Always* (2009). Another Williams picture book, *"More More More" Said the Baby*, was a Caldecott Honor Book in 1991.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with a lead-in in the style of a diner menu: "look what the rainbow special is on today's menu," a book about a terrific team, a family that works together as well as ice cream and cake. It then names the book and its author and moves straight into the story.

Amy Linker reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, with the mother turning out the light as her daughter falls asleep in her lap.

Content note: the reading includes a house fire, with fear for the grandmother until she is found safe, and the loss of the family's belongings.

Not in the podcast audio: a cold open of rehearsals for the Reading Rainbow song and dance "Teamwork" with a choreographer, a segment on firefighters training as a team at the New York Fire Academy, and children describing books about families.
