## About the book

Max is born into a family of superheroes. His parents, Captain Lightning and Madam Thunderbolt, are legendary catchers of thieves and bullies, and the whole family expects him to be flying soon. They bounce him, coax him and wait, but Max just floats gently back to Earth. At school he is an ordinary boy in a cape and mask, and his classmates ask why he doesn't fly like the rest of the family.

That makes it a warm pick for any small child who is waiting to grow into something everyone else seems to expect. The house is the color of the sun and shaped like a lightning bolt, and Phantom the dog is never far away.

Publishers Weekly (reviewed July 31, 2000) called it a "sweetly silly picture book about discovering one's specialness," praised its "chipper ink-and-watercolor artwork," and wrote that "themes of patience, acceptance and self-confidence get a lift in this humorous tale with a satisfying resolution." It recommended the book for ages 4 to 7.

## Author and illustrator

Bob Graham wrote and illustrated Max. He was born in Sydney, Australia, in 1942, loved drawing as a boy, and was greatly influenced by comics such as The Phantom and Mandrake. He studied drawing and painting, then worked as an illustrator and designer. From 1983 to 1995 he lived in Melbourne, illustrating for a publishing house, The Five Mile Press. He now lives in the UK.

Max won the 2000 Smarties Prize in the 0 to 5 years category. Graham's "Let's Get a Pup!" Said Kate won the 2002 Boston Globe-Horn Book Award for picture book, and Jethro Byrd, Fairy Child won the 2002 Kate Greenaway Medal. He gave the prize money, 5,000 pounds, to refugees. Australia nominated him for the 2012 Hans Christian Andersen Medal.

Listeners who enjoy this one could try his *How to Heal a Broken Wing*, about a city boy who rescues an injured bird. It is his most widely held book in libraries, according to WorldCat.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's lead-in: "courage, selflessness. These are the characteristics of men and women who put the needs of others before their own." He adds that we all have the potential to be heroes, and introduces Max as the story of a young superhero who learns what it means to truly be a hero, and names Bob Graham as the writer and illustrator. The lead-in begins mid-thought; the words before it were cut.

Regina King and Ian Desdone read the book. The audio ends on Max asking, "Can we go through the jetstream?" and his parent's answer, "Whenever you're ready, Max."

Not in the podcast audio: interviews in which firefighters and others name their heroes, including remarks about September 11th; a visit to the Wildlife Rescue Center of the Hamptons on Long Island, where volunteers rescue a goose caught in a plastic six-pack ring; kids reviewing three books; and a closing song about everyday heroes.
