## About the book

*Summer* is a nature book for the youngest listeners, built around one idea: summer is when young animals grow. It opens with an invitation to run outside and play in the warm sun, where the grass grows tall and sunflowers fill the fields. Baby bears grow fat and round and learn to catch their first fish dinner down by the riverbank. Baby mountain sheep learn the safest path to the summer meadows, and baby birds flap their wings to beg for juicy spiders, beetles and ants.

The text is short and plain, a sentence or two for each animal, so it suits toddlers and preschoolers who like animal babies. Along the way listeners meet warblers, hummingbirds, a coyote in its sleeker summer coat, a mother moose and her daughter, caribou and a snowy owl. The words are paired with color photographs by Thomas D. Mangelsen, which a listener does not see in the podcast.

## Author and illustrator

Ron Hirschi was born in 1948 in Bremerton, Washington, and studied at the University of Washington, where he did graduate research in wildlife ecology. He worked as a biologist for the Washington Game Department from 1976 to 1981 and began writing books in 1985. His *Seya's Song* received the Washington Governor's Writer Award in 1992.

Thomas D. Mangelsen, who took the photographs, was born in 1946 in Grand Island, Nebraska, and studied biology at Doane College. He took up photography at 21, opened his first gallery in Jackson, Wyoming, in 1978, and won the BBC Wildlife Photographer of the Year award in 1994 and 2000.

*Summer* was published by Cobblehill in 1991 as one of four Wildlife Seasons books by the pair, alongside *Winter*, *Spring* and *Fall*. Listeners who enjoy it may want to look for those next.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with a playful radio weather report: "Now for the sizzling W-H-O-T weather," promising another scorcher and suggesting lemonade in the shade. The voice then says how much it loves summer and the world of growing animals outside, and names the book, its author and photographer, and its reader. Ossie Davis reads *Summer*, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "Another winter will soon be here." Nothing in the audio is sad or frightening.

Not in the podcast audio: the rest of the TV episode, which goes through the other seasons.
- A fall weather report, a vegetable jack-o'-lantern named Seymour, and a visit to the Beaton family's cranberry bogs in Wareham, Massachusetts, where the berries are flooded and harvested.
- Winter in Florida, Alaska, Arizona and Vermont.
- Spring baby animals, including a caribou calf, a bison calf, mountain goats and pelican, coot and grebe chicks.
- Children's reviews of the three review books.
