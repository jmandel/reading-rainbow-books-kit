## About the book

Imogene wakes up on Thursday morning to find she has grown antlers. Her mother faints, the doctor can find nothing wrong, the school principal has no advice, and her brother Norman consults the encyclopedia and announces a theory. Imogene herself takes it calmly, and the cook and the kitchen maid find uses for the new arrivals. Then the family decides the antlers must be hidden, and an emergency call goes out to a hat maker. The publisher promises that "the family (and the reader) are in for a surprise" at breakfast.

The book suits listeners of about three to seven, and it is made for reading aloud. Booklist called it "A laugh-aloud read-aloud" and praised its "effervescent, softly colored illustrations." School Library Journal, in a starred review, wrote that "Small maximizes the inherent humor of the absurd situation. Hilarious, with a subtle lesson in acceptance." Publishers Weekly called it "A dazzling attraction." The publisher's description sets the household's attempts to fix the problem against Imogene's easygoing acceptance.

## Author and illustrator

David Small wrote and illustrated *Imogene's Antlers*, which Crown Publishers brought out in 1985. He grew up in Detroit, earned a bachelor of fine arts at Wayne State University and a master of fine arts at Yale. His drawings have appeared in the *New Yorker* and the *New York Times*, and he is married to the author Sarah Stewart.

His website says the book has sold more than 550,000 copies. The publisher lists a 1985 Parents' Choice Award for it, and Small returned to the character in 2020 with *Imogene Comes Back!*

Small won the 2001 Caldecott Medal for illustrating *So You Want to Be President?* His other picture books include *The Gardener*, written by Sarah Stewart, and *One Cool Friend*. Both were Caldecott Honor books. His graphic memoir *Stitches* was a 2009 National Book Award finalist.

## In this episode

The podcast opens mid-scene with LeVar at the zoo: "Can I try these on?" he asks, and then he wonders aloud what it would be like to wear antlers like these all the time. Those antlers are in cut zoo footage, so a child hears the question without context. The book, by David Small, is introduced and the reading begins.

Imogene Coca reads the book, and the audio ends as Imogene comes down to breakfast on Friday and her family is overjoyed to see her back to normal. LeVar's remarks afterward are not in the podcast.

Not in the podcast audio: the rest of the TV episode, filmed at the Philadelphia Zoo. A zoo expert explains how antlers differ from horns, and the show visits the Philadelphia Mummers' Quaker City String Band for its sequined undersea costumes and a sardine dance. Kids review three books.
