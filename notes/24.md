## About the book

Tortoise is friendly and quiet and does everything slowly. Hare is flashy and rude and does everything quickly, and he cannot resist teasing his neighbor. When Tortoise eats breakfast, Hare jokes that it will be dinner time by the last bite. Then one afternoon Hare boasts that he could win a race hopping backwards on one paw, and Tortoise's friends talk him into accepting the challenge.

This is Janet Stevens's retelling of the Aesop fable, published by Holiday House in 1984. Her version gives the old story a lively animal community: Rooster reads out the rules and describes the six-mile course, and Raccoon, Frog and other friends help Tortoise get ready. Children who like cheering crowds will enjoy it.

Gale's biography quotes Booklist's Denise M. Wilms on Stevens's illustrating in general, not this book alone: "strong, showing a sense of movement, composition, and drama." It adds that some reviewers felt Aesop's stories cannot be improved, yet this version was showcased on Reading Rainbow.

## Author and illustrator

Janet Stevens was born in Dallas, Texas, on January 17, 1953, the youngest of three children. Her father was a naval officer, so the family moved often, and she graduated from high school in Hawaii. She earned a B.F.A. from the University of Colorado in 1975 and now lives in Colorado. She worked in advertising and textile design before picture books. At a 1978 workshop, Tomie dePaola encouraged her to try children's book illustration, and his Holiday House editor offered her a contract.

She dressed her early characters in her own clothes and shoes, and they needed stories. She often works with her sister, Susan Stevens Crummel.

The Tortoise and the Hare was one of her fairy-tale and fable retellings, with *The Princess and the Pea* (1982), *Goldilocks and the Three Bears* (1986) and *The Three Billy Goats Gruff* (1987). Her *Tops and Bottoms* (1995) was a Caldecott Honor Book in 1996. Young listeners who enjoy this one might also try *Tops and Bottoms* or *Cook-a-Doodle-Doo!*, which she illustrated and wrote with her sister.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar's exercise lead-in: "Okay everybody, listen up. This is our last sprint for tomorrow's Rainbow Mini Classic." LeVar, pedaling in training, gets a couple of pointers from another voice, and then the fable is handed over as "one of my favorite fables," with LeVar saying he feels like the puffing tortoise. The Rainbow Mini Classic is a bike race that was cut from this audio, so a child hears the mention without context.

Gilda Radner reads the book, from "Once upon a time, there was a tortoise and a hare" through the final line, "Hard work and perseverance bring reward." There is nothing in the audio that needs a content warning.

Not in the podcast audio: children telling about times they tried hard, a song about trying, a karate class at a dojo, a bike-repair scene, three young reviewers of the review books, and the Rainbow Mini Classic race itself.
