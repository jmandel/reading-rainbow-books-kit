## About the book

Little Gopher is smaller than the other children in his tribe on the plains. He cannot keep up with the boys who ride and shoot their bows, but he has a gift of his own: from an early age he decorates stones with the juices of berries. A wise shaman tells him his path will differ from the others', and a dream vision sets him a task: find a pure white buckskin and, one day, paint a picture as pure as the colors of the evening sky.

It suits young listeners who like gentle, patient tales about finding your own way. Tomie dePaola retold and illustrated it, and Kirkus Reviews called the retelling "pleasantly cadenced" and said his "somber tones burst forth into satisfyingly brilliant sunsets." Kirkus added that an author's note traces the story to a collection of newspaper articles by Ruth D. Isely, which "doesn't really give much clue to its Native American source," and that the tale says more about an artist's need for self-expression than about Plains Indians.

## Author and illustrator

Tomie dePaola (1934-2020) was born in Meriden, Connecticut. He graduated from the Pratt Institute in 1956 with a Bachelor of Fine Arts degree and taught art at several colleges before retiring from full-time teaching in 1978 to write and illustrate books. He went on to create more than 260 children's books, and his work has been translated into over 20 languages.

The first book he wrote and illustrated was *The Wonderful Dragon of Timlin*, in 1966. His best-known work, *Strega Nona*, appeared in 1975 and was a Caldecott Honor book in 1976. In 2011 he received the Children's Literature Legacy Award for his lifetime contribution to American children's literature.

For this book, a full-page author's note traces the story to *Texas Wildflowers, Stories and Legends*, a collection of newspaper articles by Ruth D. Isely, according to Kirkus.

Listeners who enjoy his work might look next for *Strega Nona*, *The Art Lesson* and *Nana Upstairs & Nana Downstairs*, all by dePaola.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar asking, "Did you ever see something so beautiful that you wanted to hold onto it forever?" He introduces Little Gopher, a boy who listened carefully to the earth until she showed him the way. The book is then read in full by Harold Littlebird, and the audio ends on the line calling Little Gopher "He who brought the sunset to the earth."

Not in the podcast audio, the TV episode also includes:

- a Native American painter at sunset, who talks about remembering the colors of the sky and where paint comes from
- Pueblo potters in New Mexico, who dig and mix clay and shape pots by hand
- a Taos Pueblo dancer, who explains his outfit, face paint and eagle dance
- three young reviewers, who recommend books
