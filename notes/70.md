## About the book

*Sunken Treasure* is a nonfiction picture book that tells a true story as a plain, steady narrative. It opens in 1622, when the Spanish treasure ship *Atocha*, loaded with gold, jewels, silver bars and thousands of coins, sails toward Florida as a hurricane gathers. A huge wave throws the ship against a reef, and it sinks. Spain sends search ships, but the wreck is not found, and hundreds of years go by.

The story turns in the early 1960s, when a man named Mel Fisher reads about the lost ship and decides to look for it. From there the book follows the search step by step: the boats and equipment, the divers, Fisher's invention called mailboxes, the clues in old Spanish shipping records, and the careful work that comes with any find.

It suits curious listeners who like to know how things are done, and it explains each step in short, clear sentences. A listing for a later edition describes it as the "many-years-long search for the treasure that went down with the Atocha."

## Author and illustrator

Gail Gibbons wrote and illustrated the book herself. She was born in Oak Park, Illinois, in 1944, and made her own small books as a child, sometimes binding them with yarn. She studied graphic design at the University of Illinois, then moved to New York City, where she worked on artwork for television, including artwork for a children's show. Children on that show asked whether she had thought of writing books for them, and her first book, *Willy and His Wheel Wagon*, was bought by a publisher soon after she proposed it.

She turned to nonfiction because she enjoys research, and she has written and illustrated more than 170 books. In 1987 she received the Washington Post/Children's Book Guild Award for her contribution to nonfiction children's literature.

Young listeners who enjoy this book may like other Gibbons titles on water, light and underground places, among them *Beacons of Light: Lighthouses*, *Exploring the Deep, Dark Sea*, *Caves and Caverns* and *Tunnels*.

## In this episode

The podcast begins with the host on a beach at dawn: "we're up at the crack of dawn to spend a day at the beach, but we're not going swimming or sunbathing. We're going treasure hunting." He shows a treasure map signed by a pirate named Dog Tooth Louie, then introduces the book about a treasure hunter who spent twenty years searching the ocean floor. The pirate hunt is not resolved in the audio, since that part was cut.

Robert Morse reads *Sunken Treasure*, and the audio ends on the book's last line.

Content note: the reading includes a ship wrecked in a hurricane, and the line "All but five aboard drown."

Not in the podcast audio: salvage footage of divers at the wreck site, a *Titanic* segment with an underwater explorer, a compass and metal detector on the beach, three children's book reviews, and a bloodhound named Baskerville.
