## About the book

Maxwell runs a food shop, and business is sometimes good and sometimes bad. Each week, though, he arrives to find his shelves emptied overnight and his cash register stuffed with money, and all he can say is "Must be magic." His cat, Casey, and his dog, Sedgwick, are the ones he scolds for sleeping all day. The first turn comes the moment he locks up and leaves: the two animals leap up, the kitchen starts sizzling, and the shop becomes a restaurant for a parade of animal guests with names like Cutlet, Professor Tuscanini and Dr. Quack.

It suits young listeners who enjoy a secret the grown-up never learns, with silly menus, grumbling guests and a race against the dawn. Publishers Weekly, reviewing the paperback in 1986, called it "unalloyed buffoonery, illustrated by imaginative full-color pictures." Kirkus Reviews was more reserved, calling the rewards moderate and pointing to the animals' shared secret joke and the wisecracking mod cartoons.

## Author and illustrator

John Stadler wrote and illustrated *Animal Cafe* himself; the title card calls it "Story and pictures by John Stadler." He is an author-illustrator who has written nearly thirty children's books. His publisher biographies name *Hooray for Snail* and *Cat at Bat* as bestsellers, and one listing adds that many of his titles have been ALA Editor's Choice and Reading Rainbow selections and that his work has appeared among the New York Times best books for children.

Stadler lives in New Hampshire with his wife, Nomi. On his Penguin Random House author page he writes that, in his role as author-illustrator, he has visited "what seems like zillions of schools." He also says many of his stories are autobiographical, and describes *Wilson and Miss Lovely* as a true story "if by true you mean mostly made up."

A young listener who likes this book might look next for *The Adventures of Snail at School*, which a bookseller biography calls his bestselling book, or for *The Ballad of Wilbur and the Moose*.

## In this episode

The podcast opens in a New York all-night diner, where LeVar is greeted at the counter: "Hi LeVar, there's one open right here." He orders a chicken salad sandwich and explains that the place stays open all night, then says he knows another little night spot, in the book *Animal Cafe*. The customers there, he says, are "a different breed entirely."

Martin Short reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, "And that they did." The diner opening belongs to a cut New York-at-night theme, so the child hears it without that context.

Not in the podcast audio: a late-night radio host dedicating songs to night workers at Fulton Fish Market, a flower market and bakeries, a tour of Bracken Cave in Texas with bat researcher Merlin Tuttle, and kids reviewing books.
