## About the book

*The Life Cycle of the Honeybee* opens on a sunny day, when a queen honeybee leaves her hive and male bees called drones follow her. From there the book moves through a hive's year in short, plain sentences: the queen lays one egg in each cell, the eggs hatch into larvae, worker bees feed them, and about ten days later a young bee comes out of its cell.

The book then follows the workers through their jobs. They make wax and build cells, beat their wings to cool a hive that has grown too hot, guard the door against robber bees, and fly out to find flowers. Back home, a bee tells the others where the flowers are, and the others feel her move. It suits toddlers and preschoolers who like to know how things work, and anyone curious about where honey comes from. There is no plot, only a hive going about its work. A resource listing describes the book as telling "in simple terms the life cycle of the honeybee."

## Author and illustrator

Paula Z. Hogan wrote nature books for young readers. A bookseller's author page lists her titles, among them *The Honeybee*, *The Butterfly*, *The Whale*, *The Dandelion*, *The Beaver*, *The Penguin* and *The Oak Tree*. A listener who likes this one could look for her butterfly, frog or dandelion books, which share the honeybee book's one-subject titles.

Geri K. Strigenz is the illustrator credited on the honeybee book. The same bookseller page names her as the illustrator on editions of Hogan's *The Butterfly*, *The Frog*, *The Penguin* and *The Gorilla*, and TeachingBooks lists the honeybee book on both women's pages.

The book carries a 1979 copyright from Heinemann/Raintree, according to a homeschool resource listing, which also marks it as out of print.

## In this episode

The podcast begins with LeVar's lead-in, which opens on location: "If these directions are right, we should be at a factory where a million experts are hard at work." The field looks like a sleepy field until he sees that the workers are bees, and he introduces the book as their sweet story. It leans on pictures the child cannot see.

Barbara Feldon reads the book, beginning with "One sunny day, a queen honeybee leaves her hive," and the audio ends on the book's last line, "all over again."

Content note: the reading says that robber bees and workers fight and that both bees die after stinging once.

Not in the podcast audio: beekeeper Tony opening a hive to show the queen, larvae and a new bee emerging; beekeeper Dave extracting honey from combs; a "Busy Busy Bees" song; children's reviews; and Tony installing a mail-order package of bees in a new hive.
