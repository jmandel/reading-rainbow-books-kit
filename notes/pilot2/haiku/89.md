## About the book

*Through Moon and Stars and Night Skies* opens with the child's own voice: "Once I was a picture you held in your hand." The boy recalls the photographs his new family sent, and then the long flight he took to reach them. He keeps their picture in his hand the whole way, and he is frightened by the plane, the crowded room at the airport, and the strange dark corners of his new home. The story is told entirely from his side. *Publishers Weekly* lists it for ages 4 to 8 and calls it a book with "a quiet charm, thanks to newcomer Hale's gentle watercolors," praising the "lyrical, thoughtful text" of Ann Turner.

## Author and illustrator

Ann Turner (Ann Warren Turner) was born in 1945 in Northampton, Massachusetts. Her other books include the nonfiction title *Vultures*, the novel *The Girl Who Chased Away Sorrow* (1999) and the memoir *Learning to Swim* (2001). *Through Moon and Stars and Night Skies* was published by Harper & Row in 1990.

James Graham Hale illustrated the book with watercolors. In its review, *Publishers Weekly* called him a "newcomer."

## In this episode

The lead-in is set at a family skate day in Prospect Park. Parents and children tease each other, one mother calls her children by each other's names, and the same people then say what they love about their families. The host closes it by saying, "Families are created in many different ways," and that this story tells how one began "when parents who wanted a child found a child who needed parents." The book's title is announced just before the reading begins. The reading runs about five minutes, from the title to the line "where your arms were always held out to me."

The book is read by Andrew Lei.

These parts are not in the podcast audio:
- Interviews with three adoptive families, the Harris family, the Peek family with nine adopted children, and the Abney family, about how they adopted and how they talk with their children about birth parents.
- Segments in which people and children introduce *Horace*, *Fathers, Mothers, Sisters, Brothers* and *Free to Be... a Family*.

Content warning for the audio: the child in the book is scared on a long flight and cries out when he is put down in a new room.
