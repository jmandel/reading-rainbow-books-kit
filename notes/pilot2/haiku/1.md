## About the book

*Tight Times* (1979) opens with a small boy asking his mother why he can't have a dog. She says "Not now," so he asks his father, who answers "Because of tight times" and says the boy is too little to understand. The boy is sure he is not too little, and the story keeps returning to the gap between what a child wants and what a family can afford.

It suits children who have just heard a "no" they did not like and want to see someone else hear the same answer. The narration stays in the boy's voice and never explains the money trouble in grown-up terms, so the reader works it out alongside him.

Kirkus Reviews was less warm. Its reviewer wrote that the pencil drawings "partake of the drabness of the times." What stands out is the point of view: a child reporting, in his own words, what changes at home when money is short.

## Author and illustrator

**Barbara Shook Hazen** was born February 4, 1930, in Dayton, Ohio. She earned a B.A. from Smith College in 1951 and an M.A. from Columbia University in 1952. She worked at *Ladies' Home Journal* and then as a children's book editor at Western Publishing before becoming a freelance writer. Her book *Even If I Did Something Awful* won a Christopher Award, and *The Knight Who Was Afraid of the Dark* won a Children's Choice Award in 1990.

**Trina Schart Hyman** (1939-2004) was born in Philadelphia. She won the Caldecott Medal in 1985 for *Saint George and the Dragon*, retold by Margaret Hodges, and received three Caldecott Honors: for her own *Little Red Riding Hood*, for *A Child's Calendar* with text by John Updike, and for *Hershel and the Hanukkah Goblins* by Eric A. Kimmel. From 1973 to 1979 she was the first art director of *Cricket* magazine.

## In this episode

LeVar's lead-in is in the podcast audio, from "Some kids just can't have pets" through "This is a book called 'Tight Times.'" He says that wanting something badly and not getting it still hurts, "no matter what the reason is for the no." Patrick McCaffrey reads the book.

Cut from the episode and not in the podcast audio: a pet show where kids bring a turtle named Tim, a seagull named Sweet Pea, an imaginary hamster and pet rocks; a bet between LeVar and Hillary that turns into rag basketball, double Dutch, sock puppets and tug of war; a trip to the library; and LeVar's closing thoughts after the book.

Content note for the podcast audio: the father loses his job, both parents cry, and the boy finds a very thin kitten in a trash can.
