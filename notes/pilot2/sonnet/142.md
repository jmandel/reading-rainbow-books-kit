## About the book

An old man lives in a small house with small windows, in a wide windswept place "near nowhere and close to forgotten" that is filled with things no one wanted. Each day he sifts, sorts, burns and buries the garbage. Each night he dreams of a forest full of tropical trees, toucans, tree frogs and tigers. Each morning the world outside is the same. Then something catches his eye, and an idea takes root.

The story is short, with spare text and a quiet, fable-like pace, which suits listeners who like to watch one slow change unfold. It is a book about patience and about making something from what is at hand. It was published by Dutton in 2001.

Kirkus calls it a parable and praises the illustrator's close detail and a shift from dingy blues to brighter colors as the story turns. Publishers Weekly describes the illustrations as exquisitely detailed and the message, that one person who dares to dream can make a difference, as inspiring in its understatement.

## Author and illustrator

Helen Ward trained at Brighton School of Art, where her teachers included Raymond Briggs. She was the first recipient of the Walker Prize for Children's Illustration, in 1985, her final year there. She lives in Stroud, Gloucestershire. *The Tin Forest* won the National Art Library Award. Her other picture books include *The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse* (2011), *Old Shell, New Shell* and *Spots in a Box*. Her *The Cockerel and the Fox* was shortlisted for the Kate Greenaway Award in 2003. Kirkus also lists her *Little Moon Dog* and *Varmints*.

Wayne Anderson is the illustrator. No detailed biography turned up in the sources consulted. Bookroo lists his earlier picture books *Invasion of the Giant Bugs* (1996), written by A.J. Wood, and *The Squirrel Wife* (1972), written by Philippa Pearce.

## In this episode

In the podcast, LeVar Burton introduces the book as one "all about the message of hope." It is about a man who lives in a dark and gloomy place, who has hope in his heart and discovers that his hope changes his world. Jeff Bridges then reads *The Tin Forest* aloud.

Not in the podcast audio: the TV episode was filmed near ground zero in New York City, after September 11, 2001. LeVar visits the students of PS 234, who spent months at a borrowed school after the attacks and then returned home. He talks with them about that day and about the gifts that came from around the world. He helps them make a mural and photograph local helpers for a thank-you music video. The students recommend three books.
