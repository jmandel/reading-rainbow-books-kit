## About the book

A small boy wants a dog. He asks his mother, then his father, and gets the same answer: not now, because of "tight times." His father explains what that means one example at a time: cereal comes in a big bag instead of little boxes, there was a sprinkler last summer instead of the lake, and Sunday dinner is soupy lima beans instead of roast beef. Because of Mommy's job, a sitter named Mrs. Macintosh picks him up after school. Then one afternoon his father comes home in the middle of the day, and the boy learns what has really been going on.

The story is told in the boy's own voice, with plenty of dry humor. It suits listeners old enough to ask "why can't I?" and parents who want a gentle way to talk about money worries and disappointment. Kirkus Reviews, in 1979, found the mood gloomy and was not won over. Booklist praised its "sweet, sad poignancy," and Zena Sutherland of the *Bulletin of the Center for Children's Books* called the narration "convincingly that of a child."

## Author and illustrator

Barbara Shook Hazen was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1930, and studied at Smith College and Columbia University. She worked as poetry editor at *Ladies' Home Journal* and as a children's book editor at Western Publishing before becoming a freelance writer in 1960. Her first book was *Animal Alphabet from A to Z* (1958). *Even If I Did Something Awful* won a Christopher Award in 1981, and *The Knight Who Was Afraid of the Dark* won a Children's Choice Award in 1990.

Trina Schart Hyman (1939-2004) was born in Philadelphia and later settled in Lyme, New Hampshire. She illustrated this book in pen and ink. She won the 1985 Caldecott Medal for *Saint George and the Dragon*, retold by Margaret Hodges, and Caldecott Honors for *Little Red Riding Hood* and *Hershel and the Hanukkah Goblins*.

## In this episode

LeVar's lead-in (in the podcast audio): "Some kids just can't have pets." He says everyone knows the hurt of wanting something and being told no, and introduces the book as the story of a boy who wants things he can't have. Patrick McCaffrey reads the book. After the reading, LeVar says that sometimes even moms and dads cry when something important goes wrong. That closing comment is in the podcast audio.

Content note for the podcast audio: the boy's father has lost his job, and both parents cry in one scene before a family hug.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar at a children's pet show (a turtle named Tim, a seagull named Sweet Pea), a girl named Hillary and LeVar betting on free games made from rags, bottles, and yogurt lids, a rhyming song about the public library, and three kids reviewing books.
