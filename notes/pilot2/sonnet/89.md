## About the book

A boy tells the story of how he came to his new parents, and he insists on telling it himself: "Let me tell the story this time, mama." Before he ever met them, he carried their pictures in his hand: a new papa and mama, a red dog, a white house with a green tree out front, a bed with a teddy bear quilt. Then comes the long trip. He flies through a day and a night, awake and afraid of the dark, the roaring plane, and all the new things, holding on to those pictures. The first turn comes when he arrives and finds that the real house is full of strange things and dark corners.

The book is a gentle, reassuring match for adoptive families, and for any child who has felt nervous about a new place. The pace is slow, the narration is in a child's own voice, and the pictures are soft watercolors. Publishers Weekly (March 1990) called it "a quiet charm," crediting "newcomer Hale's gentle watercolors" and Turner's "lyrical, thoughtful text," and listed it for ages 4 to 8. It is a good fit for bedtime.

## Author and illustrator

Ann Turner (born Ann Warren Turner in 1945 in Northampton, Massachusetts) studied at Bates College and the University of Massachusetts, then taught high school English in Great Barrington, Massachusetts. She has written picture books, historical fiction, novels, nonfiction and poetry. The Publishers Weekly review of this book identifies her earlier title *Dakota Dugout*, a picture book that a young listener could try next. Wikipedia also notes that she has taught writing at Antioch University and the University of Massachusetts.

James Graham Hale illustrated this book for Harper & Row in 1990. Publishers Weekly called him a "newcomer" at the time. No biography of him turned up in the sources checked, so nothing more is claimed here.

## In this episode

LeVar opens at family skate day in Prospect Park. Skaters and their families talk about the habits that annoy them and about what they love: bike rides, a brother who is there when needed, a dad who is a best friend. LeVar's point is that "families are created in many different ways." He then says this story tells how one began when parents who wanted a child found a child who needed parents. The book is read by Andrew Lei.
