## About the book
Sophie's Aunt Lena lives for Broadway. Every Wednesday she heads off to the matinee, leaving a trail of lavender-smelling perfume behind her, and every evening she sweeps into the kitchen to hum the music and act out the romance between the handsome hero and the beautiful heroine. Then one Wednesday, in the middle of a number, she slips on a piece of cooked cabbage and lands with a thump. A sprained ankle means rest, and rest means no matinee.

Sophie gets an idea, but she needs help from a household of busy grown-ups, and not everyone is quick to say yes. The story is full of family warmth, kitchen-table comedy and the sound of old show tunes, so it suits children who like to sing, dress up and twirl, and the grown-ups who read to them. Google Books describes it as a story in which Sophie persuades the family to stage a show for her musical-loving aunt.

## Author and illustrator
The book is credited to Eileen Kurtis-Kleinman in the Google Books record. The Reading Rainbow credits spell the name Eileen Curtis Kleinman. Hyperion Books for Children published the book, with a listed date of May 5, 1997, and Disney-Hyperion issued a later edition listed in 1999.

Diane Greenseid is the illustrator. Google Books and a Book Review Digest entry list her as Greenseid. A Harcourt School Publishers anthology says she grew up in Los Angeles, lives there today, and likes to garden, ride bikes and read. She has illustrated other picture books, including *Waynetta and the Cornstalk: A Texas Fairy Tale* by Helen Ketteman, published by Albert Whitman and Company in 2012, and *Barn Storm* by Charles and Debra Ghigna, part of Random House's Step into Reading series.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with the host setting off down old Broadway on a matinee day in New York City, singing show tunes and heading to the theater to choose a show. The host explains that Broadway is famous for being the best of the best, which is why Aunt Lena goes every Wednesday afternoon in this book. The walk through Broadway was filmed on location, so a child hears the description without the scenes.

Lucie Arnaz reads the book, and the audio ends on the line "Now, here's where the magic begins."

Not in the podcast audio: the rest of the TV episode, including the reviews by young readers and the segments after the reading.

Content note: a sprained ankle and an aunt who is sad to miss her matinee.
