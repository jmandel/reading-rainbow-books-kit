## About the book

*Worksong* is a short rhyming poem about the many kinds of work that fill a day. Hammers flash in the light, trucks make one more nighttime run, and sidewalks, buildings, food and shoes all arrive because someone made them. The lines move quickly from one scene to the next, so a toddler can listen for sounds and objects, while an older child can start to notice how many people it takes to put a day together.

Harcourt published it in 1997 as a 32-page picture book for ages 4 to 8, with oil paintings. Kirkus called it "a refreshing slant for a culture mired in the worship of celebrity," and noted that the text "doesn't always name the job or worker" but refers to an aspect of it, leaving the pictures to show a kitchen, an office or a nurse with a new mother. Publishers Weekly described it as a "gentle rhyming hymn to the dignity of work" and praised the textured oil paintings. It suits families who want a calm read-aloud about grown-ups' jobs.

## Author and illustrator

Gary Paulsen was born in Minneapolis on May 17, 1939, and died at his home in Tularosa, New Mexico, on October 13, 2021, aged 82. He wrote more than 200 books, primarily for teenagers and best known for coming-of-age stories about the wilderness. Wikipedia calls *Hatchet* (1987) probably his best-known novel, and lists *Dogsong* (1985) and *The Winter Room* (1989) among his other well-known works. In 1983 he entered the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race and placed 41st of 54 finishers.

Ruth Wright is an illustrator of children's books. She and Paulsen met in Taos, New Mexico, and married in 1971. She is credited as Ruth Wright Paulsen on *Worksong*, which she illustrated in oils.

The couple also made *Dogteam* together, a picture book published in 1993 that Kirkus calls a celebration of their longtime avocation of training sled dogs; Gary Paulsen is the author and Ruth Wright Paulsen the illustrator. It follows one moonlit run, from harnessing the dogs to the return home.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in: "Everyone knows that we need to work in order to make money to support ourselves." He goes on to say that work also lets people share their talents and contribute to the world, then introduces the book and its creators. David Canary reads *Worksong* in full, and the audio ends on the book's last line.

Not in the podcast audio, the rest of the TV episode shows firefighters, a boiler-room technician fixing a heating system, an obstetrician giving a pregnant mother a checkup with a heartbeat listen and an ultrasound, and children saying what they want to be. It also visits a tiger-cub caretaker, a George Washington Bridge inspector, an African drum maker, a diner cook, a cartoonist, a fireworks-making family and a train engineer. Three young reviewers then present the books below.
