## About the book
The market is closed, so a cook sets out to gather the ingredients for an apple pie the long way around. The trip begins with a suitcase and walking shoes, then goes on to Italian wheat, a French chicken, cinnamon from Sri Lanka, an English cow and sugar cane from Jamaica, with the narrator speaking straight to the listener: bring the chicken, be quiet if a leopard is napping nearby.

It suits listeners who like journeys, lists and food. Publishers Weekly called Marjorie Priceman "a master of whimsy" and said her watercolors "radiate an offbeat nostalgia." Kirkus Reviews found the illustrations "lovely and lively" and the narrative moving at "a spritely pace," though it also judged the trip neither logical enough to be informative nor silly enough to be satisfying, and said the recipe appended at the end will take some adult help. Its verdict was "Still, fun."

## Author and illustrator
Marjorie Priceman wrote and illustrated this book, which Knopf published in May 1994. Born in 1958, she has written or illustrated more than 30 children's picture books, and her work is known for bright watercolors, free-flowing lines and a whimsical spirit. She lives in Lewisburg, Pennsylvania.

She has twice been named a Caldecott Honor illustrator, for *Zin! Zin! Zin! A Violin*, written by Lloyd Moss, and for *Hot Air*, her own account of the first hot-air balloon ride. Hot Air carries the subtitle "The (Mostly) True Story of the First Hot-Air Balloon Ride."

Listeners who enjoy this book may like other titles she both wrote and illustrated, including *Emeline at the Circus* and *My Nine Lives by Clio*. She later returned to the same idea with *How to Make a Cherry Pie and See the U.S.A.* Kirkus lists many more books she illustrated for other authors, among them *Jazz Age Josephine* by Jonah Winter and *Who Says Women Can't Be Doctors?* by Tanya Lee Stone.

## In this episode
The audio opens with LeVar Burton in a kitchen, reading a muffin recipe and hunting for the apples. He says that great recipes sometimes call for going "a bit beyond your kitchen pantry" to find the best ingredients, and that this is what happened to the cook in the book. The lead-in refers to the cooking scene that came before it, which is not in the podcast.

Helen Mirren reads the book, from the market being closed to the last line about eating the pie plain.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar helps a guest chef make a vegetable marinara sauce, with tricks for chopping onions without tears and peeling tomatoes, then a rice-and-spinach lasagna. The chef talks about learning to read at age 26. A food scientist at an ice cream company shows how new flavors are tested. Three children review *The Edible Pyramid*, *The Kids Around the World Cookbook* and *What Food Is This?*
