## About the book
Mohawk families have long taught their children to begin each day by giving thanks. This picture book offers that Thanksgiving Address, a "good morning message" that treats the natural world as a gift and every part of the universe as one large family. The thanks move from the waters and grasses to the foods and healing herbs, the animals and trees, the birds, winds, thunder, sun, moon and stars, and finally the Great Spirit.

The repeating "thank you" suits waking up, mealtimes or bedtime, and a first conversation about being grateful. Kirkus Reviews said that Chief Jake Swamp "has made the [Thanksgiving] address accessible to all ages without hindering its easy reverence," and called the book "A simple, tender celebration of good fortune," recommending it for ages 3 to 8. Publishers Weekly said the "flat planes and saturated colors" of Printup's paintings "form a handsome backdrop." Lee and Low published it in 1995, with the Mohawk-language text at the end.

## Author and illustrator
Chief Jake Swamp (Tekaronianeken) was born on the Akwesasne Mohawk Reservation in upstate New York and lived in Hogansburg, where he worked as a cultural adviser for the Mohawk Council of Akwesasne's Child and Family Services. He founded the Tree of Peace Society, which has been planting trees in public places since 1984, including near Philadelphia's Constitution Hall in 1986. He delivered the Thanksgiving Address around the world, including at the United Nations, and died in October 2010. *Giving Thanks* was his first picture book.

Erwin Printup Jr. is a Cayuga and Tuscarora painter, born in Niagara Falls, who earned a fine arts degree from the Institute of American Indian Art in Santa Fe, New Mexico. He lives in Lewiston, New York. This was his first picture book as well, and it is also available in a Spanish-language edition, *Gracias te damos*.

Lee and Low lists the book as a Reading Rainbow selection, a CCBC Choices title and one of Bank Street College of Education's Best Children's Books of the Year.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton saying how much he loves autumn: bright trees, crisp air, favorite fruits and vegetables ripe and ready. Looking at all that comes from the earth makes him want to stop and say thanks, and he explains that the Iroquois begin each morning with a message of respect called giving thanks. He names the book and its creators, and Nicholson Earl Billey reads it. The audio ends on the book's last line, about being happy and free every day and every night.

Not in the podcast audio, the TV episode continues with:
- a cranberry harvest on a family farm in Massachusetts
- three generations of one family making clay pots at Santa Clara Pueblo
- children planting a tree and flowers on a New York City street
- a song about caring for the earth
- kid reviews of the books below
