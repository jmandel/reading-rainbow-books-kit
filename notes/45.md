## About the book

The narrator and a best friend, Luca, fly to the small Caribbean island where the narrator was born. From the air it looks like a giant green turtle swimming in the sea, and the brightly painted wooden houses on the hills look like little rainbows. Over a few days the two friends meet cousins, donkeys, goats and iguanas, choose fruit at a busy market, watch fishing boats come in, snorkel, listen to steel drums, and stay up late for carnival.

The pages work like a travel scrapbook, suiting young listeners who enjoy naming fruits, flowers, animals and boats.

Kirkus Reviews, assessing the Lippincott edition in 1985, described "sunny primitivist paintings of life on a Caribbean island" and listed scenes of "market, square, and seashore, school, volcano and carnival." The same review found the book "somewhat confusing as a first-person account," since the narrator and Luca are hard to pick out in the pictures, but "engaging as a look at the milieu."

## Author and illustrator

*My Little Island* was written and illustrated by Frané Lessac. She was born in Jersey City, New Jersey, in 1954 and grew up in Cliffside Park. At 18 she moved to California to study ethnographic film at the University of Southern California and UCLA.

In 1979 she moved to Montserrat and began painting its old West Indies architecture and its people. She left in 1983 for London, where she exhibited regularly. Her first book, published in London in 1984, was inspired by the beauty of Montserrat and illustrated with her paintings of the island. Wikipedia describes it as her "love letter" to the island, and notes it was later featured on *Reading Rainbow*. She now lives in Western Australia.

Young listeners may also like her *Caribbean Alphabet* (1989), *Island Counting 1-2-3* (2005) and *A Is for Australia* (2015), and *Caribbean Carnival* (1992), which she illustrated. Traci Sorell's *We Are Grateful: Otsaliheliga*, which she illustrated, won a Boston Globe-Horn Book Picture Book Honor in 2019.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton at an airport, just after the call "Last call for boarding flight 919," and boarding a plane to Montserrat, an island in the Caribbean Sea. He mentions that the flight takes about five hours, so he brought a book that gives a sneak peek at the island, then introduces *My Little Island*. Ron Foster reads the book in full, ending on the line "We just say we'll come back soon."

Everything after the reading is cut from the podcast audio. In the television episode, LeVar lands in Montserrat and visits Plymouth Market, where a vendor shows him a christophine, a pawpaw and a pineapple. He then goes out at night with a local guide to catch a mountain chicken, which is a frog, and meets an iguana. Three young reviewers recommend other books, and LeVar closes by listening to a steel drum band.
