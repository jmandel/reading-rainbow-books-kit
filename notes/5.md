## About the book
*Louis the Fish* opens with its own surprise: "One day last spring, Louis, a butcher, turned into a fish." The story then goes back to explain how a Flatbush butcher got there. Louis runs the family shop because his grandfather and father did, but he has never liked meat. He likes watching fish, and he fills his hours in the refrigerator drawing them. Before long he sees fish everywhere.

The tone is deadpan and a little surreal. It suits listeners who enjoy a funny, odd story.

Kirkus was divided. It praised the art, saying Egielski "gives this strange tale an appropriately, expressively surreal tone, with a sturdy Flatbush setting and some apt asides." The same review was less sure of the story, writing that adults "will make what they will of Louis' transformation" and that the reviewer was "stumped for a coherent interpretation with meaning at the picture-book age level."

## Author and illustrator
Arthur Yorinks was born in Roslyn, New York, in 1953. He trained as a classical pianist from age six and came to picture books in high school through the work of Tomi Ungerer, William Steig and Maurice Sendak. At sixteen he took manuscripts to Sendak's studio, and the two became friends. Sendak later introduced him to Richard Egielski.

Richard Egielski was born in New York City in 1952 and studied at Parsons School of Design. He has illustrated more than fifty picture books and written eight of them.

*Louis the Fish* (1980) was their second picture book together, after *Sid and Sol* (1977). Wikipedia says it was inspired by Franz Kafka's *The Metamorphosis*. School Library Journal named it one of its Best Books of the Year for 1980. Young listeners who like this pair might try *It Happened in Pinsk* (1983) or *Hey, Al*, which won the Caldecott Medal for Egielski's illustrations.

## In this episode
The episode begins at the New England Aquarium, where LeVar Burton looks at the fish and wonders what it would be like to be one. He asks how fish sleep without eyelids, then says he just read a book about a person who loved fish so much that was all he could think about. Talking fish chatter about the human reading it, and the story begins.

Vincent Gardenia reads the book. The audio ends on the story's last line, "After a hard life, Louis was a happy fish."

Not in the podcast audio: kids say what animal they would be; a song about easy living under the sea; a dolphin trainer at a Cape Cod park; LeVar shows a sea urchin, horseshoe crab, sea star and moon snail, penguins at mealtime, and three children's book reviews.

Content note: the story mentions Louis's parents dying suddenly, his unhappiness and a nightmare in which cuts of meat attack him.
