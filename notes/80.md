## About the book

*Snowy Day: Stories and Poems* is an anthology edited by Caroline Feller Bauer and painted by Margot Tomes, published by HarperCollins. It gathers stories, verse and activities, with poems by writers including Ogden Nash, Gwendolyn Brooks, John Ciardi and Myra Cohn Livingston, a story by Yoshiko Uchida about Jizo statues, and a recipe for Snow Muffins from Northern Canada. A bibliography of books about snow closes the volume.

Publishers Weekly wrote that "anyone who has ever made an angel in the snow or reveled in winter" will find plenty to enjoy, and that the reader "will delight in this collection of stories, verse and activities." The review called Tomes's paintings "varied and beautifully designed," adding that they "etch black shadows on white pages with graceful charm." It also said plainly that "some poems are better than others," though it called the book "nonetheless a strong collection," and listed the book for ages 7 to 10.

## Author and illustrator

Caroline Feller Bauer edited this collection and wrote *Midnight Snowman*, a picture book Publishers Weekly reviewed in 1987. In it, a girl and her friend Elizabeth go out on a dark winter night in a town where "it never snows" until one evening, and the whole neighborhood helps roll a snowball into a huge figure. The review recommended it for ages 4 to 7.

Margot Tomes (August 10, 1917 to June 25, 1991) was born in Yonkers, New York. After graduating from Pratt Institute she worked as a designer of wallpaper and fabric. Her first book illustration came in 1959, for Daphne du Maurier's *The Breaking Point*, and in 1963, at age 46, she began illustrating children's books full time.

She illustrated over 60 titles. *Jack and the Wonder Beans* (1977) and *If There Were Dreams to Sell* (1984) were each named among The New York Times Best Illustrated Children's Books of the Year. Tomes received the Kerlan Award posthumously in 1995.

## In this episode

The podcast opens on location in Jackson Hole, Wyoming, where the host describes the below-freezing winters and the snow that "frosts everything in white," then goes snowshoeing. The host then introduces the book and the first poem, *Winter Morning* by Ogden Nash, read by Lena Horne. That recording stops partway through the poem, before its end.

Next is a poem called *Snow*, read by Lacey Chabert; it starts without a spoken title and also stops partway through. The last piece is Robert Frost's *Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening*, read by Richard Courtney and ending on "And miles to go before I sleep." The third poem is introduced by a child reviewer in cut material, so the audio begins with its title.

Not in the podcast audio: sledding down Gut Flop Hill, a visit with Alaska sled-dog racer Susan Butcher, a short lesson on snowflakes, building a snowman, and children's reviews of other books.
