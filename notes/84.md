## About the book

Babushka lives alone in a little country house and is known far and wide for the eggs she paints, which always win first prize at the Easter Festival in Moscow. After a snowstorm she rescues a goose that falls from the sky, names her Rechenka, and nurses her in a basket lined with a quilt from her own bed. The goose repays the kindness by laying an egg every morning, until one day an accident in the house changes everything and Babushka has to wonder what will become of her festival eggs. It suits listeners who like animal-rescue stories and folk tales.

Publishers Weekly (1988) called the story "truly rewarding," saying Babushka's gift for coloring eggs comes across in "a splendid array of folkloric patterns, minute detail and batik-like paintings," and listed it for ages 4-8. Kirkus Reviews (1988) was more reserved, calling the egg designs "the best feature of this slight story" and the book "not a strong offering."

## Author and illustrator

Patricia Polacco wrote and illustrated *Rechenka's Eggs*. She was born Patricia Barber on July 11, 1944, in Lansing, Michigan. After her parents divorced when she was three, she moved with her mother and brother to her grandmother's farm in Union City, Michigan. She did not learn to read until she was nearly fourteen and struggled in school until a junior high teacher recognized her dyslexia; her book *Thank You, Mr. Falker* retells that experience. Before writing for children she worked as a restoration specialist in art museums, and at 41 she began her first children's book.

In the Reading Rainbow segment, Polacco says her grandmother was Ukrainian and the family spent evenings telling stories by the fireplace. She says the idea for this book came from feeding the Canada geese at a lake near her home and thinking that geese lay eggs, and that her grandmother became the model for Babushka. The book won the 1989 International Reading Association Award.

For a next read, Polacco's other picture books include *The Keeping Quilt*, *Chicken Sunday* and *Thank You, Mr. Falker*.

## In this episode

The podcast begins partway through LeVar Burton's lead-in: "there are lots of other artists besides me who have found the egg irresistible." He shows decorated eggs, including a marbleized egg, a glass egg with painted zebra stripes, a Venetian glass egg, a wooden nesting egg with a world map and an egg printed with leaves, then introduces Ukrainian decorated eggs and the story. The earlier part of the lead-in, where he decorates his own egg from a craft kit, is not in the podcast audio, so the child hears "besides me" without that context.

Viveca Lindfors reads the whole book, and the audio ends on its last line.

Not in the podcast audio: Polacco demonstrates making a Ukrainian pysanka with wax and dye; a person stands on a row of eggs to show how strong the shape is; and film shows hatching animals, including penguins, horseshoe crabs, snakes and crocodiles. Three child reviewers follow.
