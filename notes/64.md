## About the book
Two brothers are bored. Their mother tells them she was never bored at their age and sends them outdoors to do something, and in the barn they find a propeller. From there the book follows them step by step as they decide to build an airplane, gather parts from around the house and the family car, and get it into the sky, until their parents find out what has stopped working.

Kirkus Reviews opened with "More pictured mischief from busy kids, in the sunny manner of Oh, Were They Ever Happy!" and judged it "sprightly" though "not as close to home" as that book. It was published by Doubleday in September 1978 and runs 40 pages.

It suits children who love machines, building and making things, and anyone who has ever announced there is nothing to do. Young listeners will enjoy spotting how many ordinary household things the boys borrow, and wondering what the grown-ups will say when they notice.

## Author and illustrator
Peter Spier is the author of this book. He was born in Amsterdam on June 6, 1927, the son of the popular artist and illustrator Jo Spier. During the Second World War, Peter and his father were imprisoned, first at Villa Bouchina and later at Theresienstadt. After the war he studied at the Rijksakademie in Amsterdam and served four years in the Royal Netherlands Navy. The family emigrated to the United States in 1950.

He began as a commercial artist for advertising agencies and only later focused on children's books. His *Noah's Ark* won the Caldecott Medal in 1977, the year before this book appeared, and its paperback edition won a National Book Award in 1982. He died on April 27, 2017, in Port Jefferson, New York.

Kirkus lists more of his picture books for young listeners, among them *Oh, Were They Ever Happy!* (1978), *Gobble, Growl, Grunt* (1971), *Fast-Slow, High-Low* (1972) and *The Fox Went Out on a Chilly Night* (1961).

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton on location with a quarter-scale remote-control model airplane, talking to a control tower and then showing how the radio transmitter works. He says he can do loop-the-loops, stalls and nose dives with it that he would never try in a real plane. He then turns to the book: what if you feel like flying and you do not have a plane? That is the problem the two boys face.

The book is read by Alex Borg and David Hyman, and the audio ends on the last line of the story.

Not in the podcast audio: a history of flight in song, a look at how a jet is prepared at an airport, LeVar's flying lesson at Nassau Flyers flight school, and three young reviewers.

Content note: the parents are shocked and angry about the missing household things, and the boys are sent to their room.
