## About the book

Mr. Popsnorkle's old green Thunderclap 8 is just about worn out, so he, Mrs. Popsnorkle and the five little Popsnorkles pile in and go car shopping along the highway where all the dealers are. The family spots a shiny Tooth-Gnasher Superflash, and salesman Mr. Sandy says it is the last one left. Then Mr. Popsnorkle asks whether he might drive, and he starts pushing buttons on the dashboard to see what each one does.

The book rewards being read aloud. The children shout "Yay!" at every surprise, Mrs. Popsnorkle keeps praising the lovely color, and Mr. Popsnorkle stays cheerfully calm while Mr. Sandy grows more puzzled. Young listeners who like vehicles, silly escalation and a steady refrain will be at home here.

Kirkus, reviewing it in March 1981, opened with "Funny-man Pinkwater takes us on another ride" and called the car "versatile". Rob Reid, a library system coordinator, wrote on a children's-literature mailing list in 2002 that it is "just plain fun to read aloud" and one of Pinkwater's "most appealing books for general audiences".

## Author and illustrator

Daniel Pinkwater wrote and illustrated this book himself. He was born Manus Pinkwater on November 15, 1941, in Memphis, Tennessee, and was raised in Chicago. He earned a B.A. at Bard College in 1964 and studied sculpture privately. His first children's book was *The Terrible Roar* (1970). He has also been a longtime commentator on National Public Radio, including *All Things Considered* from 1987, and has hosted *Chinwag Theater*.

Encyclopedia.com describes his illustrations as usually "deceptively simple, cartoonlike", with heavy outlines filled with bright colors. Tooth-Gnasher Superflash first appeared from Four Winds in 1981.

Listeners who enjoy this one may like his other self-illustrated picture books, including *Bear's Picture* (1972) and *Blue Moose* (1975). His wife, Jill Pinkwater, was an author and illustrator who illustrated many of his later books.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar at a service station, where he says he loves working on cars and has since he owned his first one. He checks an air filter and spark plugs, then says he is not sure what he would do with the car in this book.

Victoria Jackson reads *Tooth-Gnasher Superflash* in full. The audio stops at the family's closing remark that the new car cannot do half the things their old Thunderclap 8 could. Nothing in the audio is frightening or sad.

Not in the podcast audio: children describe fantasy cars, including a shark car and an invisible one; a man shows off his homemade car, decorated with crickets, fruit and the word "peace" in many languages; a solar-powered race car crosses Australia; and three children review the books below.
