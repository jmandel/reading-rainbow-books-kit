## About the book
A girl, her cousin Sam, her mother and her Aunt Rosie pool their money to buy a red canoe someone is selling in a yard, then plan a three-day camping trip down a river. The book is an album the girl made of the trip: shopping for life jackets and dried apricots, a very long drive, a misty morning, a waterfall to carry the canoe around, rain that gives way to a rainbow, and one mishap on the last day.

It suits children who like real things, such as how a tent goes up or how a camp kitchen works, and its small scenes can be read a little at a time.

Kirkus Reviews (April 1981) noted that the "crayon drawings are suggestive of a child's," and that the book's "illustrated recipes for dumplings and fruit stew" and "illustrated instructions for putting up a tent" sit alongside the story. The reviewer's verdict: "The resonant wordings, the eventfulness, the information" combine "into an experience that can be relived, with new discoveries, again and again."

## Author and illustrator
Vera B. Williams wrote and illustrated this book herself. She was born on January 28, 1927, in Hollywood, California, grew up partly in the Bronx, and earned a degree in graphic art at Black Mountain College in North Carolina in 1949. She helped found the Gate Hill Cooperative Community and taught for years before moving to Canada after her 1970 divorce to commit to writing and illustrating children's books. Her publisher was Greenwillow Books.

Williams herself once took a 500-mile trip down the Yukon River, and the library-system biography that mentions it describes this book as combining how-to information with the story of a family adventure. It received a Parents' Choice Award for Illustration in 1981.

Young listeners may want to find her next book, *A Chair for My Mother*, a Caldecott Honor book, or *"More More More," Said the Baby*, also a Caldecott Honor book. In 2009 she received the NSK Neustadt Prize for Children's Literature. She died on October 16, 2015.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton: "I'm about to leave to meet some friends and go on an overnight camping trip," as he gathers food, a sleeping bag, a tent and a waterproof jacket. The friends never appear in the audio.

Carolyn McMahon reads the book, in four excerpts rather than the whole: shopping through the waterfall camp; two sentences about the evening fire; the rainy morning, rainbow and fishing; and the last day, ending on "the sound of the river running over the rocks."

Content note: in the last excerpt Sam stands up in the canoe and falls in the water, is towed to shore, and the grown-ups look upset and scared.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar pitching a tent and cooking fruit stew, friends joining him for a campfire story game, LeVar's own whitewater rafting trip on the Zambezi River, a segment on children making summer albums, and children reviewing books.
