## About the book
*Evelyn the Adventurous Entomologist* is a picture-book biography of Evelyn Cheesman, born in England in 1881, when girls were expected to be quiet and tidy. Evelyn crawls through mud and fills her pockets with bugs anyway. The first turn comes when she wants to be a veterinarian, and the college will not take women. She finds another path, taking charge of the insect house at London Zoo, and later sets off for the Pacific Islands to study tropical insects. A refrain runs through the book: "But Evelyn went anyway."

It suits children who love bugs, explorers and true stories, and it is full of small, vivid scenes, such as a tangle in spider webs and a scramble up a steep cliff. Back matter follows the story. Kirkus Reviews, which recommended ages 6 to 8, found the art appealing but the human characters bland and one-dimensional, and judged the book "Too glib for comfort." The same review noted that endnotes, a bibliography and an interview with a present-day entomologist come after the story. Innovation Press published it in 2019.

## Author and illustrator
Christine Evans is a British writer. In a 2019 interview she said she had lived in the San Francisco Bay Area for almost eight years, had written for *Highlights* and *High Five*, and has two daughters. She wanted to write about a woman who defied expectations, and said that on finding Evelyn Cheesman "my stomach fluttered and I knew I'd found my subject." Her main sources were Cheesman's own autobiographies and other books; because Cheesman made eight solo trips, deciding what to leave out was the hardest part.

Yas (Yasmin) Imamura lives and works in Portland, Oregon. Editor Asia Citro at Innovation Press suggested her for this book. Her other picture books include *Love in the Library* by Maggie Tokuda-Hall, *Winged Wonders* by Meeg Pincus and *The Very Oldest Pear Tree* by Nancy I. Sanders, according to her Albert Whitman author page. The Perfect Picture Book Friday blog, quoting the book, says Cheesman collected over seventy thousand specimens, some not yet recorded by scientists.

## In this episode
The host opens the book segment by asking whether listeners are looking for a book, and says that anyone who likes meeting insects and making bug hotels will like this one. Those bug hotels come from the cut part of the episode, so the child hears that line without context. Actress Constance Wu reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, "go anyway."

Not in the podcast audio: a nine-year-old named Lucy takes the host on a bug hunt and introduces her pet bugs, including a tarantula and a Madagascar hissing cockroach; children make shoebox bug hotels at the library; Emery's Memory Club asks quiz questions about the beetles and the book; and three children review other books.

Content note: in the reading, Evelyn is stuck in sticky spider webs, slips and tumbles down a cliff, and is described as being all alone; she also cares for sick dogs.
