## About the book
One morning Big Bear is eating breakfast and grumbling over the city paper, because none of its stories are about his own neighborhood. His fix is to start a newspaper for the animals, and he hands out the jobs himself: he will be publisher, and Rabbit becomes the news editor. The book then follows a story from tip to finished paper: reporters ask questions, Rabbit pushes for a stronger lead and a catchy headline, Fox oversees the features, and the staff sells ads and pastes up the pages.

Listeners pick up newsroom vocabulary (headline, editorial, features) inside a lively animal story. It suits curious preschoolers and early-elementary children who like to know how everyday things are made.

Kirkus calls it "A cheerful, entertaining introduction with enough sensible suggestions to get young journalists off to a good start," and notes that it uses "animal characters and a cartoon style." The print edition also has a glossary and an outline of more specific instructions at the back. Holiday House published it in 1990.

## Author and illustrator
Loreen Leedy wrote and illustrated *The Furry News*, so one person made both the words and the pictures. According to her website, she graduated from college with a degree in Art and was not sure at first how to use her skills, so she sculpted jewelry and chess sets from polymer clay and sold them at festivals. At age 25 she began combining words and illustrations in children's books and soon signed her first publishing contract. She says she has written and illustrated over 40 children's books, known for teaching math, science and language arts in an engaging way. Later she became a mixed media artist and now lives in central Florida.

Her books in the same how-it-works style include *Messages in the Mailbox: How to Write a Letter* (1991), which Kirkus called "an appealing introduction to correspondence," and *The Great Trash Bash* (1991), about animals tackling a litter problem. Her math picture books include *Fraction Action* (1994) and *Measuring Penny* (1998).

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton talking about newspapers: "Every morning, one of the first things I do is buy a newspaper." He shows papers in other languages, including a Spanish-language one, the Japanese Nihon Keizai Shimbun, which is read up and down, and The Forward, in Yiddish, which is read right to left. He then introduces the book.

The book is read by the cast of Forbidden Broadway (Susanne Blakeslee, Dorothy Kiara, Brad Oscar and Craig Wells) and Lee Murphy, ending as the animals read their finished paper. Content note: a flood and a burglary come up as story tips.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks about newspaper sections after the reading, a visit with Ray Billingsley, creator of the comic strip *Curtis*, who tells how Curtis was first drawn in the dark, a visit to the Bear Facts school newspaper staff at Bergenfield High, and the children's reviews of the recommended books.
