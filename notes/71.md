## About the book

Alistair is "a boy of science" who collects slimy things in jars, enjoys watching cells divide and knows the Milky Way "like the back of his hand." When his town holds its second annual science competition, everyone expects him to win, so he builds a time machine. Setting it for the day before yesterday, he pushes the button and lands somewhere much farther back: lunch with King Arthur's knights, a royal ball in France, a pirate ship, an arena in Rome and a campsite of cave people. Each time he tries to head home, the machine takes him somewhere else, and a stampede of woolly mammoths soon needs sorting out.

The jokes come from the gap between a very calm narrator and very odd events, which suits listeners who like silly history and a hero who takes it all in stride. School Library Journal's Lauralyn Persson, reviewing it for Grades 1-3, wrote that "the deadpan, uninflected prose is a perfect counterpoint to the brightly exaggerated, cartoon-like pictures." The book was first published in 1986.

## Author and illustrator

Marilyn Sadler was born November 17, 1950, in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. An author biography on Goodreads says that with a degree in fine arts she assumed she would be an illustrator, but she turned to writing. Her first children's book featured Alistair, and she went on to create the P. J. Funnybunny series, followed by the Honey Bunny series about P. J.'s little sister, and *Elizabeth and Larry*, about a friendship between an old lady and a young alligator. Her television work includes two Reading Rainbow programs featuring Alistair, and she is one of the creators of the Disney Junior show *Handy Manny*.

Roger Bollen (1941-2015) was born in East Cleveland, Ohio, and graduated from Kent State University. He drew the newspaper comic strip *Animal Crackers* from 1967 to 1994. With his second wife, Marilyn Sadler, he produced more than 50 children's books; their first, *Alistair's Elephant*, appeared in 1983. Listeners who enjoy this one could try Sadler's *Alistair in Outer Space* or the P. J. Funnybunny books.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar visiting Discovery Park, "the only spot in the universe where you can actually meet inventors from different times in history." There he talks with Blug, a Stone Age inventor working on the bowl, and watches Blug's brother's nutcracker turn into the first wheel. LeVar then introduces *Alistair's Time Machine*, and Arnold Stang reads the book. The audio runs to the book's last line, where Alistair knows his machine worked but wonders how he could prove it.

Not in the podcast audio: kids describe the inventions they would like to make; a film of wild inventions that never caught on, such as a flying car and a push-button nursery; LeVar meeting Thomas Edison, who invents a boom box and a Frisbee; kids reviewing three books; and a talking machine named Frisbee.
