## About the book

*More Than Peach* is a picture book about a girl who loves her mornings, her brothers, her mom and her school. At school, friends keep asking for "the skin color crayon" and mean the peach one. Every time she hands it over, something in her feels off, and she can't understand why nobody else seems bothered. Her mom has one idea, the girl has a better one, and the book follows what happens when she starts quietly asking a question of her own.

It is based on the real experience of its author, Bellen Woodard, and told in a calm first-person voice. It is a good starting point for talking with a child about skin color, fairness and speaking up kindly.

Kirkus Reviews described the "appealing, realistic artwork" and said "the story is stirring and will motivate budding activists." The book was published by Scholastic in 2022 and has 40 pages, including a note from Woodard about her journey into activism and instructions for starting a drive of your own.

## Author and illustrator

Bellen Woodard is from Leesburg, Virginia. In third grade she noticed that classmates called the peach crayon the "skin-colored" crayon, and she began making art kits with crayons and markers in many skin tones. NBC reported that her goal was to put multicultural art kits in every elementary school in Loudoun County. She started the More Than Peach Project, and Because of Them We Can reports that she was named a TIME "Kid of the Year" at age 10 and had donated more than $40,000 worth of art bundles.

*More Than Peach* is her first picture book. In the episode she says her second book, *Ballet Brown*, had come out recently; Open Library lists it with the same illustrator. She explains in the episode that she coined the term "ballet brown" to go with it.

Fanny Liem illustrated the book. Her bio says she is an artist based in Jakarta, Indonesia, who has always loved to draw and to read comic books and children's books, and who studied digital art on her own. She also illustrated *Ballet Brown*.

## In this episode

The host, Mychal Threets as "Michael the Librarian," opens in the library and says it is a place for talking as well as quiet. He introduces the book as one by a girl who was brave enough to speak out, and who wrote about it at age 9. Gabrielle Union reads *More Than Peach*, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "one crayon at a time."

Not in the podcast audio: a short quiz on the story, a visit with Bellen Woodard at 14 in her dance studio and at a library book signing for *Ballet Brown*, a postcard-writing activism craft with children, an activist named Blair Imani answering the children's questions, and kids at a library recommending books written by kids.
