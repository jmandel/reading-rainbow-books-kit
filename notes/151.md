## About the book

Sam thinks he is the luckiest boy in the universe. His teacher, Mr. Albright, turns the classroom into a solar system every year, with desks clustered like constellations and planets to paint. The one bad thing about this year is the big test, which covers everything the class has ever learned. On Monday Mr. Albright names the date, and Sam stops feeling lucky.

From there the rumors multiply. Classmates, older kids, a cousin, a grandfather and even Sam's mother each have a story about what the test does to a person, and Sam worries harder with every telling.

This is a gentle, funny book for any child who has worried before a big day, and the space jokes carry through the whole story. Kirkus said "Poydar works her magic here," praising the "goofy preposterousness of the legends surrounding the test." Holiday House published it in 2005 as a 32-page picture book for ages 4 to 8.

## Author and illustrator

Nancy Poydar wrote and illustrated this book herself. Kirkus lists a long run of picture books by her. They include *Snip, Snip...Snow!* (1997), *Mailbox Magic* (2000), *The Perfectly Horrible Halloween* (2001), *Bunny Business* (2003), *Last Day, Hooray!* (2004), *The Bad-News Report Card* (2006), *Zip, Zip...Homework* (2008), *Fish School* (2009), *No Fair Science Fair* (2011) and *Bus Driver* (2012).

Her school-day books suit listeners who liked this one. Kirkus described *The Bad-News Report Card*, about a girl named Isabel who is afraid of what her report card will say, as having illustrations in gouache and pencil that "perfectly reflect her worry and excitement."

Poydar has also illustrated books written by other authors. These include *Will There Be a Lap for Me?* by Dorothy Corey (1992), *Beezy* by Megan McDonald (1997) and its follow-up *Beezy at Bat* (1998).

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar Burton finding a bunch of old school work, including a sixth-grade multiplication test. He remembers how nervous he was that day and that he asked his mom if he could stay home, and she said no. He then introduces Sam and his friends, who cannot avoid the biggest test in the universe.

Richard Gere reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, that Sam "had taken the biggest test in the universe and lived."

Content note: the story is about worry. Sam wants to cry, says he is sick to stay home, and hears tall tales such as arms falling off.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's closing remark about the pressure kids feel, then a visit to TADA!, a theater group in New York City, where kids audition for, rehearse and perform a show called *Everything You Wanted to Know About Camp Almost*. Three young reviewers discuss the recommended books.
