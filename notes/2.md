## About the book
Miss Nelson is leaving for a week to have her tonsils out, and the second graders in Room 207 plan to take full advantage. Then a bigger kid from Room 309 reminds them what happened to others who tried it: the dreaded substitute Viola Swamp, "the meanest substitute in the whole world." On Monday morning the class waits nervously, but the door opens on the principal, Mr. Blandsworth, whose card tricks and slides of his goldfish turn out to be a different kind of problem. The class decides something must be done, and a plot of costumes and borrowed clothes begins.

This is the sequel to *Miss Nelson Is Missing!*. It suits listeners who enjoy a classroom caper and a disguise. Kirkus called it "A nifty performance, always a step or two ahead of the audience." Publishers Weekly, reviewing in 1986, called it "A nutty sequel to Miss Nelson Is Missing." The book was published by Houghton Mifflin in 1982.

## Author and illustrator
Harry Allard was born in Evanston, Illinois, on January 27, 1928, and died on February 1, 2017. He studied at Northwestern University, earned a master's degree in French from Middlebury College and a doctorate in French literature from Yale in 1952. Besides the Miss Nelson books, he wrote *The Stupids* series and *It's So Nice to Have a Wolf Around the House*, all illustrated by James Marshall. *Miss Nelson Is Missing!* won the Georgia Children's Book Award and the California Young Readers Medal, among other honors.

James Marshall was born in San Antonio, Texas, in 1942. A promising viola student, he left music after an accident injured his hand at 19, later taught French and moved to New York to illustrate. His first picture book was *Plink, Plink, Plink* by Byrd Baylor (1971), and *George and Martha* (1972) was his first as author and illustrator. As illustrator of *Goldilocks and the Three Bears*, he was a Caldecott Medal runner-up in 1989. Listeners who like this book may enjoy the *George and Martha* stories. Marshall died in 1992.

## In this episode
The host's lead-in begins with LeVar Burton saying it is his birthday and hoping for cards or a package. A messenger delivers a package with a note and a clue, and LeVar follows it to a bookstore, where a clerk hands him *Miss Nelson Is Back*. The child hears this without the treasure hunt around it.

Ruth Buzzi reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line, about the class wondering why Miss Nelson had not seen Miss Viola in the hall.

Content note: the children are afraid of the substitute Miss Swamp, who orders them about.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's remarks on disguise, a Hollywood makeup artist transforming him with pieces fitted over his face, three young reviewers, a riddle book, and the rest of the treasure hunt, ending at a magic show with Blackstone and a birthday cake.
