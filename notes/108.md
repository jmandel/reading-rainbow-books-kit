## About the book
Stellaluna is a baby fruit bat who is carried through the night sky by her mother as she searches for fruit. One night an owl swoops down, and the little bat is knocked loose and tumbles into the forest below. She ends up in a nest with three young birds, Pip, Flitter and Flap, and their mother, who has rules for anyone who lives in her house. A bat who loves hanging by her feet has a lot to sort out.

Kirkus listed it for ages 4 to 8, called the illustrations "exquisite," and gave this verdict: "Delightful and informative but never didactic: a splendid debut."

Young listeners who like animals, nighttime and a good mix-up about who belongs where will find plenty to enjoy.

## Author and illustrator
Janell Cannon wrote and illustrated Stellaluna. She was born in St. Paul, Minnesota, and began painting with acrylics in sixth grade. After graduating from Burnsville High School in 1975 she moved to Southern California, where she worked as a graphic artist for the Carlsbad public library from 1981 to 1993.

Stellaluna was her first book. Cannon has said she empathized with bats because so many people fear and mistreat them, and while working at the library she noticed that only three books in the children's section featured bats. A trip to Thailand, where she felt at home despite not speaking the language, led her to ask, "How can we be so different, yet feel so much the same?" She drew the pictures with wax-based pencils and airbrushed acrylic paint, working from photographs of Gambian epauletted fruit bats.

Stellaluna was published by Harcourt in 1993 and has been translated into thirty languages. It appears on the National Education Association's "Teachers' Top 100 Books for Children" list. Cannon's later picture books include *Trupp: A Fuzzhead Tale*, *Verdi* and *Crickwing*.

## In this episode
The episode opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in: "I just love nighttime," he says, describing the calm, the stars and the moon. He goes on to the flying fox, a fruit bat that hangs upside down, sleeps by day and prefers fruit, with a ripe fig as the example, and then pulls out a book about a fruit bat. The book is read aloud by Anne Jackson, and the audio ends on its last line.

Content note: the story includes an owl attack, a fall, cold and fear, and a baby bat separated from her mother.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit with bat scientist Dr. Merlin Tuttle inside Bracken Cave in Texas, where millions of bats live; a visit to a sleep lab at the NYU Bellevue Sleep Center, where a girl is hooked up to electrodes so scientists can watch her brain waves through the night; and kid reviews of three books.
