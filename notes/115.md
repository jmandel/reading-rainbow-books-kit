## About the book

Archibald Frisby is a boy who is, as Kirkus puts it, "obsessed with all things scientific." He reads constantly, takes things apart, ignores kids his own age, and dissects his mother's flowers. When his school calls about the algebra text he took to recess, Mrs. Frisby decides that a summer camp might teach him what fun is. Archibald protests, then watches her car drive away and wonders whether she was right.

The story is told in rhyme. Kirkus praised the "scientific terms deftly inserted into neatly fashioned verse" and the "witty illustrations of a cheerfully irrepressible kid," and closed its review of the 1994 book with "Hooray for nerds!" That makes it a good match for curious listeners who ask how and why about everything, and for any child who has been nervous about going somewhere new.

## Author and illustrator

Michael Chesworth wrote and illustrated *Archibald Frisby*, published by Farrar, Straus and Giroux in 1994, so one person made both the words and the pictures. His earlier picture book as author and illustrator was *Rainy Day Dream* (1992).

Alongside his own books, Chesworth has illustrated stories by other writers. These include *Pippi Longstocking's After-Christmas Party* (1996), by Astrid Lindgren, and *Jacob Two-Two's First Spy Case* (1997), by Mordecai Richler. He also drew *Inventor McGregor* (2006) by Kathleen T. Pelley, *Mom for Mayor* (2006) by Nancy Edwards, and *Creaky Old House* (2009) by Linda Ashman.

Listeners who enjoy the wordplay in *Archibald Frisby* may like his later book *Alphaboat* (2002), which he also wrote and illustrated. In it the letters of the alphabet build a boat to hunt for buried treasure. Kirkus called it "alphabetical legerdemain" and said "anyone who appreciates language will laugh out loud."

## In this episode

The audio opens on location at Six Flags Great Adventure, where the host rides a roller coaster, wonders how it goes so fast, and introduces a book about a guy who never stopped asking questions. It begins "Should I or shouldn't I? That is the question." Arnold Stang then reads the whole book. The audio ends on the book's last line, which breaks off as Archibald tells his mother he has forgotten his best souvenir.

Content note: Archibald is sent to camp against his wishes, watches his mother drive away, and feels blue.

Not in the podcast audio: how the coaster works, hook-and-loop fasteners, crash test dummies, astronauts eating and sleeping in space, a frog game, a high jumper using computer analysis, and the book reviews.
