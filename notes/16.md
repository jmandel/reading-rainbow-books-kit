## About the book

Henry is a Siamese cat who wants one thing: to fly. His family has gathered on a snowy March day for a hot-air balloon outing, and the man of the house has declared that he is not flying with that cat. Henry grumbles and waits for his chance. When the balloon stands fat and beautiful in the air, he races across the snow and leaps into the basket. One snagged claw on the burner cord later, the balloon is rising, and Henry is up, up and away with nobody else aboard.

*Hot-Air Henry* was published by William Morrow in 1981 and is the second of the Henry the Siamese cat picture books. It suits listeners who like a small adventure with a bit of danger and a lot of humor. A reviewer at Pussreboots calls Erick Ingraham's illustrations "wonderful" and "the right balance of realistic and humorous," and a publisher's description promises "a fur-raising flight indeed."

## Author and illustrator

Mary Calhoun was born in Keokuk, Iowa, on August 3, 1926. She earned a journalism degree from the University of Iowa in 1948. Her storytelling began as a way to entertain her two sons, and her first book, *Making the Mississippi Shout*, appeared in 1957. She went on to write more than 50 books, including the Katie John series, and her final book, *Henry the Christmas Cat*, appeared in 2004. An obituary says one of her best-received works was the Henry picture-book series, with *Cross-Country Cat* the most popular. She died in 2015.

Erick Ingraham's bio says he began calling himself an illustrator in 1974, when he received his first book contract, and credits him with an American Book Award for *Porcupine Stew*. He is best known for the Henry books. He lives in southern New Hampshire.

Listeners who enjoy Henry can look for *Cross-Country Cat* and *High-Wire Henry*, which won the California Young Reader Medal in 1994.

## In this episode

The podcast begins with LeVar Burton at a balloon launch saying, "Pretty magical, isn't it?" This refers to the hot-air balloon launch from the cut opening, so the child hears it without that context. He then introduces the high-flying cat and names the book, author and illustrator. The book is read by William Windom, and the audio ends on the book's last line, "Wise old flying cat."

Content note: the reading includes a loud burner roar that frightens Henry, a close call with power lines and a goose that nips his tail.

Not in the podcast audio: the opening at the Palmetto Balloon Classic in Camden, South Carolina, where LeVar helps launch a balloon; a comic look at old flying contraptions that never got off the ground; a visit to the National Space Camp in Huntsville, Alabama; and children's reviews of the books below.
