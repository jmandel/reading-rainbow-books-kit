## About the book

Appelemando lives in a very drab village and loves to dream. His dreams are "magic chariots pulled through his mind by galloping hues of color," and when he daydreams with his friends, the dreams drift out of the top of his head where everyone can see them. The villagers think he never does anything useful. The children discover that a dream will stay on anything damp, so a rainy day turns the town into a surprise, and the grown-ups are not pleased.

The book suits ages 4 to 8 and it is a good fit for children who like colour, daydreaming or drawing. Kirkus Reviews found the plot "a bit contrived" and the dreams themselves less compelling than the art as a whole, but called the book "a potent original fable" and praised the energetic illustrations. Publishers Weekly was warmest about the pictures, singling out the contrast between the grey village and the bright dreams, and said the message, about the value of dreamers, is somewhat similar to that of Leo Lionni's *Frederick*, though the prose lacks Lionni's subtlety.

## Author and illustrator

Patricia Polacco wrote and illustrated *Appelemando's Dreams*, published by Philomel Books in 1991. She was born on July 11, 1944, in Lansing, Michigan. After her parents divorced when she was about three, she moved with her mother and brother to her grandmother's farm in Union City, Michigan, where she grew up hearing stories from both sides of her family. She said, "In both households I had these amazing storytellers." She did not learn to read until nearly age 14, and a junior high teacher realized she had dyslexia. Her book *Thank You, Mr. Falker* retells that experience.

She earned a master's degree and a PhD in art history and worked as a restoration specialist in art museums. She began her first children's book at age 41, and she lives in Union City, Michigan.

Her awards include the 1988 Sydney Taylor Book Award for *The Keeping Quilt*. For listeners who enjoy her work, *The Keeping Quilt*, *Chicken Sunday*, *Thank You, Mr. Falker* and *Mr. Lincoln's Way* are other Polacco picture books.

## In this episode

The podcast opens with a speaker painting a picture of the street, saying "This is the way I see it," and pointing out the neighbour next door and a tree at the end of the street. Children then say where their art ideas come from, including the subway, the zoo and dreams.

*Appelemando's Dreams* is read by Michael Learned. The audio ends on the book's last line, "For him, dreaming is a way of life."

Content note: the villagers are angry and the mayor shouts at the children. The children get lost in the woods and worry, and Appelemando cries that he cannot dream anymore.

Not in the podcast audio: a reflection on expressing feelings with crayons and paper; a wire sculptor; a muralist painting a brick wall; two potters; a potato-and-sponge printing demonstration; a New York sidewalk chalk artist; and the kids' reviews.
