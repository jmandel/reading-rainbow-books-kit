## About the book
*The Bionic Bunny Show* is two books in one. Kirkus Reviews said so in 1984, and the setup is simple: Wilbur, a rabbit, plays a superhero on a television show, and the story follows a single day of making an episode. Viewers meet him in the makeup chair, then in wardrobe, where he is fitted with bionic ears, sneakers and a wristwatch. Then the cameras roll, and the show's heroics keep colliding with the work behind the camera: retakes, rubber bricks, a wind machine turned up too high.

Kirkus was mixed. The reviewer found "hard-to-distinguish technical business in the behind-the-scenes scenes," but added that "the full-color watercolors are packed with on-the-set detail" and "the action doesn't let up." Wilbur's "good-humored fluster," the review said, "has built-in appeal."

It suits children who like to see how things are made, and parents who don't mind pausing to explain a few unfamiliar terms. The book was published by Atlantic/Little, Brown in 1984.

## Author and illustrator
Laurene Krasny Brown is a writer and, according to Wikipedia, a psychologist at Harvard University. She married Marc Brown on September 11, 1983, the year before this book was published.

Marc Brown was born November 25, 1946, in Erie, Pennsylvania, and graduated from the Cleveland Institute of Art in 1968 with a degree in graphic design. His first book as an illustrator was *What Makes the Sun Shine?* by Isaac Asimov, in 1970. Around that time his four-year-old son Tolon asked for a bedtime story about a weird animal, and Brown made up Arthur, an aardvark. *Arthur's Nose*, his first Arthur picture book, appeared in 1976 from Little, Brown. The animated *Arthur* series premiered on PBS Kids on October 7, 1996, and Brown is a three-time Emmy award winner.

The couple also wrote the Dino Life Guides for Families together, including *Dinosaurs Divorce*, *Dinosaurs Travel* and *Democracy for Dinosaurs*. Young listeners who enjoy Marc Brown's drawings may also like his *Arthur* picture books, starting with *Arthur's Nose*.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton welcoming listeners aboard the starship Enterprise from *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, where he played the ship's navigator, Lieutenant Geordi La Forge. He explains that the people, equipment, time and energy behind a television show never appear on screen, and then introduces this book about making one. His "let me show you" points to set footage that is not in the podcast.

Gene Klavan reads the book, from Wilbur's morning arrival to its last lines at home.

Not in the podcast audio: the rest of the episode, a visit to the *Star Trek: The Next Generation* set. It covers the actors' makeup, a rehearsal, a scene shot from several camera angles, editing at a post-production studio, and a look at how the model of the Enterprise and the visual effects are made. Three children also review other books about performances and making shows.
