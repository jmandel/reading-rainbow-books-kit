## About the book
*Snowy Day: Stories and Poems* is an anthology built around one subject: snow. Caroline Feller Bauer gathered short fiction, poems, tales and a few hands-on activities, with a bibliography of snow books at the end for teachers and parents. The contributors include Gwendolyn Brooks, Ogden Nash, Isaac Bashevis Singer, John Ciardi, Myra Cohn Livingston and Yoshiko Uchida,. Publishers Weekly, which listed it for ages 7 to 10, called it "a strong collection" while admitting that "some poems are better than others," and described Margot Tomes's paintings as "varied and beautifully designed." TeachingBooks lists it as a 1986 CCBC Choices selection.

An anthology suits short listening: each piece stands alone, so a toddler can drift in and out without losing a plot.

## Author and illustrator
Caroline Feller Bauer (1935-2013) was a children's librarian, teacher and storyteller. According to the Writers Directory entry on Encyclopedia.com, she worked at the New York Public Library, served as librarian at the Colorado Rocky Mountain School, and was an associate professor of library science at the University of Oregon from 1966 to 1979. Her *Handbook for Storytellers* appeared in 1977. She edited *Rainy Day: Stories and Poems* (1986) and wrote *Midnight Snowman* (1987) and *Windy Day: Stories and Poems* (1988), all listed in the same entry.

Margot Tomes (1917-1991) was born in Yonkers, New York, and after Pratt Institute worked as a wallpaper and fabric designer. At forty-six, in 1963, she began illustrating children's books full time. Her *Jack and the Wonder Beans* (1977) and *If There Were Dreams to Sell* (1984) were each named among the New York Times Best Illustrated Children's Books of the Year, and she illustrated Jean Fritz's *Homesick: My Own Story*, a Newbery Honor book.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton in Jackson Hole, Wyoming: "Hi, here we are in Jackson Hole, Wyoming, where the temperature is below freezing almost all winter." He takes a walk in snowshoes, noting that snow crunches underfoot and that, when you stop, you can hear the quiet. He then picks up the book and reads out the first poem.

Because this is an anthology, the podcast contains three poems from it: Ogden Nash's "Winter Morning," read by Lena Horne; "Snow" by Carla Kuskin, read by Lacey Chabert; and Robert Frost's "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening," read by Richard Courtney. The Nash poem is trimmed before its final lines, and the audio ends on the last line of the Frost poem.

Not in the podcast audio: sledding on Gut Flop Hill; a visit with Alaska sled-dog racer Susan Butcher, who talks about running the Iditarod; snowflakes; building a snowman; and three children's book reviews.
