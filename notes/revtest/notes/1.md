## About the book
A boy keeps asking his parents for a dog, and the answer is always "Not now." Then his father explains why: these are "tight times," which means everything keeps going up. The boy sees it in small changes at home: bulk cereal instead of little boxes, a sprinkler instead of the lake, lima beans instead of roast beef, and Mrs. Macintosh minding him because his mother is at work. Then his father comes home in the middle of the day, and the boy learns what has been lost. The boy tells it all himself, with a young child's logic and dry humor.

It suits children who have heard "we can't afford it." As quoted at Encyclopedia.com, Booklist said it has "a sweet, sad poignancy." Kirkus was less kind, calling the illustrations drab and the family's emotional moments heavy-handed, though it noted the boy's happiness with his pet and his name for it as the one lighthearted touch. Viking published it in 1979.

## Author and illustrator
Barbara Shook Hazen was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1930 and studied at Smith College and Columbia University. She worked as poetry editor at *Ladies' Home Journal* and as a children's book editor at Western Publishing before turning freelance in 1960. Her publisher's biography credits her with more than 80 books for young readers, including the Little Golden Book *The Please and Thank You Book*. Her *Even If I Did Something Awful* won a Christopher Award, and *The Knight Who Was Afraid of the Dark* won a Children's Choice Award. Other titles listed at Encyclopedia.com include *Fang*, *Mommy's Office* and *Alone at Home*.

Trina Schart Hyman (1939 to 2004) grew up in Wyncote, Pennsylvania, where *Little Red Riding Hood* was her favorite story. She studied in Boston and in Stockholm and was the first art director of *Cricket* magazine. She illustrated more than 150 books and won the 1985 Caldecott Medal for *Saint George and the Dragon*. Her own *Little Red Riding Hood* and *Hershel and the Hanukkah Goblins* were both named Caldecott Honor books.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton telling listeners that some kids just can't have pets, and that not getting something you badly want still hurts, before he introduces *Tight Times* by Barbara Shook Hazen, pictures by Trina Schart Hyman. The reading follows. Patrick McCaffrey reads the book. The opening refers to a pet show from the TV episode, which is not included, so a child hears it without that context.
