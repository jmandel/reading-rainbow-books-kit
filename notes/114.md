## About the book

Clarence is a sign painter, and the town calls him Crabby Clarence because he complains about everything, most of all his job. His one pleasure is a history book about the Revolutionary War, and he wishes he had lived in George Washington's day, when ordinary people did glorious and magnificent things and were never bored.

Then a small gray-haired woman comes into the shop and asks for a big sign reading "Free apples if you need them." She wants it glorious and magnificent, and she wants it for nothing. Clarence quotes his price and sends her away, and that night a very unusual visitor turns up in his dreams.

The story is a funny lesson in generosity, with a grumpy hero children can laugh at and root for. It suits listeners who like a stubborn character, a rhyming bit of family wisdom from Aunt Tilly, and a touch of history. Publishers Weekly, reviewing it for ages 4 to 8, called it "A gentle charmer."

## Author and illustrator

Roger Almon Roth wrote *The Sign Painter's Dream*, published by Crown in 1993; Publishers Weekly said he "imbues these zestful and believable characters with an infectious spirit all their own." Kirkus Reviews lists thirteen children's books written or illustrated by Roth, from 1992 to 2012.

Among his earlier books is *The Giraffe That Walked to Paris* by Nancy Milton (Crown, 1992), the true story of the first giraffe seen in France. In 1826 she was a gift to King Charles X, and she traveled by ship to Marseilles and then walked to Paris. Kirkus described Roth's pictures there as lively, detailed panoramas with gently caricatured people and animals.

Roth also wrote and illustrated *Fishing for Methuselah* (HarperCollins, 1998), which Kirkus called "an unabashedly outrageous tale" of two rival fishermen, Ivan and Olaf, who compete at a Winter Carnival to catch a giant fish. Kirkus praised his "wry humor" and ended with the verdict "A rousing adventure with irascible, lovable heros."

## In this episode

The audio opens with a lead-in about signs: "Oh, signs. Signs. Signs. They're everywhere." It lists traffic signs, billboards and store signs, then plays quick sign-themed clips, including a walking, talking sign, before introducing this story.

Jamie Farr reads the book, and the audio ends in the story's final sentence, as Clarence makes a sign now and then "just for fun."

Content note: in the dream, a soldier's hardship is mentioned, with men walking in the snow with rags wrapped around their feet.

Not in the podcast audio: a visit to Times Square, where workers replace bulbs and a New York sign company designs, builds and hangs a huge lighted coffee cup with neon tubing. The kids' reviews of three books follow, then a short history of advertising, from door-to-door selling to radio and television commercials. LeVar signs off in front of a Times Square screen.
