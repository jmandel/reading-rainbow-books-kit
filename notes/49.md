## About the book
*Brush* begins with a mischievous pup. After Turko eats a family member's hat, the señora of the house decides enough is enough, and the dog is sent to live with the gardener's married daughter. Little Salah is so sad he thinks he will die, and once the crying stops he sets out to fill the emptiness. He tries his aunt's canary, then the floor lamp, but neither works for long. Then, in a corner of the attic, he finds a brush banished from the broom closet, runs his hand over the bristles, and has the definite impression that he is petting a dog.

The story turns on a question every small child understands: what happens when you pretend hard enough, and the grown-ups will not play along? His mother tells him to get rid of the piece of junk, and he keeps quiet because he is sure justice will prevail. It suits young listeners who have a favorite toy that is more than a toy, or been told to be sensible.

## Author and illustrator
Pere Calders was born in Barcelona in 1912. He trained at the Llotja School of Fine Arts and worked as a journalist and cartoonist. He fought on the Republican side in the Spanish Civil War and went into exile in Mexico in 1939, living there for twenty-three years and working from 1943 as a graphic artist and technical draftsman. He died in 1994. His best-known book, *Cròniques de la veritat oculta*, won the Víctor Català Prize. *Brush* is the English edition of *Raspall*, a short story he first published in 1941; the picture-book version earned the Serra d'Or children's literature prize in 1982.

Carme Solé Vendrell was born in Barcelona in 1944 and has illustrated more than 100 books since 1968. *Raspall* appeared in 1981. Among her awards are the Premio Nacional de Cultura, in 1979 and again in 2012, and the Creu de Sant Jordi in 2006. In English she also illustrated *Water* and *Sally's Story* (1984). The English text of *Brush* was translated by Marguerite Feitlowitz.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton's lead-in, which begins "Hi there. Today we're in a real fantasy land that few people ever get to see." He describes a New Jersey warehouse where the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade figures sleep for most of the year, then introduces the story of an ordinary scrub brush that comes to life. The child hears this without the warehouse pictures.

Héctor Elizondo reads the book, and the audio ends on its last line: "We're not sure that it's alive, but it deserves to be."

Content note: the pup is sent away, Salah is very sad and cries, and a burglar fights Salah's father.

Not in the podcast audio: children saying what they would bring to life, a look at how the parade's giant turkey is worked, a visit to the parade's float designer, animators making a clay frog and a dinosaur character, kids reviewing three books, and a puppeteer who joins LeVar for the sign-off.
