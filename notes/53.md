## About the book

A boy and his grandfather sit together at night, and the boy asks to hear his own story again. The boy was born during a wild storm in the mountains, and the grandfather tells how he was named and what the boy has learned since. The boy is blind, and the telling is full of the ways he finds to see: with his hands, with his memory, and with a horse he trained to race. As the story goes, the grandfather ties a knot in a counting rope each time it is told.

The book moves at the pace of a spoken conversation, which suits listening very well. It is gentle but serious, best for children old enough to sit with a quiet story about courage, love and growing up. Kirkus reviewed it in 1987, calling it the trio's fifth collaboration, and praised Ted Rand's paintings of the desert and the night sky. Its verdict: "An excellent contribution."

## Author and illustrator

Bill Martin Jr. (1916 to 2004) was born and raised in Hiawatha, Kansas, and had difficulty reading until he went to college. He taught high school, served as a newspaper editor in the Army Air Force during World War II, and became a full-time writer in 1972. By the end of his life he had published more than 300 children's books. He met John Archambault in 1983 at UC Riverside, and they went on to write more than a dozen books together, including *Chicka Chicka Boom Boom*.

Archambault is originally from Pasadena, California, and decided he wanted to be a writer in third grade after reading *Charlotte's Web*. He studied writing and journalism at UC Riverside and later taught first grade in the Bronx. He is also a poet, storyteller and musician.

Ted Rand (1916 to 2005) was born on Mercer Island, Washington, and illustrated 78 children's books. Wikipedia notes that he illustrated children's books in his 60s, 70s and 80s. He also illustrated *Barn Dance!* and *Here Are My Hands* by Martin and Archambault.

## In this episode

The audio opens on location, with LeVar Burton camping alone in the wilderness for the first time. He says he has camped before but never by himself, then says he needs a little inspiration and names a book that "always gives me courage when I need it", about "a boy who conquers fear every day". The book is *Knots on a Counting Rope*.

The book is read by J. Ruben Silverbird and Kenneth Blank, and the audio ends on the book's last line, the grandfather's promise that the boy will never be alone.

Content note: the grandfather says he will not always be there, and the boy fears losing him.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's rainy night and closing words. A television news reporter born with deformed hands and feet talks about her career and about feeling different. Children say when they were last brave, a song plays, and children review other books.
