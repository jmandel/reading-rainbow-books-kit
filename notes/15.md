## About the book
On a hot, humdrum day, Ty slips away to the cool grass by the pond and hears a strange sound: step, thrump, step, thrump. It belongs to a man with one wooden leg, who juggles his tin cup and plate, drums on them with a spoon, and then vanishes into the grass. When he pops back out, laughing, he says he is Andro, a one-man band, and asks Ty to bring a washboard, two wooden spoons, a tin pail and a comb. He will make music at sundown.

Ty collects the instruments from his family, who all doubt that a comb or a pail was made for music, and his friends laugh at the idea. Then comes a long wait under the street lamp.

The text is full of sounds meant to be said aloud, like "tink, tink" and "tippity tap." Four Winds Press published it in 1980. It suits children who like rhythm, waiting stories, and the idea that ordinary household things can make a song.

## Author and illustrator
Mildred Pitts Walter (1922-2026) was born in Louisiana, studied English at Southern University, and taught in the Los Angeles schools for many years. Encyclopedia.com quotes her saying she "wanted to know why there were so few books for and about the children I taught who were black." Her first book was *Lillie of Watts: A Birthday Discovery* (1969), and she wrote more than 20 books for young readers. *Justin and the Best Biscuits in the World* won the Coretta Scott King Award. Young listeners might also enjoy her *My Mama Needs Me* and *Brother to the Wind*.

Margot Tomes (1917-1991) was born in Yonkers, New York, and studied at Pratt Institute. She designed wallpaper and fabric for many years, and described her move into children's books as happening "more by chance than by intent." She illustrated over 60 titles, including *Jack and the Wonder Beans* (1977) and *If There Were Dreams to Sell* (1984), both named among the New York Times Best Illustrated Children's Books of the Year.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with a visit to a one-man-band maker. Asked how many instruments he has, he answers, "I have 55 right now," and says he has been at it for 12 years. The host then introduces the book as a story about "a very mysterious stranger who promised to make a one man band right out of thin air, and about the boy who believed that he could."

*Ty's One-Man Band* is read by Lou Rawls and the audio ends on the book's last line, "Only Ty saw Andro slip away back into the night."

Not in the podcast audio: a short burst of tinkling music and the closing reflection on making music from ordinary things, plus the rest of the television episode.

Content note: a stranger appears in the dark, and Ty is laughed at by his friends while he waits.
