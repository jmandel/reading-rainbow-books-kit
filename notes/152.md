## About the book
Amina lives in Portland, Oregon, and the whole family is flying to Mali to visit her father's relatives. Just before the plane lands she discovers a wobbly tooth, and her father tells her about a local custom: a tooth lost in Africa and left under a gourd earns a chicken from the African tooth fairy. Amina wants that chicken badly, so she wiggles, pushes and tries every tongue trick she knows. Young listeners step into a family compound with Grandma, aunts, uncles and cousins, shared meals around one big bowl, and a rooster that crows at dawn. Kirkus called the descriptions "an amusing introduction to one African country" and said the book is "an excellent way to encourage children to start writing their own family stories." It suits children around the age of their first loose tooth, and anyone curious about how other families live and celebrate small milestones. The pace is gentle, and whether the chicken will really come stays open until late in the story.

## Author and illustrator
Penda Diakité wrote the story when she was eight, according to Kirkus, which describes it as written by the illustrator's elder daughter. Kirkus lists this as her only book, published by Scholastic in 2006.

Baba Wagué Diakité, her father, illustrated it. He is a Malian author and illustrator whose first book, *The Hunterman and the Crocodile* (1997), drew on his childhood memories of West Africa, with pictures inspired by hand-painted ceramic tiles. Kirkus called the art in his *The Hatseller and the Monkeys* (1999) "festive, authentic, painted-tile" and praised the book's "unusual sound effects for read-aloud fun." In *The Magic Gourd* (2003), which he wrote and illustrated, Kirkus notes its mud-cloth patterns carry meaning for the Bamana people of Mali, and its author's note describes learning the clever Rabbit stories in childhood. Young listeners who enjoy his style might like *The Hatseller and the Monkeys*, a retelling of a folktale familiar from *Caps for Sale*, or *The Magic Gourd*. Kirkus also lists *Mee-An and the Magic Serpent* (2007), which he wrote and illustrated.

## In this episode
The podcast opens with LeVar Burton explaining that he is taking the training wheels off his nephew's bicycle, and how children outgrow things, including their first teeth. He says the loose tooth in this book travels thousands of miles, so its owner can make an unusual claim in America: "I lost my tooth in Africa." The title is then announced by a voice the captions do not identify, and the full reading of the book follows, ending on Amina counting the days until she returns to Africa. No reader is credited in the episode records. Content note: the story includes a gentle goodbye to Africa and Amina's chickens, which leaves her a little sad.

Not in the podcast audio: LeVar's tooth traditions from Egypt and Korea, a four-year-old's first dentist visit with a children's dentist, a girl in Queens, New York, preparing for her quinceañera, and kids reviewing books.
