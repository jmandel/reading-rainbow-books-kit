## About the book
Tony helps at his father Nino's pizzeria: he kneads dough, stirs sauce, grates cheese, clears plates and passes extra pizzas to hungry people in the alley. The restaurant is so small that customers wait in long lines. Then a man visits after closing, and Nino makes a plan that changes the little shop and the way father and child spend their days.

The story is short and easy to follow, with a clear lesson about what matters in work and family. It suits ages 4 to 8, and children who like helping in the kitchen will recognize the jobs Tony does.

The pictures carry much of the appeal. Kirkus praised the "Matisse-like blocks of bright color" and called the story "pithy." Publishers Weekly liked the "bright color and lively pattern" of the illustrations, but called the book "an old-fashioned morality tale" but thought the illustrations' appeal "perhaps more suited to adult book buyers than young readers", and said the characters remain as flat as the stylized pictures.

## Author and illustrator
Karen Barbour wrote and illustrated this book. Encyclopedia.com lists her birth as October 29, 1956, in San Francisco. She earned a B.A. at the University of California, Davis, in 1976 and an M.F.A. at the San Francisco Art Institute in 1980, and works as a freelance illustrator, author, animator and painter. She lives in Point Reyes Station, California.

*Little Nino's Pizzeria* was her first self-illustrated picture book, published by Harcourt Brace Jovanovich in 1987. The same year it received a Certificate of Excellence from the American Institute of Graphic Arts Book Show and a Parents' Choice Award. Her first illustration job, a cookbook, was published in 1988, and picture books followed quickly.

Listeners who enjoy her bright, patterned art can look for her other self-illustrated books: *Nancy* (1989), *Mr. Bow Tie* (1991) and *Mr. Williams* (2005). She also illustrated books by other authors, including *I Have an Olive Tree* by Eve Bunting (1999) and *A Sip of Aesop* by Jane Yolen (1995).
