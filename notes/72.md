## About the book

*The Adventures of Taxi Dog* is told in rhyme by Maxi, a stray who grew up hunting for food in the dark in New York City until a man with a flat tire stops, pats his head and takes him home. The man is Jim, a cab driver, and he gives Maxi a bright red scarf, a name and a spot in the front seat of his taxi. From there the book rides along through a day of fares. It suits young listeners who like animals and vehicles, and the book is told in rhythmic verse.

The book was published by Dial in 1990. Publishers Weekly wrote that the Barraccas' narrative "so perfectly echoes Maxi's jaunty attitude" that children might think being a New York taxi dog is "the best job in the world," and called the book "a sheer delight" for "dog fanciers, taxi riders and lovers of fine picture books." Kirkus was mixed: it faulted the rhyming text but praised Mark Buehner's illustrations, with their brash colors and black-and-white checked borders.

## Author and illustrator

Debra Barracca and Sal Barracca wrote the book together. They went on to write two more books about Maxi: *Maxi, the Hero* (Dial, 1991), again illustrated by Mark Buehner, and *A Taxi Dog Christmas* (1994), illustrated by Alan Ayer. Kirkus found the text of *Maxi, the Hero* unexceptional and the paintings its strength.

Mark Buehner was born in 1959 in Salt Lake City, Utah, and illustrates children's books. His wife, Caralyn Buehner, wrote some of the books he has illustrated. He often hides small images such as rabbits, cats and dinosaurs in his illustrations. Publishers Weekly named his illustrated *My Life with the Wave* (1998) a Best Picture Book. Publishers Weekly also noted that the black, yellow and white borders around the text in *Taxi Dog* "cleverly suggest Checker cabs."

Listeners who enjoy his paintings can look for the Caralyn Buehner titles he illustrated, including *Fanny's Dream* (1996), *Snowmen at Night* (2002) and *Superdog* (2004).

## In this episode

The podcast opens with LeVar in New York City: "Today we're in New York City where there's more traffic than anywhere else in the country." The lead-in then gives a quick tour of a New York taxi, covering the rate sign, the top light that shows a cab is free, the meter, the trip sheet and the hack license.

Vincent Gardenia reads the book. The audio ends on the book's last line, as Maxi invites listeners to come along next time.

Content note: Maxi starts out a hungry, homeless stray, and one passenger is rushed to the hospital because a baby is on the way.

Not in the podcast audio: a cold open before the title, LeVar's closing remarks about dogs as partners, a segment about a teenage girl in Colorado and her service dog, Cosmo, trained to pull her wheelchair and carry things, and three reviews by children.
